    ADR-0015: Exact line-source kernel as the default of the image family¶
    Status Accepted
    Date 2026-11-07
    Deciders Project maintainers
    Scope Engine.kernel; the reaction matrix and the field evaluation of image, image_2layer (and the deprecated names that run them), solve_mutual_matrix, solve_mutual_field, FieldResult.potential; PointSource.direction

    Context¶

    Up to 0.15.0 the image family placed the current of every wire segment at the segment midpoint (point kernel) and used the collocation self term \(2\ln(L/a)/L\) on the diagonal. Review pass 10 (finding 10.28) showed that this kernel converges to a biased limit:

    • Per row of the reaction matrix the self term is \(+0.61/L\) too large and the two collinear neighbours \(-0.77/L\) too small; the row sum errs by about \(-0.23/L\) for every segment length \(L\). Because the row sum itself scales like \(1/L\), the relative error of the resistance does not vanish under refinement: rods, rings and strips converge 0.9–1.9 % below the exact solution of the uniform-current model. A convergence study cannot see it.
    • Field points near a wire see point sources: the surface potential spikes at every segment midpoint and is several per cent off above a wire at typical segment lengths (finding 10.33).
    • Near a layer interface the point form of the interface image needs segments much shorter than the distance to the interface (−46 % for a ring 5 cm below \(h_1\) at ds = 0.5 m).
    • The kernel error has the opposite sign of the uniform-current ansatz error, so for impedances the two partly cancel — which made the old numbers look closer to some references than they were.

    The review proposed the exact line-source kernel as an opt-in with a default decision at 1.0. The user decided to make it the default in this release, with kernel="point" reproducing the old numbers.

    Decision¶

    1. Engine.kernel takes None (default), "line" or "point". None lets the backend choose its preferred kernel: "line" for the image family, the backend's own kernel ("point") for every other backend. An explicit "line" on a backend without it raises BackendCapabilityError. The request is carried in the value, so an engine rebuilt from JSON or from a result's provenance block runs like the original; metadata["kernel"] records the kernel that ran.
    2. Galerkin form. The line kernel is the segment-averaged potential of uniform line currents with the reduced kernel \(1/\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}\) — the Galerkin pairing dual to the uniform-current ansatz, symmetric, and exact for a straight wire at any segment length. A Galerkin self term with collocated exact mutual terms was rejected: it biases every interior row by about \(-0.6/L\), worse than the point kernel.
    3. Evaluation. Pairs within six segment lengths are integrated exactly (closed form for parallel pairs, graded Gauss–Legendre with the exact inner line potential otherwise); farther pairs use the point form plus the quadrupole term of the segment average, remainder \(O((L/R)^4)\). The Tagg/Sunde images use the point form unless closer than six segment lengths. On the cross-layer path pairs in different layers carry the transmitted resistivity \(2\rho_1\rho_2/(\rho_1+\rho_2)\) and the interface image is a Galerkin integral for near pairs, which keeps the matrix reciprocal.
    4. Field evaluation. Line-kernel results store the unit tangent of every source segment (PointSource.direction), and FieldResult.potential evaluates exact line potentials near the segments. Results without directions (written by 0.15.0) are evaluated with the point kernel.
    5. The mom family keeps the point kernel. Its point-matching scheme is defined with point sources; cross-checks between image and mom pin kernel="point" so that they compare current models on one kernel.
    6. Cost control. The far-field term over all pairs is the expensive part. It is compiled with numba when available (optional extra fast, bit-identical blocks) and runs in NumPy otherwise; kernel="point" remains the opt-out.

    Consequences¶

    Numbers change. Grounding resistances of single electrodes rise by about 1–2 % at typical segment lengths (the point kernel's bias); network quantities move less — the input impedance of a synthetic 30-house network by +0.35 % (performance page). The generated kernel check of the validation page shows the size per geometry and segment length; the release notes list the moved golden cases. What remains against the true thin-wire solution is the uniform-current ansatz itself (about +0.9 % for the 3 m rod of benchmark B2, larger near grid corners for field quantities, see the image engine page).

    Cost. The Engine.kernel documentation gives the measured factor for a 16-house solve (\(N\) = 1920): 2.7 with numba, 5.7 without; the performance page puts it into the context of whole studies.

    Accuracy statements become clean. With the kernel error removed, the remaining deviations of the image family are attributable to the current model, which mom checks — the separation the validation page relies on.

    Alternatives considered¶

    • Keep the point kernel as default, line opt-in (the review's proposal). Rejected: every study would carry a known, invisible 1–2 % bias by default.
    • Far-field cut-off (no quadrupole term beyond some distance): about 1.5 times the point-kernel cost at about \(10^{-4}\) accuracy instead of a few \(10^{-6}\). Not taken; the numba path makes the full form affordable.
    • Quadrupole terms for every Tagg/Sunde image: at most 0.05 % gain on a straight strip in two-layer soil for about twice the series assembly cost. Not taken in 2026.11.0 at first; taken in wave 3 with a taper (amendment below).

    Amendment 2026.11 (wave 3) — a continuous switch, far images, the cross-layer path¶

    User decision D2 of 2026-11-15, audits W2B-01/-02, finding 10.28.

    1. Continuous switch. Decision 3 switched from the exact integral to the far form at six segment lengths in one step. The pairs six segments apart on a straight wire, and in two-layer soil every segment's own Tagg/Sunde image when an image distance equals six segment lengths, sit exactly there, so the round-off of the coordinates decided their form: the same world at different positions spread by up to 4.4e-7 (a 12 m strip) and 2.6e-5 (two layers) in the impedance. Pairs between six and 7.5 segment lengths now take a C¹ blend of the exact and the far value, for direct pairs, every image, the images of the cross-layer correction and field points. The spread is at most 1e-13 over local positions and the UTM solves lie as close to the local ones as with the point kernel (3e-11). Alternatives: a margin on the distance (a new knife-edge at another distance, which a chosen \(h_1\) hits again) and a far form exact to round-off at six segment lengths (about ten more multipole orders); the blend costs the integrals of the pairs in the band (+24 % solve time in homogeneous soil).
    2. Far form. The far form of segment pairs carries the radius term \(-a^2/(2R^3)\) of the reduced kernel \(1/\sqrt{R^2 + a^2}\) that every exact pair uses; without it the far form was \(a^2/(2R^2)\) away from the exact pairs it joins, which made straight wires converge non-monotonically at the 1e-6 level.
    3. Far Tagg/Sunde images carry the quadrupole term (the alternative above). On every image it cost 2.2 … 3.9 times the solve of 16 house grids; the term is therefore tapered off between 60 and 120 segment lengths of vertical distance between observer and image (at most \(1/(6\cdot 60^2)\) of the image's point value, for a vertical wire and its collinear image; \(1/(8\cdot 60^2)\) for horizontal wires; the order of the far form's remainder at the switch), a property of the pair, and images beyond every observer are skipped: 1.2 … 1.5 times, the impedance within 1e-6 of the term on every image. Gain: the strip of the alternative within 7.7e-7 instead of 5.1e-4, monotone in the segment length; its surface potential within 8e-6 instead of 0.94 %; house grids 3e-6 … 1.8e-4.
    4. The cross-layer path (ADR-0007) treats the images of its layered correction alike (ADR-0007, amendment wave 3).

    Amendment 2026.11 (wave 3, audit W3A) — image pairs, the cost of the far images, a point-form option¶

    The author's decision of 2026-11-15 and audit W3A (stream fw3xl).

    1. Image pairs. Decision 2 applied the reduced kernel to every pair, images included, and point 2 of the wave-3 amendment gave the far form of images the radius term. An image line that passes a wire at a transverse distance \(\rho_\perp \ge a\) acts with its axis value (the average of \(\ln\lvert x - y\rvert\) over a circle of radius \(a\) is \(\ln\max(\rho, a)\)), so the reduced kernel left out \(a^2/(2d^2)\) of an image \(d\) away: a ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface read +1.60 % against its exact thin ring (audit W3A-02, finding of w4axi). The line potential of an image at a point of the observer now uses \(\max(\rho_\perp, a)\), which is the reduced kernel for a collinear image (a rod against its own image, where it is exact); an image within one wire radius of its own segment blends into the reduced kernel of the direct pair it nearly coincides with, so that a wire on the interface is the same on both sides; the far form of images has no radius term; the image corrections are symmetrised (the pointwise rule differs between the two orders of a skew pair). The ring is within 0.004 % now, a rod through the interface within 1.4e-6 of its exact two-owner value. The soil-surface image keeps the reduced kernel (homogeneous results unchanged; with the transverse distance a house grid at 0.8 m would move by 4e-7, at 0.1 m by 1.7e-5 — an open decision).
    2. Cost of the far images. Every image the longest segment of the world reached was evaluated on every pair (audit W3A-01: eight 9.4 m pieces among 1920 segments multiplied the far-image work by 5.6), and the image density of a high contrast made a 40-house network in 1000 / 30 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1.5 m (82 images within the taper) 4.4 times the solve of the base. An image is now evaluated on the pairs whose longer segment reaches it (bit-identical), the far-image part of the reaction matrix on its upper half (it is symmetric), with one division per evaluation and the taper and tolerance tests left out where they hold for a whole block (round-off). That network takes 1.8 times the solve before wave 3 with numba (3.6 times without; 4.2 and 9.7 before the audit), 16 house grids with eight long pieces 1.5 times (2.8 before; performance page). Rejected: tabulating the far term per image family in the horizontal distance (an interpolation error instead of round-off) and multi-threading (the base is single-threaded, parameter sweeps run processes in parallel).
    3. Option. Engine.image_far_field = "point" keeps the far images in point form: the dense far-field term is left out, the near image pairs (within 7.5 segment lengths, a segment's own images included) stay Galerkin integrals on the series and the cross-layer path alike, and the continuous switch and the transverse distance stay. It is not the result before wave 3: within the upper layer up to 2.3e-4 from it (the blend of D2). The quadrupole form stays the default. The point form moves networks by 3e-6 … 4.3e-4 (16 house grids 0.4 m above a 1000 / 10 Ω·m interface), a straight wire near the interface by 4e-4 and the surface potential above it by up to 0.8 %, and takes 0.49 of the default's time on the 40-house network (0.91 times the solve before wave 3; the network solves on the cross-layer path). A common Engine field next to image_max_terms / image_series_tol rather than a backend option, because solve_mutual_* and FieldResult.potential read it too; any other value raises ValueError (audit W3R2A-02).

    Audit W3R2A-01 (round 2): the option first kept, on the cross-layer path, the line form of the interface image only, as before wave 3. The own surface and transmitted images of a segment below the interface were then point sources, so a 75 m strip below a 0.5 m top layer read −1.0e-3 at a segment length of 2.5 m and −58 % at 37.5 m, and a network with a 75 m conductor in two pieces +1.1 % against the default. Every image of the layered correction now keeps its near pairs, and a segment's own images follow the switch of every pair (exact within six segment lengths, blended to 7.5, point beyond — the self pair of the series path): the strip within 1.1e-5 at every segment length, the network within 4e-6. Below thin top layers this costs the near image pairs the option had saved: 1.5 … 2.4 times the solve before wave 3 instead of 1.2 (still 0.51 … 0.94 of the default's time). The auditor's variant (every self image at every distance) and the default's tapered diagonal agree with it to 2e-6 on networks and cost up to 14 % more. 4. Wires within a fraction of a radius of the interface (audit W3R2A-03). The image of a wire within \(a/20\) of the interface blends into the reduced kernel of the direct pair over a tenth of the wire radius (before: over one radius, whose blended distance peaked at \(a/\sqrt 2\) and gave a ring 1.75 mm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface +22 %). Z then moves through \(\pm a\) of the interface with wiggles of at most 0.17 % against its direction (strips and rings in 1000 / 30 and 30 / 1000 Ω·m; before up to 24 %).
