    ADR-0016: A conforming finite-element model in fem¶
    Status Accepted — part A (axisymmetric model) and part B (three-dimensional model, user decision Q40 on the shell overlap) 2026-11-15
    Date 2026-11-15
    Deciders Project maintainers (user decision Q37 of 2026-11-14)
    Scope groundfield.solver.fem (now a package), the fem entry of the backend registry, FemOptions, the optional fem3d extra

    Context¶

    Up to 2026.11 fem replaced every galvanic cluster by one equivalent hemisphere — the hemisphere with the closed-form resistance of the electrodes in homogeneous soil of the top-layer resistivity — and solved the volume problem of that hemisphere. In homogeneous soil that reproduces Dwight's formula by construction; in layered soil it describes another geometry. Every audit round of the release found new limit cases (an interface 1 cm below a deep ring read +191 … +308 % without a message, a rod reaching 1 µm … 8 cm into a more conductive top layer −44 … −78 % with a warning, a ring lying exactly on an interface −21 … +30 %), and the reduction had grown a dozen structural rules (review pass 10, 10.23; user decision Q23; audits D2A, R2A). User decision Q37 stops adding rules and replaces the reduction by a conforming model; the scope decision of 2026-11-15 adds the full 3-D model to the release (part B, with the overlap of concrete shells at junctions, user decision Q40).

    fem stays a reference backend (grey-box principle): the integral engines are the production engines, fem checks them with a method that shares nothing with them but the network solve.

    Decision¶

    1. Physical model: the electrodes as they are¶

    The quasi-static field \(-\nabla\cdot(\sigma(z)\nabla\varphi) = 0\) in the soil, no current into the air, \(\varphi \to 0\) far away. Every electrode is a perfect conductor at its own potential — a port: a rod is the solid cylinder of its wire_radius with flat end faces, a ring the solid torus of its wire_radius. The port conductance matrix \(G\) (currents leaked for the port potentials, symmetric positive definite) is the earth part of the solve; nothing is reduced or averaged.

    A concrete shell (ADR-0012) keeps its meaning: the soil sees the shell surface \(r_b\) (wire_radius) and every metre of wire adds the radial resistance \(C\) = concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m. fem applies it locally, as the surface conductance \(g = 1/(2\pi r_b C)\) between the metal potential and the soil at every point of the electrode surface, so the current may redistribute along the electrode. For a cylindrical shell that is exact (Sunde's decomposition) and needs neither \(\rho_c\) nor \(r_a\), which the electrode does not carry; resolving the concrete as a volume is left to part B, where the overlap at junctions (Q40) needs it.

    2. Axisymmetric discretisation (part A)¶

    A world whose electrodes are rods on one vertical axis and horizontal rings centred on it is a body of revolution and reduces to the meridian half-plane \((s, z)\) with the weight \(2\pi s\):

    • Graded tensor mesh. Lines \(s = s_i\) and \(z = z_j\) through every feature — rod surfaces and end faces, layer interfaces, soil surface, axis, truncation box — graded geometrically away from them (\(h(x) = \min_f(h_f + g|x - x_f|)\), \(g = 0.2\)). The rod's end edges are re-entrant corners of the conductor; with a spacing of \(0.002\,a\) there (a few dozen lines more) the convergence stays of second order even for a rod tip lying on an interface. Linear triangles, the weight integrated exactly.
    • O-grid for the ring wire. A square block of up to three wire radii around the wire cross section is filled by an O-grid conforming to the tensor lines on its boundary and inscribed in the circle of the wire (32 nodes at level 0). The block keeps clear of the surface, the axis, the other electrodes and every interface more than \(1.05\,a\) from the wire axis; a nearer interface (a ring on it, or within its radius) is cut into the block's elements, so every element lies in one layer. Such a block is finer: the gap between the wire and the interface, and the narrow wedge where the interface cuts the wire near its edge, make the field vary over \(\sqrt{2a\,|d - a|}\) (\(0.14\,a\) at \(d = 1.01\,a\)). With the default block the levels converged at first order and not monotonically there (up to −6.0e-3 next to a layer 33 times more conductive, the error estimate below the error in 7 of 14 cases, audit W4A-01); refined to 64 intervals the default lay within 3.3e-4 at contrast 33 (stream fw4axi), but beyond it the levels converged at first order again (touching a 1000 times more conductive layer −1.6e-2, estimate 1.1e-2; audit W4R2A-02). A refinement alone does not resolve this: the field is singular where the interface cuts the wire at a narrow angle or touches it, on a scale that shrinks with the contrast. The block's O-grid is graded towards those points in angle and radius (32 intervals, cells of 1e-3 wire radii), which resolves them at fewer nodes: within 5.1e-5 of the boundary elements up to a contrast of 1000, the estimate above the error in all 113 cases of the audit's sweep (evidence e05 of stream fw4r2a).
    • Truncation. A box at r_far_factor (1000) characteristic lengths — the electrodes' size, or the depth of the deepest interface times the layer contrast if larger (a resistive bottom keeps the current in the layers above it over that distance) — carries the Robin condition \(\partial_n\varphi = -(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r/r^2)\varphi\), the exact Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of the monopole far field on any boundary. The graded mesh reaches the box with a few dozen extra lines.
    • Ports. The surface nodes of a bare electrode are one unknown; the port conductance matrix is the Schur complement of the stiffness matrix onto the ports (one sparse LU factorisation for all ports).
    • Refinement and extrapolation. Level \(\ell\) bisects every interval of the level-0 lines \(\ell\) times. The tensor part of the spaces is nested, the O-grid of a ring is not (its layers move with the level, the inscribed polygon grows): one level converges at second order and, in every case tested away from interfaces, from below — observed, not guaranteed (audit W4A-09). The default solves levels 0 and 1 and extrapolates the port conductance matrix with the observed second order (Richardson); the relative difference between the extrapolated and the finer level is reported as metadata["fem_error_estimate"] (about the finer level's error, 0.88 … 0.99 of it in 36 cases of auditor W4A and 0.94 … 1.07 in 60 cases of a ring at an interface, audit W4R2A-02; NaN with extrapolate=False, audit W4A-02).
    • Resource guard. FemOptions.max_nodes (2 million nodes of the finer mesh, about 8 GB: the axisymmetric solve needs about 4 GB per million nodes, 0.82 / 3.15 / 5.15 GB at 0.20 / 0.80 / 1.22 million measured with /usr/bin/time -l, evidence e02 of stream fw4axi; audit W4A-05) against a prediction from the mesh lines before anything is assembled. The prediction counts the O-grid exactly and bounds the cut of an interface, so it errs high (audit W4A-10).

    Every other world — electrodes other than rods and rings, a rod or ring off the common axis, galvanic distributed conductors (their leakage runs along their route), touching electrodes, a ring wire that reaches within 5 % of its radius to the soil surface (the solid torus must fit) and a ring without room for its mesh block (within about a wire radius, diagonally, of a rod's end edge; audit W4A-03) — goes to the three-dimensional model of section 5 under the default FemOptions.model = "auto". With model = "axisymmetric" it is refused with BackendCapabilityError, naming the backends that solve it. (Part A alone, before part B, refused every such world.) The common axis has a tolerance of 1e-3 of the smallest wire radius or surface gap between two electrodes (at least 1e-12 of the world's size): the resistance is even in the offset \(\delta\), so the axisymmetric reading is off by \(\kappa(\delta/L)^2\) with \(|\kappa| \le\) 6e-4 for \(L\) the wire radius and \(\le\) 1.1e-2 for \(L\) the gap (image and mom, three worlds), below 1e-6. Up to audit W4A-08 it was 1e-9 of the world's size (3 nm): a rod 1 µm off a ring's axis went to the 3-D model (40.7 s, 2.35 GB, −1.43e-3).

    3. Network and results on the shared pipeline¶

    The soil model delivers an EarthPorts record (solver/fem/_ports.py): the port conductance matrix, the display segments of the electrodes (the segments the integral backends would use at the engine's segment_length) and the current each of them leaks for 1 V on each port. solver/fem/_network.py runs the pipeline of the integral backends (ADR-0014) with one pseudo-segment per port and \(Z = R = G^{-1}\) as the multi-port matrix: lumped and isolated distributed conductors, inductive branches with their earth-return models, the skin effect, junctions, return_to and voltage sources are honoured as there, at every frequency of the engine. The result carries point sources with the model's leakage distribution, so FieldResult.potential and the touch-voltage helpers work (homogeneous and two-layer soil, as for the other backends). The solve runs the coordinate check of user decision D5.

    4. Package layout (the interface for part B)¶

    solver/fem/
  __init__.py       solve_fem (dispatch), public names, module docstring
  _mesh.py          graded lines, tensor grid, O-grid, interface cut (2-D)
  _assembly.py      axisymmetric P1 assembly, far field, shells, port Schur complement
  _axisymmetric.py  world -> coaxial electrodes -> EarthPorts (part A)
  _ports.py         EarthPorts: the interface of any soil model to the network
  _network.py       EarthPorts + world -> FieldResult on the shared pipeline
  _guards.py        refusals shared by every model, the remedy text
  _legacy.py        equivalent_hemisphere_radius (deprecated), FemReductionWarning

    Part B adds its 3-D model as further modules (for example _mesh3d.py, _assembly3d.py, _solid.py) whose entry point returns an EarthPorts for any world, and extends the dispatch in solve_fem (axisymmetric where possible, 3-D otherwise); _network.py, _ports.py and _guards.py are shared unchanged.

    5. Three-dimensional model (part B)¶

    Every world that the axisymmetric model cannot hold — strips, polylines, stars, meshes and grids, rods and rings side by side, several clusters, galvanic distributed conductors — is solved in three dimensions. FemOptions.model = "auto" (default) keeps the axisymmetric model where it applies (bit for bit) and takes the 3-D model otherwise; "3d" forces it, "axisymmetric" refuses every other world. Modules: _wires3d.py (wires, radius correction), _mesh3d.py (mesh), _assembly3d.py (stiffness, immersed elements, sources, port solve), _solid.py (world → EarthPorts).

    • Electrodes as smoothed line sources with an analytic radius correction. A resolved wire surface (radius 5 mm in a domain of kilometres) cannot be meshed affordably in 3-D, and the brief asked for embedded line sources. Every wire axis (a ring as a polygon of 1° chords) carries the current density \(j(s)\), spread over the disc of radius \(R\) perpendicular to the axis with the bump \(\eta = \frac{3}{\pi R^2}(1 - r^2/R^2)^2\); the wire sees the \(\eta\)-weighted disc average \(\Pi(s)\). What lies inside the disc is added analytically as a radial resistance per length, \(U - \Pi = (C + \Delta C)\,j\), with the shell coefficient \(C\) of ADR-0012 and the correction \(\Delta C\) = the difference between the reduced thin-wire kernel of radius \(a\) and that of the smoothed source (energy radius \(0.5442\,R\)), integrated in closed form over what the mesh does not resolve: the observer's own wire whole (its polyline as far as it has turned by at most 90° along the wire, by a weight falling linearly to 0 at 150°, and every piece on its axis line that belongs to its port or to a wire that meets it — the next piece of a galvanic conductor, the surface image of a rod reaching the surface); of the rest of its electrode (the own polyline beyond, by the rest of the weight), of a wire of another port that meets the observer's wire (their surfaces touch: a rod on a ring, a T-joint, a crossing; one conductor), fading out with the surface gap where two wires have just stopped touching, and of every other surface image only the part within \(1.5\,R\) of the point; any other wire of another port nothing, since it carries its own current (audit W4R2B-03). An exact-parallel test of folded arms switched at a tilt of 1e-6 rad (audit W4R2B-02), a test of the direction within 120° counted a meander's arm whole after a full turn and made a V jump at 120°, a meeting distance of the smaller radius made two crossing strips jump where their surfaces still overlapped, and a collinear rod of another electrode counted whole across an end gap (audits W4R3B-01, -03, -04); the axis distance from the cross product keeps long oblique pieces whole (W4R2B-01). Beyond that the disc average of a smoothed line is its line field (2-D mean-value property of the radial bump), so the mesh is exact there: for two parallel wires the exact correction is below \(10^{-3}\) of the self term from \(1.5\,R\) on (evidence f01 of stream fw4fem3d). The first version counted the surface images and every wire within \(20\,R\), with the observer's current: shallow wires read up to +1.9e-3 high and two rings 10 cm apart +4.6e-3, above their error estimate (audit W4B-01). Far inside a straight wire \(\Delta C\) is Sunde's \(\frac{\rho}{2\pi}\ln(r_\eta/a)\). The result depends little on \(R\) (evidence e07 of stream w4fem3d, re-measured as f05 of stream fw4fem3d: a 3 m rod and a ring at \(R\) = 4, 6 and 10 cm against part A). Default \(R\) = 6 cm. Wires that do not meet but run beside each other (side by side within 30° of parallel, a folded arm, a wire within reach of another over its whole length) are kept at least \(1.5\,R\) apart, two wires end to end in line with a gap at least \(5\,R\): \(R\) becomes their axis distance over 1.5 (end to end: their gap over 5) for the whole world, and below 2.2 radii of the thickest bare wire of the world the solve is refused before meshing (BackendCapabilityError, naming the distance and the integral engines). Wires that meet at 10° or more (junctions, corners, a V or a branch of 10 … 30°) and crossings at more than 30° are left alone; wires that meet at less than 10°, nearly parallel or touching along their length, run beside each other (audits W4R2B-03, W4R2A-01, W4R3B-01, W4R3B-02, and the coordinator's decisions on them; up to then two coaxial rings 7.5 cm apart read +4.35e-3 in a port entry, two rings diagonal to each other, surface gaps 1.3 … 3.8 mm, +11 … +14 %, two stacked rods 1 cm apart +1.6e-2, and a V narrower than 30° was refused). An encased wire meets others by its outer shell radius \(r_b\), what the soil sees: two encased wires of different ports whose shells touch are one conductor in the radius correction, and their separate port entries are not resolved.
    • Isotropic mesh, deterministic bisection. A coarse grid of \(8 \times 8 \times 4\) cubes (six Kuhn tetrahedra each) on the box is refined by conforming longest-edge bisection (own implementation with ties broken by node numbers; scikit-fem's adaptive refinement asserts on some backgrounds) until every element is below \(1.5\,(R/2 + g\max(0, d - R))\), \(d\) the distance from the nearest wire, \(g = 0.5\). A graded tensor grid, the first design, carries the fine lines of the layer interfaces and electrodes to the box as slabs thousands of times wider than thick: the multigrid needed 345 iterations for a 5 m slab and did not converge for a 0.3 m one; the isotropic mesh needs 16 … 115 (up to 115 with immersed layer interfaces).
    • Layer interfaces as partially penalised immersed elements. No interface is a mesh plane (a plane through the isotropic mesh would cut the large far elements into slivers). A tetrahedron that one interface cuts gets the immersed shape functions of Li, Lin & Wu (2003) / Kafafy et al. (2005) — linear on either side, continuous, with continuous normal flux — and the faces the interface crosses get the symmetric consistency and penalty terms of Lin, Lin & Zhang (2015); nodes within 30 % of their shortest edge of a plane (and a quarter of the thinner layer) are moved onto it so no cut leaves a sliver. The scheme reproduces a layered field exactly (to 3e-14; without the face terms −1.6e-4 … −6.4e-4 on the same cube, e01 / e08 of stream w4fem3d). A laminate mean (arithmetic along, harmonic across the layers) is a non-conforming average of first order; it is kept only for elements more than four times larger than the thinner layer they cut (far from the electrodes, where the field varies on scales far above the layer).
    • Solve. The disc averages couple every node of a disc with every other, so the coupling \(A^\mathsf T D A\) is never formed: conjugate gradients on \(K + A^\mathsf T D A\) with the smoothed-aggregation multigrid of \(K\) (pyamg) as preconditioner, one solve per port (\(G\) is quadratic in the solution, so its error is the square of the solver's). A direct sparse factorisation (SuperLU, scipy.sparse.linalg.splu) needed 93 s at 155 000 nodes (COLAMD ordering) and had not finished after 15 minutes at 68 000 nodes with the MMD ordering. pyamg draws the start vectors of its spectral-radius estimates from numpy's global random state; the hierarchy is set up under a fixed seed and the user's state is restored, so results are bit-identical whatever the user's seed and the user's stream is not advanced (audit W4B-07). The iterations grow with the layer contrast (16 … 115 up to the contrast 33 of AP1, 384 … 530 at 100 and 1 225 … 1 264 at 333 for a rod through the interface); at 1 000 they do not converge in 3 000 iterations, and the solve raises BackendCapabilityError naming the contrast, the axisymmetric model where it takes the world, and the integral engines; the tested range is up to about 300 (audit W4B-03; without a layer contrast the refusal names its circumstances instead, audit W4R2B-06). The multigrid of \(K + \operatorname{diag}(A^\mathsf T D A)\) gives the same counts (evidence f02 of stream fw4fem3d).
    • Levels and extrapolation. The finer level is the red refinement of the coarser one (nested); \(G\) is extrapolated with the second order as in part A. The levels are not yet asymptotic for every geometry (error ratios \(r\) = 3.3 … 4.4 for rods and 3.0 … 3.4 for rings), so the extrapolated value carries up to 2e-3. It errs by \(e_1(4 - r)/3\), the reported estimate is \(e_1(r - 1)/3\) (3 … 5e-3): a bound while \(r \ge 2.5\), 2.1 … 4.4 times the error for rings. The same level difference of the transfer entries is reported as metadata["fem_error_estimate_transfer"] (audit W4R2B-05: the estimate covered the diagonal only; where the model holds it is 1.06 … 6.3 times the error of the transfer entries, also for wires end to end at the gap of \(5\,R\) the rule keeps: the error of their transfer entry falls with \(R\) / gap, and factor 3 left 1.15 … 1.18 times the estimate for 8 mm rods 10 … 18 cm apart). This holds away from layer interfaces and other wires — the model keeps wires that run beside each other at least \(1.5\,R\) apart, and two coaxial rings 4.5 … 10 cm apart lie within 1.8e-3 of the solid tori in every port entry. Next to a strongly contrasting interface the thin-wire error applies, which the levels do not see (a ring 1.5 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface lies between the exact thin ring and the solid torus, its levels on either side of the torus; audit W4B-02). Resolved concrete converges at first order (\(r \approx 2\)): there the estimate is no bound.
    • Thin wire next to an interface. The smoothed line source shares the thin-wire limit of the integral engines next to a contrasting interface (+9.2e-3 against the solid torus for a ring 3 radii above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface, beyond its estimate; evidence f10 of stream fw4fem3d, audit W4B-02). The dispatch therefore emits InterfaceContactWarning for a horizontal wire within the reach of an interface (3.9 radii at a contrast of 33, audit W4R2A-04; the table of the reach is in the warnings reference) before the 3-D model runs, as the integral engines do (audit W4A-07); the axisymmetric model resolves the wire and does not warn. The 3-D model's own guard refuses bare wires closer than about 2.8 radii (the disc of the source must lie in one layer).
    • Ports and network. Every electrode is one port, every leaking piece of a galvanic distributed conductor one more; _network.solve_network takes the pipeline's topology of those pieces, the rest is section 3.
    • Concrete (user decision Q40). By default each encased wire carries its shell coefficient \(C\) (the model of the image family and mom). With FemOptions.concrete_resistivity = \(\rho_c\) the concrete is resolved: a square prism of conformal radius \(r_b\) (side \(r_b/0.59017\)) around every straight axis-aligned encased wire, the metal radius following from \(C = \frac{\rho_c}{2\pi}\ln(r_b/r_a)\), the prism faces as immersed interfaces, so overlapping shells at corners, T-joints and crossings share their concrete. The square keeps the resistance to the soil, but in the shell-dominated limit its concrete is 3.8 % less resistive than the circular shell: the concrete is set by the square's inner conformal radius, \(0.914\,r_b\) (audit W4B-04; one side cannot match both radii, so the prism is not resized). Together with the first order of the prism faces and edges the resolved concrete reads the conductance per metre 2.8 … 4.6 % high at the default levels, so it measures differences on one mesh. Against the per-wire shells a junction acts like a few cm of wire, at most about \(2\,r_b\), sign not settled — the junction numbers are not converged (one refinement of the prism faces moved the corner by about 5 cm; evidence e09, e11 of stream w4fem3d, audit W4B-05) — and the house mesh of the generators changes by about 1 % or less (0.35 … 0.85 % with every sign aligned after the fix of W4B-01, evidence f12 of stream fw4fem3d). The per-wire shells keep their place as the default (no correction rule). A cover so thin that the smoothed source the prism allows does not reach beyond the metal (energy radius at most 1.2 metal radii: a 5 mm bar with 5 mm cover) is refused before meshing, naming concrete_resistivity=None (audit W4R2B-04; up to then a bare ValueError after meshing).
    • Resource guard. Before anything is meshed: the display segments against Engine.max_segments, the number of ports against FemOptions.max_ports_3d (default 100; every port is one conjugate-gradient solve per level, 0.93 … 0.96 s per port and level at 583 000 nodes, auditor W4B and evidence f14 of stream fw4fem3d; audit W4B-08) and FemOptions.max_nodes_3d (2 million nodes of the finer mesh; the solve adds about 2.9 GB per million, evidence f15) against a prediction; the exact count again before the finer level. metadata["fem_peak_memory_gb"] is what the solve added to the peak resident memory of the process, fem_process_peak_memory_gb that peak (audit W4B-09).
    • Face pairing. The immersed elements' face terms pair the faces by their sorted node triples (numpy.lexsort); a packed integer key aliased above \(2^{21}\) nodes (audit W4B-06).
    • Dependencies (decision D6). scikit-fem (BSD-3: the Kuhn background MeshTet.init_tensor, the red refinement) and pyamg (MIT), as the optional fem3d extra; import groundfield imports neither. Without the extra a world that needs the 3-D model raises ImportError naming the extra. No GPL or LGPL mesher (gmsh, TetGen, CGAL, netgen) is used: the design needs no resolved surface, so nothing beyond permissive packages was required.

    Consequences¶

    • Every fem number changes (both stored golden cases by −4.6 %; the benchmark tables of the fem page). fem agrees with independent references to about 1e-4 (within 5.1e-5 for a ring wire within 1.05 radii of an interface, up to a contrast of 1000) at a cost of a second per solve (fem page).
    • fem solves every world again: the axisymmetric model where it applies (about 1e-4), the 3-D model otherwise (with the optional fem3d extra; about 2e-3 away from layer interfaces, wires that run beside each other kept at least \(1.5\,R\) apart, which refines the mesh or refuses the closest pairs); without the extra the 3-D worlds raise ImportError.
    • fem gains concrete shells, junctions, two-terminal and voltage sources and every conductor model (registry capabilities).
    • FemOptions changes: refinement, extrapolate, r_far_factor (default 1000), far_field, max_nodes; for the 3-D model model, source_radius, growth, concrete_resistivity, max_nodes_3d, max_ports_3d. The 2026.11 fields n_radial, n_axial, z_far_factor are gone; as keyword arguments of solve_fem (0.15.0) they warn and have no effect. equivalent_hemisphere_radius is deprecated; FemReductionWarning is kept and never emitted.
    • The 3-D model shares the thin-wire assumption of the integral engines (uniform current around the wire, no end faces); it is independent of them in everything else (the layering, the surface, the far field, the current distribution along the wires). Its accuracy (about 2e-3 away from interfaces and other wires, the wires beside each other at least \(1.5\,R\) apart, levels not yet asymptotic for every geometry; next to a strongly contrasting interface the thin-wire error of the integral engines) is a reference for the integral engines' biases of 0.4 … 3 %, not for effects below 1e-3; differences of two concrete models on the same mesh (Q40) are finer than that.
    • No stored number changes with part B: the golden fem cases are axisymmetric and stay on part A bit for bit.
    • fem stays experimental until the audits of the release have passed.

    Alternatives considered¶

    • Thin-wire line sources in the mesh with a radius correction (Peaceman-type well index). Cheaper meshes, but the correction depends on the mesh, and the end faces and the contact with an interface — the configurations where the reduction failed — are exactly what a line source cannot represent. A resolved surface costs only logarithmically many lines on a graded mesh.
    • Extending the spherical-shell mesh of the reduction. It conforms to a hemisphere, not to rods and rings.
    • An unstructured mesher. Not needed in 2-D; in 3-D the smoothed-source formulation needs no surface-fitted mesh, and the bisection mesh of section 5 is built with scikit-fem's tensor grid and an own refinement (decision D6: no GPL / LGPL mesher).
    • 3-D alternatives tried and dropped (stream w4fem3d). A graded tensor grid (slab anisotropy, multigrid stalls); quadratic elements on the bisection mesh (the multigrid did not converge in 500 iterations); laminate interfaces (first order); a mesh cut along the interface planes (slivers where thin layers cross the far elements); plain immersed elements without face terms (not consistent: −1.6e-4 … −6.4e-4 on a layered cube that the penalised form reproduces exactly); a fixed source quadrature coarser than the finer mesh (the finite elements resolve the individual points: the rod's level 2 overshot by +0.6 %); forming \(K + A^\mathsf T D A\) for the multigrid (3.4 GB instead of 1.8 GB at 600 000 nodes). Singularity subtraction with the analytic segment potential (Gjerde et al. 2019) would make the method a moment method with a finite-element correction in homogeneous soil.
    • Resolving the concrete as a volume. Needs the concrete resistivity and the metal radius, which the electrode does not carry; for a cylindrical shell it is equivalent to the surface conductance.
    • Infinite elements or a mapped layer at the truncation. The monopole Robin condition on a box a thousand electrode lengths away moves the result by less than 1e-5 and needs no special elements.
