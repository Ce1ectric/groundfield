    Concepts¶

    This page explains, in plain words, the objects you build a computation from, what happens when you call solve, and what a result contains. Read it once to get the overall picture; the quickstart shows the same in code, and the engines page explains the numerical methods.

    Position in the software family¶

      groundmeas   ──▶   groundinsight   ◀──   groundfield
  (measurements)     (reduced network       (field model,
                      model)                 reference)

    groundfield is the field-theoretical reference of the family. It computes the potential field of earthing systems in layered soil from their geometry and material data and compresses the solution into reduced models — impedance functions \(Z(\rho, f)\), multiport matrices — that groundinsight uses as building blocks of a network model, and against which groundmeas measurements can be interpreted. The field model is a reference, not the end product: every solution exposes the quantities the reduction needs.

    The data model¶

    A computation separates the physics (what is in the ground) from the numerics (how it is solved). The physics lives in a World, the numerics in an Engine; world.solve(engine) returns a FieldResult.

    Object What it is Created with
    soil model horizontal layers of constant resistivity: HomogeneousSoil(rho), TwoLayerSoil(rho_1, rho_2, h_1), MultiLayerSoil(layers); depth \(z\) positive into the soil the class itself
    World the physical model: one soil, and the electrodes, junctions, conductors and sources in it create_world
    electrode a thin wire in contact with the soil: rod, ring, strip, polyline, star, mesh, grid; optionally encased in concrete (concrete shells) create_electrode
    junction a node without soil contact: a cable joint, an instrument terminal create_junction
    conductor a branch between two nodes: an ideal bond, or a cable with resistance, optionally leaking into the soil along its length and optionally inductive create_conductor
    source a current or voltage source that drives a node against a second node or against remote earth (sources and measurement) create_source
    Engine the numerics: backend, frequencies, segment length, source kernel, earth-return model, backend options create_engine
    FieldResult potentials, leakage currents and impedances per frequency, the sources for evaluating the field, and the metadata that reproduces the solve world.solve(engine)

    Two ideas recur everywhere:

    • Cluster. Electrodes joined by ideal conductors (a conductor without a cross_section) are one galvanic cluster with one potential. A cable with a cross-section is a branch with its own resistance between two clusters.
    • Node. Electrodes and junctions are the nodes of the conductor network. Conductors connect nodes; sources inject current at a node.

    What happens in a solve¶

    world.solve(engine)
 1. check        the backend's capabilities (soil layers, kernel, concrete shells)
                 and the resource limit, before anything is allocated
 2. discretise   every wire into straight segments of at most segment_length,
                 split at layer interfaces
 3. soil         the reaction matrix: the potential every segment's current
                 causes at every other segment (Green's function of the soil)
 4. network      the nodal equations: soil matrix + Kirchhoff's current law at
                 every node + Ohm's law along every conductor + the sources
 5. result       one solution per frequency: potentials, currents, impedances,
                 point sources for the field, metadata

    Below 1 kHz the soil behaves as a pure conductor: no displacement current, and a skin depth large against a low-voltage network. Step 3 is therefore the stationary current field of the electrodes in a horizontally layered half-space; it does not depend on frequency and is computed once per world. Frequency enters in step 4 through the conductors: their resistance (optionally with skin effect), partial inductances and the earth-return impedance make the network equations complex. A purely galvanic world is solved once and reused for every frequency; a world with inductive conductors is solved once per frequency, which is cheap against step 3. The engines page gives the equations, the earth-return page the conductor impedance.

    The engines differ in steps 2 and 3: how many unknown currents a wire carries (one per straight wire, or one per segment) and how the Green's function is evaluated (closed-form image series, numerical Sommerfeld quadrature, or a finite-element volume mesh instead).

    What a result contains¶

    Access Meaning Unit
    result.frequencies the frequencies of the engine, in their order Hz
    result.input_impedance(source) the voltage at a source over its current: what a fault or an earth tester sees Ω
    result.cluster_impedance(electrode) the potential of a cluster over the current it leaks itself Ω
    result.grounding_impedance(electrode) the potential of a cluster over one electrode's leakage current Ω
    result.electrode_potentials[name] the potential of an electrode against remote earth (the EPR for the source current) V
    result.electrode_currents[name] the current an electrode leaks into the soil A
    result.potential(points) the potential at any points in or on the soil (homogeneous and two-layer soil) V
    result.metadata backend, effective backend, kernel, version, segments, solve time, series controls, provenance —

    Every quantity is a complex RMS phasor with one value per frequency (units and conventions). Touch and step voltages, current sharing, plots, sweeps and reduced models are functions that take a result: touch_voltage, step_voltage, cluster_current_balance, plot_surface_potential, sweep, fit_rho_f_standard and others (postprocess). save_result and load_result store a result with its provenance.

    The three impedances coincide for a single electrode and differ in a network: the input impedance is what the feeding point sees (and what a measurement approaches), the cluster impedance is the local spreading impedance of the fed cluster. The input impedance is the default of the sweeps and fits since 2026.11; the sources page has the definitions.

    Modelling assumptions¶

    • Quasi-static field for \(f \lesssim 1\) kHz: stationary current flow in the soil, no displacement current; the soil permittivity is inert.
    • Horizontally layered soil with piecewise constant resistivity; layer stacks are canonicalised (equal adjacent layers merged).
    • Thin wires: the wire radius is small against the segment length and the distance to other wires; the current flows on the axis, the potential is observed on the surface.
    • Current model: uniform leakage per unit length per straight wire of an electrode (and layer) in the image family — impedance-grade, not field-grade near grids and stars (image); one current per segment in the mom family.
    • Remote earth as the potential reference.
    • Conductor impedance from partial inductances and an earth-return correction; the Sommerfeld/Pollaczek kernel by default, Carson's series as a fast approximation for long parallel lines.

    The reduced model¶

    The objective of a field computation is not only a number but a model that a network calculation can use. For one feeding point the reduced model is the impedance as a function of soil resistivity and frequency. The standard form exported to groundinsight is

    \[ Z(\rho, f) = k_0 + k_1\rho + (k_2 + jk_3)\,f + (k_4 + jk_5)\,f\rho , \]

    with the resistance \(k_0\) that does not scale with \(\rho\) (the share of a lower soil layer, or the series resistance of conductors), the spreading resistance \(k_1\rho\), the soil-independent loop terms \(k_2 f\), \(k_3 f\) and Carson-type earth-return terms \(k_4 f\rho\), \(k_5 f\rho\). It is fitted by weighted linear least squares to a family of solves that spans both \(\rho\) and \(f\), with relative weights by default; max_relative reports the worst pointwise error and a fit above 5 % warns. Where the six-term form cannot follow the data (strongly layered soils), a vector fit of the frequency response per \(\rho\) is the alternative. For several feeding points the reduced model is the multiport matrix of the earthing systems (solve_mutual_matrix): its entry \(Z_{ij}\) is the potential of cluster \(i\) per ampere leaking from cluster \(j\). Together with the branch admittances \(\mathbf{Y}_L\) of the network and \(\mathbf{Y}_G = \mathbf{Z}^{-1}\) it forms the nodal system \((\mathbf{Y}_L + \mathbf{Y}_G)\,\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{i}\) of the AP1 catalogue, with the node voltages \(\mathbf{u}\) and the injected currents \(\mathbf{i}\).

    Typical questions¶

    groundfield is built for the dissertation questions on interconnected earthing systems of medium- and low-voltage networks:

    1. How strongly does a remote current injection influence an earthing measurement, and how is the measurement loop to be modelled?
    2. How important are coupling and return-path effects below 1 kHz?
    3. Which robust statements hold for typical distribution-network configurations, and how do they reduce to a small set of parameters?
    4. Which touch voltages arise in the fault case, and over which spectrum?

    The tutorials walk through these computations; this explanation section gives the theory and the evidence behind them.
