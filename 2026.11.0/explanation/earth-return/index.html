    Earth return and inductive coupling¶

    This page explains the longitudinal impedance of the conductors of a grounding study — PEN conductors, cable screens, measurement leads, interconnections between earth electrodes — and in particular the earth-return impedance that the current returning through the soil adds to it. Read it before you give conductors an inductance_model, when you choose Engine.earth_inductive_model, or when a reactance looks suspicious.

    At 50 Hz, and more so at the harmonics up to 1 kHz that matter for AP2, conductors are not only resistors: their longitudinal impedance has an inductive part, they couple magnetically to each other, and the soil closes the loop. The galvanic part of the problem (leakage, potentials in the soil) is not affected; it is explained on the engine pages.

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    A conductor with inductance_model="neumann" is split into branches (one per sub-segment of discretize_segment_length, or one branch for a lumped conductor). Their longitudinal impedance matrix enters the network equations of the engine family:

    \[ Z_b(\omega) = R + j\omega L + \Delta Z(\omega) , \]

    the voltage drop along each branch per unit current in every branch, at the angular frequency \(\omega = 2\pi f\). Its three parts:

    • \(R\) — the diagonal of the DC resistances of the branches; with resistance_model="skin" a frequency-dependent internal-impedance correction is added (see skin effect).
    • \(L\) — the partial-inductance matrix. Between two branches \(i\) and \(j\) it is Neumann's double line integral \(L_{ij} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_i\int_j \frac{\mathrm d\mathbf l_i\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf l_j}{r}\), the magnetic flux that a unit current in one branch links with the other (\(\mu_0\) the permeability of free space, \(r\) the distance of the two line elements). groundfield evaluates it in closed form for parallel, skew, touching and coaxial pairs (Grover 1946). The self terms are those of a thin wire with the DC internal inductance \(\mu_0\ell/(8\pi)\) of a solid round conductor of length \(\ell\) (none for a cable shield, conductor_type="cable_shield", a thin-walled tube). To \(L\) belongs the image of the earth model (below).
    • \(\Delta Z\) — the earth-return correction: how much the finite conductivity of the soil changes the magnetic field of that image.

    The physical scale is the skin depth of the soil,

    \[ \delta = \sqrt{\frac{2\rho}{\omega\mu_0}} \approx 503\,\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{f}}\ \text{m}, \]

    the depth over which an alternating field in soil of resistivity \(\rho\) decays by \(1/e\). In the frequency range of the package it lies between about 110 m (50 Ω·m, 1 kHz) and 1.6 km (500 Ω·m, 50 Hz): comparable to or larger than the extent of a low-voltage network. The return current therefore spreads deep and wide, and neither a perfect mirror nor a perfectly conducting earth describes it. FieldResult.metadata["penetration_depth"] reports \(\delta\) for every frequency of a solve with inductive conductors.

    The soil-side galvanic coupling (leakage, potentials) is unaffected: the field kernel stays real and quasi-static, the earth return acts on the longitudinal impedance of conductors only.

    Corrected statement

    Earlier versions of this page gave the range of \(\delta\) as "about 350 m (50 Ω·m, 1 kHz) and 5 km (500 Ω·m, 50 Hz)". The formula above gives 112.5 m and 1591.5 m there (groundfield.coupling.carson.skin_depth agrees); the 159 m at 1 kHz in 100 Ω·m quoted below were right.

    The three earth models¶

    Engine.earth_inductive_model selects the model. Each is the combination of an image convention for \(L\) and a correction \(\Delta Z\) defined against that very image — an earth-return correction is meaningless without the baseline it was derived for:

    earth_inductive_model Image in \(L\) Correction \(\Delta Z\) Use
    "sommerfeld" (default since 0.13.0) none (free space) full Sommerfeld / Pollaczek kernel minus free space, every segment pair quantitative \(Z(f)\), any geometry, layered earth
    "carson_series" anti-parallel (perfectly conducting earth) Carson per metre on near-parallel horizontal pairs, Sommerfeld otherwise (below) long parallel lines over homogeneous earth; fast
    "perfect_mirror" additive (magnetic mirror) none diagnostic baseline only

    Sommerfeld (Pollaczek kernel)¶

    The vector potential of an alternating line current in or above a conducting half-space is a Sommerfeld integral (Sommerfeld 1909; Pollaczek 1926 for the buried conductor, Carson 1926 for the overhead one). Take the time convention \(e^{+j\omega t}\), the soil conductivity \(\sigma = 1/\rho\), the propagation constant \(\gamma\) with \(\gamma^2 = j\omega\mu_0\sigma\), the radial wavenumber \(\lambda\) of the Hankel transform and \(u = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \gamma^2}\). For two points at depths \(z\) and \(z'\) in homogeneous soil, at the horizontal distance \(s\) and the distance \(R\), the Green's function of the vector potential is

    \[ G(s, z, z') = \frac{e^{-\gamma R}}{R} + \int_0^\infty \frac{\lambda}{u}\,\frac{u - \lambda}{u + \lambda}\, e^{-u(z+z')}\,J_0(\lambda s)\,\mathrm d\lambda : \]

    the field of the source in an unbounded conducting medium, plus the field reflected at the soil surface, written as a superposition of cylindrical waves (\(J_0\) the Bessel function of order zero). The factor \((u - \lambda)/(u + \lambda)\) is the reflection coefficient of the insulating air. The earth-return correction of two segments is the change of their mutual impedance against free space,

    \[ \Delta Z_{ij} = j\omega\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_i\int_j \Bigl(G - \frac{1}{R}\Bigr)\, \mathrm d\mathbf l_i\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf l_j , \]

    integrated over the actual geometry of each segment pair — any length, orientation and depth — and added to an image-free Neumann matrix. For layered earth the spectral amplitude carries the generalised reflection coefficients of the stack, and overhead pairs keep Carson's air-side geometry; the amplitudes are documented in groundfield.coupling.sommerfeld_inductance. In the long-wire limit the correction gives Pollaczek's per-metre impedance, including the positive earth-return resistance of about \(\omega\mu_0/8\) per metre; for \(\sigma \to 0\) it vanishes (the soil is magnetically transparent).

    Numerics of the Sommerfeld correction¶

    • Image-free baseline. The correction is built against free space, so no image has to be added and removed again. Conductors that cross the soil surface or lie in it are solved (the pair integrals are split at \(z = 0\)); with the image conventions of the other models such a segment is singular and raises.
    • Exact layered kernel. For any number of layers the same-layer amplitude uses generalised reflection coefficients and all other pairs a batched interface solve; a three-layer earth whose lower two layers are equal equals the two-layer earth exactly.
    • Resolved \(\lambda\) grid. The logarithmic region of the quadrature in \(\lambda\) ends before \(J_0\) oscillates, the linear region carries 16 nodes per \(J_0\) period up to four times the largest physical scale, and an exhausted panel budget warns (SommerfeldResolutionWarning) instead of truncating silently.
    • Multi-frequency assembly. Geometry, \(\lambda\) grid and Bessel tables are built once per pair for the whole frequency set of a solve, bit-identical to single-frequency assemblies, with a bounded cache.
    • Long segments. A single 16 × 16 rule over a segment cannot resolve \(e^{-\gamma R}\) along a segment much longer than the skin depth. A pair of segments longer than a quarter skin depth of the most conductive layer gets a composite outer rule (16 nodes per piece, at most 512 pieces, then a SommerfeldResolutionWarning) and a denser table of the spectral integral over the horizontal distance.
    • Self terms and close pairs (user decision Q34). Along the line of closest approach of a segment with itself, or of two segments that come close (wires beside each other, a core in its shield), the integrand has a kink — the \(\gamma^2R/2\) term of \((e^{-\gamma R}-1)/R\) and its counterpart in the spectral part, smoothed only over the decay length \(z + z'\) — which a tensor-product rule cannot follow. The self term of every segment is therefore the exact one-dimensional reduction \(2\int_0^L (L-u)\,g(u)\,\mathrm du\) of its double integral (\(g(u)\) the integrand at the axial separation \(u\)), with 16-node panels graded from \(z + z'\) up to a quarter skin depth, uniform up to 40 skin depths and doubling beyond. Close pairs (closest distance below half the longer segment) get a graded rule along their line of closest approach (a coinciding pair: the self rule). The self correction is within 1.1e-9 of a refined rule and of an independent reference from 1 m and 0.01 skin depths to 3 km at 50 Hz and 1 kHz (buried at 0.7 m in 100, 1000/30 and 30/1000 Ω·m, overhead at 10 m over 10 Ω·m) and continuous in the length (steps below 3e-13); close pairs are within about 4e-8 (3.5e-8 for two 1 m wires 1 mm apart in 30 over 1000 Ω·m at 1 kHz). Both rest on the 256-node logarithmic region of the \(\lambda\) grid.
    • Very long conductors. Both rules cost at most linearly in the length: one lumped conductor of 2 km from a rod to a junction, 50 Hz and 1 kHz, is solved in 1.1 s within 0.21 GB, 100 km in 10.6 s within 0.24 GB. Beyond about 33 km such a conductor is not converged and the solve warns (SommerfeldResolutionWarning; the resolved \(\lambda\) grid reaches \(2^{17}\cdot 2\pi\,(z + z')/30\), 38 km for a conductor at 0.7 m).
    • Tabulated assembly (the default since 2026.11, Engine.sommerfeld_assembly="tabulated"; user decision Q16). Sub-segments are grouped by depth, and one table of the spectral integral per pair of depth groups and frequency replaces the per-pair quadrature. It is much faster on large networks (a 30-house TN network 306 s → 12 s) and agrees with a converged reference better than the per-pair rule (sommerfeld_assembly="exact"), whose 24-node interpolation in the horizontal distance dominates its error (2e-10 against 1.2e-8 of two refined references). Measured times are on the performance page.
    Measurements and audit trail

    What 2026.11 changed (ADR-0006 amendment):

    • Image-free baseline (findings 10.46, F52): the numbers equal the former additive pairing to round-off.
    • Exact layered kernel (10.35): a three-layer earth whose lower two layers are equal was +17…+25 % in \(X'\) before.
    • Resolved \(\lambda\) grid (10.36): distant pair entries were off by −24 % (200 m) and +274 % (400 m) before.
    • Multi-frequency assembly (10.60).
    • Long segments (10.24, audits of 2026.11): a lumped 3 km PEN read +13.4 % earth-return resistance at 1 kHz in 100 Ω·m.
    • Self terms and close pairs (user decision Q34): up to 2026.11.0 the self correction of a buried segment was 4.8e-4 off at a quarter skin depth (growing like \(L/\delta\) below it), stepped by 3.5e-4 where the composite rule took over, and was within 5.9e-4 for longer segments; two wires 1 mm apart were 1.5e-3 off in 30 over 1000 Ω·m. With the 64 nodes of earlier builds in the logarithmic region of the \(\lambda\) grid, short segments were 1e-5 … 7.4e-5 off at 0.01 skin depths and up to 3e-4 at 1 m, about 1e-8 of a solved impedance (audit W3B-01).
    • Very long conductors: the 2 km conductor took 28 s and 1.6 GB, the 100 km one more than 7.8 GB (audit W2B-05); the limit at about 33 km is audit W3B-10.

    Carson on its own baseline¶

    Carson (1926) derived the earth-return impedance of a line over homogeneous earth as a correction to the impedance of the line and its anti-parallel image in a perfectly conducting plane. The correction is \((\omega\mu_0/\pi)(P + jQ)\) per metre, where Carson's series \(P\) and \(Q\) depend on the dimensionless parameter \(a = D\sqrt{\omega\mu_0\sigma}\) — \(D\) the distance between conductor and image, \(2h\) for a self term at height or depth \(h\) — and on the angle of the image direction. It is a per-metre result for infinitely long parallel horizontal wires, so carson_series uses it only inside that validity domain:

    • Near-parallel horizontal pairs on one side of the surface — \(\lvert\cos\varphi\rvert > 0.99\) (\(\varphi\) the angle between the two segments) and both segments inclined by less than 8.1° — get the per-metre correction at their horizontal axis distance, times the length over which their projections overlap; the self term of a horizontal segment is the per-metre value times its length. Pairs that do not overlap (staggered, end to end) and collinear pieces of one wire get none: the per-metre self value on the diagonal already contains the rest of the same wire. The tiling converges under refinement. Two conductors that overlap on one axis are wires lying side by side and get the correction at the distance of touching wires, \(r_i + r_j\) (the sum of their radii).
    • Pairs across the surface — an overhead wire parallel to a buried one — have no anti-parallel baseline (a perfect conductor screens the soil). They couple through the field transmitted into the soil, carson_mixed_mutual_correction: Carson's \(J\) plus the bounded remainder of the buried wire's attenuation (Ametani et al. 2009).
    • Tilted near-parallel pairs. Where a near-parallel pair comes close within its overlap (its smallest distance there is below 30 times the change of its offset), the per-metre correction is integrated over the overlap (Gauss–Legendre nodes) instead of being taken at the mid-point distance, so a lumped pair and its pieces add up: a core crossing its shield's axis at mid-length differs by at most 8.0e-6 of the loop reactance. Other pairs keep one entry at the mid-point distance, exact to second order there.
    • Everything else — oblique pairs and every pair and self term of a segment inclined by more than 8.1° (risers, sloping leads) — gets the exact Sommerfeld correction of a homogeneous earth of the same conductivity, moved onto the anti-parallel baseline.

    At the 8.1° boundary the result steps from the per-metre tiling to the Sommerfeld value, by the error of the per-metre model there: 13 % for two 1 km leads 2 m apart at 1 kHz, more for shorter leads (42 % for two 300 m leads at 50 Hz). That step is the price of keeping Carson's closed form where it is accurate; for oblique or short conductors use sommerfeld.

    On this baseline a 1 km isolated PEN reproduces \(l\,X'_\text{Carson}\) within 1 % (tests/test_inductive_solver_integration.py::test_carson_series_reactance_matches_carson). Carson's small-\(a\) series is evaluated to \(a^{12}\) and the self-term quadrature at \(\theta = 0\) to round-off, so the regime boundaries are continuous; the asymptotic expansion is used for \(a > 15\).

    carson_series remains an approximation for short, inclined or buried conductors and for layered soil (an effective homogeneous conductivity is used); it is the fast choice for long parallel lines over homogeneous earth, not a replacement of sommerfeld in general.

    Networks of short cables. Tiled over the pieces of service drops that are shorter than the skin depth (30 … 60 m against 159 m at 1 kHz in 100 Ω·m), Carson's infinite-line correction overstates the earth return of an LV network. For the generated star, radial and grid networks of 5 … 100 houses with an insulated Neumann PEN, carson_series reads the input reactance 4 … 21 % and the resistance up to 11 % high at 1 kHz, and the reactance 21 … 50 % and the resistance up to 0.9 % high at 50 Hz; at 50 Hz it is then no closer than the diagnostic perfect_mirror for four of the five networks measured (note below). A change of such a network is reproduced by carson_series only within that error, possibly with the opposite sign. Use sommerfeld for LV networks.

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Validity domain of carson_series: since the second adversarial audit of 2026.11 (A2C-1). Up to then an oblique pair got \(\sqrt{\ell_i\ell_j}\,\lvert\cos\varphi\rvert\) times the per-metre value once per segment pair, which grew with the number of segments (two 1 km leads at 9°: 20× the Sommerfeld coupling). Pairs across the surface: the solver used to treat the buried wire as an overhead wire at height \(\lvert z\rvert\), which gave 36 % too little mutual reactance for a lead 1 m above a PEN at 0.7 m depth at 1 kHz.
    • Tilted near-parallel pairs (audit W1R3-07): before, a core crossing its shield's axis at mid-length differed by 4.3e-4 of the loop reactance at 2e-3 rad and 5.2e-2 at 4e-2 rad; generated networks are unchanged by the rule.
    • 0.15.0 (finding 10.20 / F32): the solver added Carson's correction to the additive image matrix of the perfect mirror, which roughly doubled the earth-return reactance: 1.66–2.25 times Carson's value for 100 m – 1 km lines, +41 % on \(\lvert Z\rvert\) at 1 kHz for a TN network with inductive PEN conductors. Since 2026.11 the matrix is built on the anti-parallel image.
    • Networks of short cables (audits W3B-02, W3R2B-03). Soils: 100 Ω·m and two-layer soils of 30 … 200 Ω·m. Reference: sommerfeld with a refined discretisation (PEN in 2.5 m pieces, 1 m segments); the default sommerfeld agrees with it to 1e-3 for the 5-house radial network and to 4e-5 for the 80- and 100-house ones. Per network, input reactance at 1 kHz / 50 Hz: the 5-house radial network +13 % / +31 %, the 10-house radial network +20 % / +41 %, the 30-house star +17 % / +44 %, the 80-house radial network +19 % / +50 %; the 100-house radial network R +11 % / +0.9 %. At 50 Hz carson_series is no closer than perfect_mirror for the 10-house radial network, the 30-house star and the 80- and 100-house radial networks (perfect_mirror +40 / +37 / +41 / +41 % there), but closer for the 5-house radial network (perfect_mirror +45 %). The error changes with the geometry: across the cabinet fix of audit W1B-01/02 the star's reactance moves by +1.18 % under carson_series and −3.54 % under sommerfeld (the refined reference: −3.54 %), between two house spacings around the cabinet threshold of the 100-house grid by +1.81 / −2.77 % (reference −2.77 %), and of the 50-house grid by −21.6 / −18.9 % (reference −18.9 %).

    Equivalent depth of layered soil¶

    The complex-depth formulas of Dubanton and Deri replace a homogeneous earth by a perfectly conducting plane at the complex depth \(p = \sqrt{\rho/(j\omega\mu_0)}\) and give the line impedance in closed form. gf.penetration_depth(soil, frequencies) returns the \(p\) (and the equivalent resistivity \(j\omega\mu_0 p^2\)) for which these formulas reproduce the earth return of any layer stack: the plane-wave surface impedance of the stack over \(j\omega\mu_0\), which matches the layered surface reflection coefficient to second order in the horizontal wavenumber. Over the 39 soils of the AP1 grid, 50 Hz … 1 kHz and 0.1 … 1000 m, the earth-return correction of two overhead or buried wires is reproduced within a median of 2.2 % (the formula's own median error in homogeneous soil: 2.4 %), at most 28 … 33 % where the separation approaches \(\lvert p\rvert\); the homogeneous depth of \(\rho_1\) is off by up to 2800 %. Method and numbers: Soil. The solver's models do not use it.

    Perfect mirror¶

    perfect_mirror treats the earth as a perfect magnetic mirror (additive image, no correction). It is cheap and frequency independent but far from the physics of buried conductors below 1 kHz; it was the default before 0.13.0 and is kept as a baseline for comparisons.

    Its image is applied to every pair of inductive segments, also to a pair of which one segment lies above the soil surface (\(z < 0\), an overhead lead) and one below it (a buried PEN or cable). Such a pair has no magnetic mirror between them in any physical sense, so the additive image has no basis there. The numbers are kept as they are (a baseline), but the solve emits one UserWarning that counts these pairs, names the conductors on each side and points to "sommerfeld"; carson_series and sommerfeld treat such pairs through the field transmitted into the soil and do not warn. Segments in or across the surface plane are rejected under this image convention.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The warning on pairs across the surface exists since 2026.11 (user decision Q27).

    Conductor details¶

    • Touching segments. Chain neighbours and bends share an end point where the Neumann integrand is logarithmically singular; Grover's vertex formula and the coaxial gap formula replace the 16 × 16 Gauss–Legendre rule by default (touching="exact"), so a straight conductor keeps the inductance of the unsplit wire for every discretisation (tests/test_inductive_solver_integration.py::test_inductance_is_refinement_invariant_for_a_straight_conductor). touching="quadrature" reproduces 0.15.0.
    • Lumped and single-segment conductors. A conductor with an inductance_model that the topology keeps as one branch is one inductive branch from start to end, coupled to all others.
    • Close pairs. Pairs that miss the closed forms — nearly parallel wires close together, crossing and skew pairs, a branch starting on another segment — are integrated semi-analytically (inner integral in closed form, outer Gauss–Legendre panels graded towards the near-singular points). Measured against mpmath: 5e-11 for directions that differ by more than 1e-6 rad, 1.0e-9 for filaments crossing at 1e-8 rad; filaments from one vertex to round-off on axis-parallel geometry (4e-10 in a general orientation).

    • Wires beside each other. Where a point of one wire lies beside another wire — its foot on that wire's axis falls inside it — closer than the sum of the radii \(r_i + r_j\), the filament integral is meaningless: it diverges for coincident axes (two coincident 50 m leads read 1.87 H) and exceeds the self inductance once the wires are closer than about their radius, which makes the inductance matrix indefinite (a network whose service drops ran back along the PEN main had a negative input reactance). There the distance from the other axis is taken as \(r_i + r_j\) — two wires touching side by side, as two cables in one trench lie — pointwise inside the Neumann integral, both one-sided integrals averaged.

      A core inside a shield. Where the thinner wire runs inside the thicker one and the thicker one is a cable shield (conductor_type="cable_shield", user decision Q32), the shield radius is used instead: a core inside a shield is a filament inside a thin tube and couples to it as at the tube radius, and since the shield carries no internal inductance (user decision Q33) the loop gives the coaxial inductance per metre

      \[ L'_\text{coax} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\Bigl(\ln\frac{r_s}{r_c} + \frac14\Bigr), \]

      the flux between the core of radius \(r_c\) and the shield of radius \(r_s\) plus the internal inductance of the solid core: 26.97 µH for 100 m with a 5 mm core and a 15 mm shield (31.97 µH with the internal inductance of a solid shield). Two unmarked conductors on one axis are separate cables and touch (43.47 µH for the same pair).

      Pieces add up to the lumped pair. A wire inside a shield couples at the shield radius at every axial offset, and for two other conductors lying beside each other a point lies beside the partner when its foot falls inside the partner's whole conductor: the sub-pieces of the two conductors then add up to the lumped pair (within 3e-12). That holds for pieces that are not parallel too, at any angle and whatever their length — a core tilted against its shield or crossing its axis, wires leaving a junction or crossing at a small angle: the stretches inside, beside and away from the partner are found exactly, and a core leaving the shield's tube along its length adds up as well. The value does not depend on the order or the direction of the segments, is continuous in the geometry, and leaves chain neighbours, bends turning by less than 90° and pairs farther apart unchanged. For unequal radii it steps where the thinner axis leaves the thicker wire, from "inside" to "side by side": the two describe different cables. Equal radii never count as one wire inside the other.

      At the low level, build_inductance_matrix(..., shields=[False, True], include_internal=[True, False], conductors=[0, 1]) does the same. The marker leaves the self terms alone, so a shield needs include_internal=False (else it keeps the 50 nH/m of a solid wire, 31.97 µH), and conductors labels the pieces of each conductor (without it every segment is a conductor of its own: unmarked touching pieces of one cable then do not add up, 40.34 instead of 43.47 µH in 0.25 m pieces, while a marked shield needs no labels). - Diagnostics. conductors.find_close_wires and diagnose() report crossing, touching, interpenetrating and close wires; diagnose() also reports inductive conductors that lie beside each other closer than touching over a stretch (category overlap), the pairs the rule above evaluates as touching wires.

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Touching segments, close pairs, wires beside each other: finding 10.46. With the 16 × 16 rule close pairs were +12 % at 10 cm and up to +250 % at 1 cm for two 5 m segments.
    • Lumped and single-segment conductors (finding 10.24): up to 0.15.0 their inductance was silently dropped (a 300 m PEN read 0.0564 Ω at 1 kHz instead of 0.210 + 3.78j Ω).
    • Shields: before user decision Q33 a shield carried the internal inductance of a solid wire (31.97 µH for the coax above).
    • Pieces adding up to the lumped pair (audit W1A-02; before, the coax in 1 m pieces read 2.2 % low). Audits W1R2-01, W1R3-01 and W1R3-02 extended the rule to tilted and crossing pieces: a core tilted by 4.5e-5 rad read 7.4 % low in 0.25 m pieces, with a step at the parallel tolerance; until W1R3 the rule acted only while the lateral distance changed by at most \(r_i + r_j\) along a piece and for \(\lvert\cos\varphi\rvert \ge 0.99\), and beyond either bound the rule of the single segments mixed in: a core crossing the shield's axis at mid-length read −8.6e-5 of the loop off the lumped pair in 10 m pieces, shield pieces of mixed length −1.6e-3, two wires leaving a junction stepped by 1.5e-3 at 8.1°. The conductors labels of build_inductance_matrix: audit W1R2-08.
    • Diagnostics: finding 10.44; the overlap category of diagnose() exists since 2026.11.

    Skin effect¶

    With Conductor.resistance_model="skin" (opt-in) every branch of the conductor adds \(\ell_b\,\Delta Z'_\text{skin}(f)\) to its diagonal (\(\ell_b\) the branch length): the exact internal impedance per metre of a solid round conductor of radius \(a\) and resistivity \(\rho\),

    \[ Z'_\text{int} = \frac{k\rho}{2\pi a}\,\frac{I_0(ka)}{I_1(ka)},\qquad k = \sqrt{j\omega\mu_0/\rho}, \]

    which describes the current crowding towards the surface of the conductor (\(I_0\), \(I_1\) the modified Bessel functions of the first kind), taken relative to the DC convention (\(R'_\text{dc}\) plus the internal inductance \(\mu_0/(8\pi)\) already in \(L\)). For 150 mm² aluminium the resistance rises by the factors 1.056, 1.231 and 1.550 at 250, 550 and 1000 Hz and the internal inductance falls to 0.740 of its DC value at 1 kHz (tests/test_skin_effect.py); the IEC 60287-1-1 skin factor agrees to about 1 %. A conductor without an inductance_model has no inductance at all, so only the resistance part applies to it. The default resistance_model="dc" keeps the numbers and the JSON of earlier releases.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The skin-effect model is finding 10.37 of review pass 10.

    Validation¶

    • Carson: the solver's 1 km PEN against \(l\,X'_\text{Carson}\) (test above); Carson's \(P\) and \(Q\) against an independent mpmath quadrature across all regimes (tests/test_carson_pq.py).
    • Sommerfeld against Pollaczek: benchmark B13 takes the per-metre series impedance of an isolated line from the difference of a 1600 m and an 800 m line (which cancels the end effects) and compares it with the conductor-earth loop impedance of references.pollaczek; the generated numbers are on the validation page (rows pollaczek_x, pollaczek_r). Distant pair entries against an independent quadrature: tests/test_sommerfeld_accuracy.py.
    • references.pollaczek itself uses the exact two-layer amplitude and the \(K_0\) head and agrees with an independent boundary-value solution to \(10^{-7}\) (tests/test_pollaczek_layered.py).
    • Engine level: compare_engines compares complex impedances at every frequency, so a reactance mismatch between earth models cannot pass as consistent (benchmark B13 row image_vs_mom).
