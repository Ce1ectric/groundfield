    image — homogeneous soil¶

    This page explains the default engine for homogeneous soil: the physical model, the equations it solves, how accurate its results are and where it must not be used. Read it before you rely on image for touch and step voltages next to a grid, a star or a bonded cluster.

    At a glance
    Soil HomogeneousSoil; a TwoLayerSoil is forwarded to image_2layer
    Green's function point source plus its air image, closed form
    Current model one uniform leakage current per straight wire (user decision Q30)
    Kernel exact line source in Galerkin form (default); kernel="point" for the midpoint point source
    Unknowns one current per straight wire, node potentials, branch currents
    Accuracy exact for its model; impedances about 1 % from per-segment mom on grids, a few per cent on stars; near-field quantities up to tens of per cent (Validation)
    Status default engine; cim and image_nlayer are deprecated names that run it

    What it solves¶

    image is the classical grounding calculation of Sunde, Tagg and Dwight. It computes the stationary current field of buried electrodes and the potentials and impedances that follow from it, under these assumptions:

    • Soil. A homogeneous, isotropic half-space of resistivity \(\rho\) (in Ω·m), bounded at \(z = 0\) by insulating air. Depth \(z\) counts positive downwards.
    • Quasi-static field. No displacement current, and a skin depth large against the electrode (frequencies below 1 kHz, see the engine family). The galvanic solution is then independent of the frequency: one solve serves every frequency of a galvanic world.
    • Thin wires. The wire radius \(a\) is small against the segment length. The current flows on the wire axis and the potential is observed on the wire surface.
    • Uniform current per straight wire. Every straight wire of an electrode leaks one current that is uniform along its length: a grid has one current per full wire line, a star one per arm, a polyline one per straight run (a building outline one per side). Rods, strips and rings are one wire each. The wires of an electrode are ideally bonded, so they share one potential, and every result is reported per electrode (user decisions Q30 and Q44).
    • Average potential. The potential of a wire is the average of the field over the wire (Galerkin testing), not the value at one point.

    For this model image is exact. What remains is the error of the model itself, chiefly the uniform current, which the Validation section quantifies.

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    Green's function of the half-space¶

    A point current \(I\) (in A) injected at \(\mathbf r_s = (x_s, y_s, z_s)\) with \(z_s > 0\) produces the potential \(\varphi\) (in V) that satisfies

    \[ \nabla^2\varphi = -\rho I\,\delta(\mathbf r - \mathbf r_s), \qquad \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=0} = 0 . \]

    The first equation says that the injected current is the only source of the field; the second that no current crosses the soil surface into the air. The image construction solves the problem in closed form: a second source of the same sign at the mirror point \(\mathbf r_s' = (x_s, y_s, -z_s)\) makes the normal derivative vanish on \(z = 0\), so

    \[ \varphi(\mathbf r) = \frac{\rho I}{4\pi}\left( \frac{1}{\lvert\mathbf r - \mathbf r_s\rvert} + \frac{1}{\lvert\mathbf r - \mathbf r_s'\rvert}\right). \]

    Physically, the insulating surface doubles the potential of a source at the surface: the current that would flow into the upper half-space is reflected back into the soil.

    Potential of a straight wire¶

    A straight segment of length \(L\) from \(A\) to \(B\) that leaks the current \(I\) uniformly along its length produces, at a point \(X\) with the end-point distances \(R_1 = \lvert X - A\rvert\) and \(R_2 = \lvert X - B\rvert\),

    \[ \varphi_\text{line}(X) = \frac{\rho I}{4\pi L} \ln\frac{R_1 + R_2 + L}{R_1 + R_2 - L}. \]

    This is the line integral of the point-source potential along the segment. It holds for any orientation of the segment; its air image is the mirrored segment. Since \(R_1 + R_2\) is constant on prolate spheroids with foci \(A\) and \(B\), the equipotentials of a uniformly loaded segment are those spheroids.

    Current model and discretisation¶

    Every electrode is cut into straight segments no longer than Engine.segment_length (rings into chords, meshes and grids into their wire pieces between crossings). The unknown currents belong to owners: an owner is one straight wire of an electrode. Owner \(o\) of total length \(L_o\) leaks the current \(I_o\), distributed uniformly per unit length, so its segment \(k\) of length \(L_k\) carries

    \[ I_k = I_o\,\frac{L_k}{L_o}. \]

    Let \(K_{kl}\) (in 1/m) be the reaction coefficient: \(\rho/(4\pi)\) times \(K_{kl}\) is the average potential on segment \(k\) per ampere leaked uniformly by segment \(l\), air image included. The potential of owner \(o\) is the length-weighted average of its segment potentials, with the same weights \(L_k/L_o\) that distribute its current. With the weight matrix \(W\), \(W_{ko} = L_k/L_o\) for \(k \in o\) and zero otherwise, the owner multiport matrix is

    \[ Z_{oo'} = \frac{\rho}{4\pi}\sum_{k \in o}\sum_{l \in o'} \frac{L_k}{L_o}\,K_{kl}\,\frac{L_l}{L_{o'}} = \frac{\rho}{4\pi}\,\bigl(W^\top K W\bigr)_{oo'} . \]

    \(Z_{oo'}\) is the average potential of wire \(o\) per ampere leaked by wire \(o'\). This is the Galerkin reduction dual to the uniform-current ansatz: the same weights distribute the current and average the potential, so \(Z_{oo'} = Z_{o'o}\) whenever \(K\) is symmetric, and the multiport matrices of the engine are reciprocal.

    Source kernel¶

    \(K\) uses the exact line-source kernel by default (Engine.kernel=None resolves to "line" for this family). Every pair of segments is the double integral of the reduced kernel \(1/\sqrt{\lvert\mathbf x - \mathbf y\rvert^2 + a^2}\) over both segments: the current on the axis seen from the surface, which keeps the self term finite. For a straight wire the result is then independent of the segment length. The legacy point kernel (kernel="point") places each segment's current at its midpoint and uses the collocation self term \(2\ln(L/a)/L\); its grounding resistance converges to a value about 1–2 % below the uniform-current solution, at every segment length. The kernels page derives both and shows where the difference comes from.

    A concrete encasement enters as the coefficient \(C\) (in Ω·m) of the electrode, the radial resistance of the shell times its length: every segment adds \(C/L_k\) to its diagonal entry (concrete shells).

    Network solve¶

    The owner multiport matrix enters the augmented nodal system of the engine family:

    • electrodes bonded by ideal conductors share a node potential, and all owners of an electrode belong to its node;
    • finite conductors are branches with their resistance, internal and partial inductance and earth-return correction;
    • junctions are nodes without soil contact;
    • a current source injects \(+I\) at attached_to and \(-I\) at return_to;
    • a voltage source adds its current as an unknown and fixes \(\varphi_a - \varphi_r + Z_q I = U\) (modified nodal analysis), with the potentials \(\varphi_a\) and \(\varphi_r\) of its two terminals and its internal impedance \(Z_q\).

    Source-free clusters float with zero net current. A lone passive electrode then carries no current (under the uniform ansatz its leakage would have to be zero everywhere) and reports the potential the field imposes; a bonded group of passive electrodes exchanges current between its members to stay one equipotential. The reported potential of an electrode is the length-weighted average over its wires, which equals the potential of its cluster. The impedances that can be read from the result (input, cluster and electrode impedance) are explained on sources and measurement.

    Field evaluation after the solve¶

    FieldResult.potential(points) superposes the segment sources with the kernel of the solve:

    • with the line kernel, the exact line potential near a segment and the point form plus its quadrupole term farther away;
    • with the point kernel, point sources with a 1 mm distance floor.

    Surface potentials next to a wire are therefore correct with the default kernel. The point form misses the field above a wire by several per cent when the segment length is comparable to the distance (kernels).

    Numerics¶

    Convergence. With the line kernel a straight wire gives the same resistance at every segment length: the generated kernel check below shows a 3 m rod and a 10 m strip within about \(10^{-6}\) of their exact uniform-current resistance at every segment length it tests. A ring converges like the square of the segment length, because its chords approximate the circle (−0.24 % at 0.5 m, −1.5e-4 at 0.125 m for a ring of radius 2 m). The point kernel converges as well, but to a value 1–2 % below the uniform-current solution, so a segment-length study cannot reveal that offset (kernels). convergence_study runs such a study for any world.

    Cost. The reaction matrix needs one kernel evaluation per pair of segments: \(O(N^2)\) time for \(N\) segments and \(8N^2\) bytes. It is kept in memory while it fits 1 GiB (\(N \le 11\,585\)) and evaluated in row blocks beyond. The network system has one unknown per straight wire, node and finite branch and is small. A galvanic world is solved once for all frequencies; inductive conductors add one complex solve per frequency. The line kernel takes 3.8 times as long as the point kernel for 16 house grids (\(N\) = 1920) with numba installed, 5.8 times without (kernels). Engine.max_segments (default diagnostics.HARD_LIMIT = 20 000 segments) stops a larger world before anything of size \(N\) is allocated. FieldResult.potential works in row blocks of at most 16 MiB, so large grids of field points do not allocate (points × sources × 3) tensors. Timings of whole networks are on the performance page.

    Error estimates. The engine reports no error estimate of its own: within its model and with the line kernel the discretisation error is essentially the chord error of curved wires (the far-form remainder of the kernel stays near \(10^{-6}\) in the kernel check). The model error (the uniform current) is estimated by a second engine on the same world: compare_engines with mom, or diagnose() for the warnings that apply.

    Validation¶

    Two effects make up the accuracy of image, and they differ in size:

    • Impedances are accurate to about a per cent on grids and a few per cent on stars against mom on the same discretisation (one current per segment, same point kernel). Rings and single rods are exact by symmetry or nearly so.
    • Field quantities next to the electrode (touch, step and mesh voltages within a few metres of a grid corner or a star tip) can be off by about ten per cent next to a grid corner and by tens of per cent next to a star tip, with the step voltage under-estimated — the non-conservative direction for a safety assessment.

    The generated tables below give the measured values; the kernel check at their end compares both kernels with the exact uniform-current resistance. References and all cases are on the validation page.

    Field quantities and the uniform-current ansatz¶

    The uniform-current ansatz is impedance-grade, not field-grade. The impedance averages the potential over the electrode, and a wrong current distribution changes that average only to second order. A surface potential next to a grid corner or the tip of a star arm depends on the local current density, which the ansatz gets wrong: a real grid leaks more current at its corners and edges than in its interior, a star more at its tips. One current per wire line lets the outer lines of a grid carry more than the inner ones; along each wire, and along each arm of a star, the current is still uniform.

    In the near-field table below the touch voltage has its feet 1 m outside the corner or tip, and the step starts at the surface point above the corner or tip and reaches 1 m outwards. The warning text and groundfield.postprocess.safety quote the step from 1 m to 2 m outside instead, next to the touch voltage at 1 m: at the grid corner the image family reads +6 % / −8 % with the default line kernel (+5 % / −8 % with the point kernel).

    The safety helpers therefore warn: touch_voltage, step_voltage, touch_voltage_envelope and touch_voltage_at_part emit UniformCurrentFieldWarning when a result of the image family is evaluated within 5 m (NEAR_FIELD_DISTANCE_M) of a grid, mesh, star, strip, open polyline or a bonded multi-electrode cluster. For a safety assessment of such geometries solve with mom (or compare both); for impedances, rho-f samples and far-field potentials the image family is the right tool.

    Validity range¶

    Condition Limit Behaviour outside
    Soil HomogeneousSoil; two layers go to image_2layer BackendCapabilityError for three distinct layers
    Frequency quasi-static, \(f < 1\) kHz not checked
    Thin wire \(a \ll\) segment length; \(L \le a\) rejected, \(L < 5a\) reported by diagnose() ValueError
    Burial every segment in the soil, \(2z > a\) ValueError naming the electrode
    Shallow segments, point kernel \(L \le 4z\) ShallowSegmentWarning with the bias
    Field quantities within 5 m of grids, stars, strip ends, bonded clusters ansatz error up to tens of per cent UniformCurrentFieldWarning
    Size Engine.max_segments, default 20 000 segments ResourceLimitError before allocation

    Notes on the rows:

    • Frequency. The galvanic kernel is frequency independent, so the limit is not checked; above about 1 kHz the quasi-static model itself no longer holds.
    • Burial. The mirror image of a wire must not touch the wire itself, so a wire at depth \(z\) needs \(2z > a\).
    • Shallow segments. The point form of a segment's own image is biased high when the segment is longer than four times its depth; the warning quotes the bias in per cent. The default line kernel is not affected.
    • Field quantities. Use mom for touch, step and mesh voltages next to such electrodes, or compare both engines (Validation).
    • Size. The reaction matrix is kept in memory up to \(N = 11\,585\) segments and evaluated in blocks beyond; raise Engine.max_segments to solve a larger world on purpose.

    What to use instead, and the other limits of the family: known limitations; the warnings: warnings reference.

    Audit trail¶

    One current per straight wire (user decisions Q30 and Q44)

    Before 2026.11 the uniform current belonged to the whole electrode. Since user decisions Q30 and Q44 it belongs to every straight wire of an electrode. With one current for the whole grid, the 20 m grid with 4 × 4 meshes read +3.9 % against mom, and the touch and step voltages 1 m and 1 m … 2 m outside its corner read +20 % / −21 % (now +6 % / −8 % with the line kernel, +5 % / −8 % with the point kernel). The generated tables are measured with one current per straight wire.

    Kernel default (ADR-0015)

    The exact line kernel is the default of the image family since 2026.11. The point kernel was the only kernel up to 0.15.0 and reproduces its numbers (kernel="point").
