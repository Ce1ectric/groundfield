    mom — point-matching method of moments¶

    This page explains the engine that solves for one current per segment: the integral equation behind it, the point-matching scheme, why it is the check of the image family's current model rather than an accuracy reference, and its limits. Read it when you use mom for touch and step voltages near grids and stars, or to cross-check image and image_2layer.

    At a glance
    Soil homogeneous or two layers (a MultiLayerSoil whose canonical stack has at most two layers)
    Green's function the kernels of the image family: point source plus air image, Tagg / Sunde series, the Sommerfeld kernel of ADR-0007 across the interface
    Current model one pulse current per segment
    Testing delta at the segment midpoints (point matching, collocation) with a point-source near field
    Kernel point only (an explicit kernel="line" raises BackendCapabilityError)
    Accuracy resolves the current distribution; absolute impedances 0.2 … 2 % low, drifting under refinement (Numerics)
    Status current; bem is a deprecated name that runs it

    What it solves¶

    mom solves the same physical problem as the image family — the quasi-static current field of thin-wire electrodes in homogeneous or two-layer soil, see image and image_2layer — but drops the uniform-current ansatz. Each segment carries its own leakage current, so the solve resolves how current distributes along an electrode (end and corner effects), between the electrodes of a bonded cluster and across a layer interface.

    Its role in the family is to check the current model of the image family on the same kernel and discretisation. It is not an accuracy reference for impedances at the 1 % level, which its point-source near field does not reach.

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    The integral equation¶

    With the Green's function \(G(\mathbf r, \mathbf r')\) of the soil (the potential at \(\mathbf r\) per ampere injected at \(\mathbf r'\), homogeneous or two-layer, see image and image_2layer) the potential on the surface of a thin wire is the integral of the leakage current density \(\iota(l)\) (in A/m, \(l\) the arc length) along all wires,

    \[ \varphi(\mathbf r) = \int_{\text{wires}} G(\mathbf r, \mathbf r')\,\iota(l')\,\mathrm dl' . \]

    Every point of an electrode must sit at the potential of its cluster, so the left-hand side is known on the wires and \(\iota\) is the unknown: a Fredholm integral equation of the first kind. Physically, the current distributes itself so that the metal is an equipotential.

    Method of moments with point matching¶

    The method of moments (Harrington 1968) expands \(\iota\) in basis functions and enforces the equation with test functions. mom uses the simplest choice:

    • basis: one constant current \(I_j\) per segment \(j\) (pulse functions);
    • testing: the equation at the segment midpoints (delta functions) — point matching, or collocation.

    The reaction matrix, the potential at midpoint \(i\) per ampere on segment \(j\), is

    \[ Z_{ij} = \frac{\rho}{4\pi}\,K_{ij},\qquad K_{ij} = \begin{cases} \text{potential at midpoint } i \text{ of a unit point source at midpoint } j, & i \ne j,\\[2pt] 2\ln(L_i/a_i)/L_i + \text{images}, & i = j, \end{cases} \]

    with \(L_i\) the length and \(a_i\) the wire radius of segment \(i\). The off-diagonal uses the point kernel of the soil (its images included); the diagonal uses the potential of the uniformly loaded segment on its own surface (the Galerkin self term for very short segments, \(L < 2.99\,a\)). The matrix is assembled directly, entry by entry.

    This is not a Galerkin scheme. Galerkin testing would average the potential over each segment and integrate each source along its segment (the exact line kernel of the image family does exactly that, for a uniform current). metadata["solver"] = "point_matching" says so.

    Network solve¶

    The segment currents \(\mathbf I_\text{seg}\) and the node potentials \(\boldsymbol\varphi_n\) enter the bordered system

    \[ \begin{bmatrix} Z & -C \\ C^{\top} & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf I_\text{seg} \\ \boldsymbol\varphi_n \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf 0 \\ \mathbf I_\text{in} \end{bmatrix}, \]

    with the incidence matrix \(C\) (\(C_{in} = 1\) if segment \(i\) belongs to node \(n\)) and the injected node currents \(\mathbf I_\text{in}\), plus one row and column per finite conductor branch, as in the engine family. The first block row says that every midpoint of a node sits at the node potential, the second that the segment currents of a node add up to its injected current. \(Z\) is symmetric; the bordered matrix as assembled is not, and is solved with a dense LU.

    Every node with soil contact is an unknown, also without a source: a source-free cluster floats with zero net current, so transfer impedances are reciprocal (B8) and passive networks carry their equalising currents. The node potential is reported as the mean of its segment potentials, which point matching makes equal.

    mom runs the shared pipeline of the image family (discretise, inject, topology, branches; ADR-0014): two-terminal sources (return_to), voltage sources (one current unknown and one constraint row each), junction nodes and inductive conductors behave exactly as with image.

    Concrete-encased wires¶

    A concrete-encased wire (concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m \(= C\), in Ω·m; ADR-0012) adds the radial resistance of each segment's shell, \(C/\Delta s_k\) for a segment of length \(\Delta s_k\), to the segment's diagonal entry, and the drop \(C I_k/\Delta s_k\) to its potential: the reported potential is that of the metal. With one owner per segment the image family solves the same system: the two agree to \(7\cdot10^{-16}\) with shells, in homogeneous, two-layer and cross-layer soil, and a ring, whose current stays uniform, rises by exactly \(C/(2\pi r)\) (to \(10^{-14}\); see concrete shells).

    Numerics¶

    Accuracy of the point-matching scheme¶

    The point-source near field underestimates the coupling of neighbouring segments of the same wire. For a collinear neighbour of length \(\Delta s\), the exact potential at the midpoint is the line integral \(\frac{1}{\Delta s}\int_{\Delta s/2}^{3\Delta s/2}\mathrm dx/x = \ln 3/\Delta s\) per unit current; the point source gives \(1/\Delta s\) (−9 %). The self image of a shallow segment is a point image, too. Both errors scale with the near-field terms themselves, so mom does not converge to the thin-wire limit under refinement: the B2 rod lies below the independent thin-wire Galerkin solution and the gap grows as the segments get shorter (generated table below). Consequences:

    • mom against image with kernel="point" isolates the effect of the current model: both use the same kernel, so their difference is the ansatz (validation: B6, B10, B13 and the field quantities).
    • mom against image with the default line kernel mixes the ansatz with the 1–2 % kernel difference.
    • Absolute impedances at the 1 % level are better taken from the image family with the line kernel where the ansatz holds (rings, rods, compact electrodes), or from a convergence study (convergence_study).

    ShallowSegmentWarning is emitted when a segment is longer than four times its depth, because the point self image is then biased high.

    Cost¶

    The matrix is dense, \(N \times N\) for \(N\) segments, and its LU factorisation costs \(O(N^3)\): it dominates for large \(N\), and an inductive world needs one factorisation per frequency. Memory and timings are on the performance page.

    Validation¶

    mom resolves the current distribution that the image family assumes uniform, so near-field quantities next to grid corners and star tips are its strength (the comparison table is on the image page). Its absolute impedances carry the point-kernel deficit: the B2 rod 1.4 % below the thin-wire limit at the finest segment length, the B3 ring 1.2 % below its closed form. Reciprocity holds to \(10^{-8}\).

    Validity range¶

    Condition Limit Behaviour outside
    Soil one or two distinct layers BackendCapabilityError for three distinct layers (use mom_sommerfeld)
    Kernel point BackendCapabilityError for an explicit kernel="line"
    Concrete shells thin shell per segment, \(C/\Delta s\) on the diagonal (ADR-0012) —
    Horizontal wires near \(h_1\), point form at least 0.6 segment lengths from \(h_1\), on either side PointKernelInterfaceWarning; within 0.387: BackendCapabilityError
    Rod ends at an interface head and tip more than one wire radius away (+21 % with the tip at \(h_1\)) InterfaceContactWarning
    Horizontal wires next to an interface axis outside the reach (3.9 wire radii at contrast 33; +3.6 % at 2 radii) InterfaceContactWarning
    Size dense \(N \times N\) matrix, one LU per inductive frequency, \(O(N^3)\) memory and time (performance)
    Frequency quasi-static, \(f < 1\) kHz —

    The interface limits are those of every thin-wire engine: the point form of the interface image needs short segments next to \(h_1\), and the thin wire has no end face and no current crowding to its near side; they are explained on the image_2layer page. What to do instead, and the other limits: known limitations of the current ansatz and kernels and of the soil layers; the warnings: warnings reference.

    Audit trail¶

    Assembly, naming and passive clusters (findings 10.29, 10.31, 10.59)
    • Up to 0.15.0 the reaction matrix was extracted by applying the image-family operator to an identity matrix, an \(O(N^3)\) product per image term with bit-identical entries; it is now assembled directly (finding 10.59).
    • Since review pass 10 the documentation and metadata["solver"] = "point_matching" call the scheme point matching, not Galerkin (finding 10.31).
    • Up to 0.15.0 passive clusters were dropped from the unknowns and read back as an unweighted field mean, which broke reciprocity; every node with soil contact is now an unknown (finding 10.29).
    Concrete shells (since 2026.11)

    Up to 2026.11 mom rejected concrete-encased electrodes. Since then it carries the per-segment shell term of ADR-0012.
