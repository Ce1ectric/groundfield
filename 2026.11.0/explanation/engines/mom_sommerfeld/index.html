    mom_sommerfeld — layered Green's function by quadrature¶

    This page explains the engine for soils of three and more layers: the layered Green's function as a Sommerfeld integral, how the integral is evaluated without silent truncation, what it costs and where its limits are. Read it when your soil model has more than two layers, or when you want the kernel reference for two-layer soil.

    At a glance
    Soil any number of layers (canonical stack; equal adjacent layers merged)
    Green's function the Sommerfeld integral with the recursive reflection coefficient, evaluated numerically
    Current model one pulse current per segment, point matching (the scheme of mom)
    Kernel point only
    Options MomSommerfeldOptions: lambda_max_factor, epsabs, epsrel, max_osc_panels, tabulate
    Accuracy the kernel to \(10^{-9}\) or better; the point-matching deficit of mom on impedances
    Status current; the only engine for three or more distinct layers

    What it solves¶

    mom_sommerfeld computes the quasi-static current field of thin-wire electrodes in a horizontally stratified half-space: layers \(i = 1 \dots n\) of resistivity \(\rho_i\) and thickness \(h_i\), the last one infinite, insulating air above. It evaluates the layered Green's function without expansion or fit: the reflection coefficient \(\Gamma_1(\lambda)\) of the whole stack enters the Sommerfeld integral as it is, and the integral is computed by quadrature for every pair of segments.

    That makes it the reference for the kernel: on two-layer soil it must reproduce the Tagg / Sunde series of mom on the same discretisation, and for three and more layers it is the only kernel there is. The discretisation and the current model are those of mom (one current per segment, point matching), so it is not an independent check of the point-matching scheme.

    Concrete-encased wires carry the per-segment shell term of mom (ADR-0012). On two-layer soil the two engines agree to about \(10^{-8}\) with and without shells where neither engine warns; under SeriesTruncationWarning (a truncated series) they can differ by 9 %. An encased ring in three layers rises by exactly \(C/(2\pi r)\) over the bare ring of the outer radius (\(C\) the shell coefficient, \(r\) the ring radius).

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    The Sommerfeld integral of a layered soil¶

    The potential of a point current \(I\) at depth \(z_s\) in the top layer, observed at horizontal distance \(s\) and depth \(z\) in the top layer, is

    \[ \varphi = \frac{\rho_1 I}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda\lvert z-z_s\rvert} + e^{-\lambda(z+z_s)} + \Gamma_1(\lambda)\bigl[e^{-\lambda(2h_1-\lvert z-z_s\rvert)} + e^{-\lambda(2h_1-z-z_s)}\bigr]} {1 - \Gamma_1(\lambda)\,e^{-2\lambda h_1}}\,J_0(\lambda s)\,\mathrm d\lambda , \]

    with the spatial frequency \(\lambda\) (in 1/m) and the Bessel function \(J_0\). It is the static limit of Sommerfeld's (1909) half-space problem: the potential is decomposed into cylindrical waves \(J_0(\lambda s)\), each of which is reflected by the stack below the top layer with the coefficient \(\Gamma_1(\lambda)\). The numerator holds the direct field, its air image and the first reflection; the denominator sums the reflections back and forth between the surface and the first interface.

    The reflection coefficient of the stack¶

    The reflection coefficient seen from the top layer is built bottom-up from the interface coefficients \(K_i = (\rho_{i+1}-\rho_i)/(\rho_{i+1}+\rho_i)\) of the interfaces \(i = 1 \dots n-1\):

    \[ \Gamma_{n-1} = K_{n-1},\qquad \Gamma_i(\lambda) = \frac{K_i + \Gamma_{i+1}(\lambda)\,e^{-2\lambda h_{i+1}}} {1 + K_i\,\Gamma_{i+1}(\lambda)\,e^{-2\lambda h_{i+1}}}. \]

    Each step combines the reflection at interface \(i\) with what comes back, attenuated by the round trip \(e^{-2\lambda h_{i+1}}\) through layer \(i+1\), from the stack below it. Short wavelengths (large \(\lambda\)) see only the first interface, \(\Gamma_1(\infty) = K_1\); long wavelengths see the deep layers. \(\Gamma_1\) is therefore constant only for two layers: for \(n = 2\) the integral expands into the image series of image_2layer, for \(n \ge 3\) no closed form exists.

    Electrodes and layers¶

    For \(n = 2\) with every segment in the top layer the diagonal of the reaction matrix is the series diagonal of mom, and the off-diagonal entries are quadratures; a world with an electrode at or below the interface is delegated to the two-layer Sommerfeld kernel of groundfield.coupling.layered_green (ADR-0007). For \(n \ge 3\) every segment must lie in the top layer.

    Numerics¶

    Quadrature¶

    The integrand does not decay for two points at the same depth: the direct term \(e^{-\lambda\lvert z-z_s\rvert}J_0(\lambda s)\) with \(z = z_s\) converges only through the oscillation of \(J_0\), and a truncation at \(\lambda_\text{max}\) leaves an error of order \(\sqrt{2/(\pi\lambda_\text{max}s)}\) that no quadrature tolerance can remove. The kernel is therefore split with Lipschitz' integral \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda d}J_0(\lambda s)\,\mathrm d\lambda = (s^2+d^2)^{-1/2}\):

    1. the direct term (\(d = \lvert z - z_s\rvert\)), its air image (\(d = z + z_s\)) and the leading interface reflection with \(\Gamma_1(\infty) = K_1\) (\(d = 2h_1 - z - z_s\)) are taken in closed form;
    2. the remainder decays at least like \(e^{-\lambda d_\text{rem}}\) with a strictly positive length \(d_\text{rem}\), even when both points sit on the interface, so its truncation error is bounded by \(e^{-\lambda_\text{max}d_\text{rem}}\);
    3. the remainder is integrated with Gauss–Legendre panels on a grid graded towards \(\lambda = 0\) (exponentials and \(\Gamma_1\)), with a geometric family down to the scale \((1-\Gamma_1(0))/(2h_1)\) of the reflection multiplier (which collapses for \(\lvert K_1\rvert \to 1\)), and subdivided at the half period \(\pi/s\) of \(J_0\);
    4. the grid is refined until two successive grids agree within \(\max(\varepsilon_\text{abs}, \varepsilon_\text{rel}\lvert G\rvert)\), \(G\) the kernel value.

    Failing that is reported, never silent: SommerfeldConvergenceWarning names the achieved and the requested tolerance; a capped oscillation budget (max_osc_panels) counts as non-convergence; metadata["converged"], ["converged_per_frequency"] and ["n_unconverged_entries"] record it. Distances are floored at 1 mm like in the image family; keep segment midpoints a few millimetres clear of an interface, where the floor would saturate the interface term.

    Cost: distinct pairs and depth-group tables¶

    Every distinct argument \((s, \lvert z-z_s\rvert, z+z_s)\) is integrated once. For \(n \ge 3\) the pairs of a depth class in the top layer share one function \(G(s)\) of the horizontal distance alone; with tabulate=True (the default, option of MomSommerfeldOptions) its smooth quadrature remainder is tabulated on 64 log-spaced nodes per decade of \(s\) and interpolated with a cubic spline in \(\ln s\), while the closed-form terms stay exact. Each table is checked at eight interval midpoints against the pointwise quadrature to \(10^{-7}\) of \(\lvert G\rvert\), refined once, and dropped in favour of pointwise quadrature when it fails. Benchmark B12 bounds the effect on the assembled matrix and the impedance by \(10^{-6}\) (generated table below); the measured speed-up is on the performance page. The current model adds the dense LU of mom, \(O(N^3)\) for \(N\) segments.

    Validation¶

    The kernel is checked against independent references: the exact Tagg / Sunde series for two layers (B4, within 2.0e-10 over 1050 points) and mpmath quadrature for three layers (B5, within 2.3e-13). On the same segments it reproduces mom on two-layer soil to about \(10^{-8}\) (B6, B7, B11). Impedances carry the point-matching deficit of mom, which these checks do not measure.

    Line kernel not supported BackendCapabilityError

    Size dense matrix, one quadrature per distinct pair argument seconds to minutes (performance)
    Frequency quasi-static, \(f < 1\) kHz —

    The thin-wire limits next to an interface (rod ends, horizontal wires, the point form near \(h_1\)) are those of mom. What to do instead, and the other limits: known limitations of the current ansatz and kernels and of the soil layers; the warnings: warnings reference.

    Audit trail¶

    Concrete shells against mom (since 2026.11; audits W2B-07, W2R2-09)

    Since 2026.11 concrete-encased wires carry the per-segment shell term of mom (ADR-0012). On two-layer soil the two engines agree with and without shells:

    • \(10^{-9}\) … \(1.5\cdot10^{-8}\) for a 10 m grid in four soils (audit W2B-07);
    • \(9\cdot10^{-8}\) with the layer interface 0.2 m below the wires, under PointKernelInterfaceWarning, and 9 % for a truncated series under SeriesTruncationWarning (audit W2R2-09);
    • the shell increments agree to \(1.4\cdot10^{-10}\), the agreement of the two kernels.
    Convergence reporting and tabulation (findings 10.47, 10.61)

    SommerfeldConvergenceWarning and the converged metadata were added by finding 10.47, so a quadrature that does not converge is never silent. The depth-group tabulation with its midpoint check and fallback is finding 10.61.
