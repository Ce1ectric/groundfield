    Performance and scaling¶

    This page tells you what a groundfield solve costs in time and memory, which part of the computation the cost comes from, and which settings to change first. Read it before a large study — a whole TN network, a sweep of hundreds of cells, a three-dimensional fem solve — or when a solve is slower than expected. Recipes for running large studies (parallel sweeps, Monte Carlo, speeding up large networks) are in the how-to guides.

    At a glance: measured costs

    Task Size Time, memory
    galvanic TN network, 30 houses, homogeneous, point kernel \(N\) = 3687 0.26 s, 115 MiB
    the same, 80 houses \(N\) = 9712 1.98 s, 773 MiB
    the line kernel (default) on such networks 1.1 … 1.9 times the point kernel
    30 houses, two-layer soil, 11 frequencies, point kernel \(N \approx\) 3 700 2.6 … 9.8 s
    inductive TN network, 30 houses, Sommerfeld earth return, 4 frequencies 338 inductive branches 12.2 s
    fem, axisymmetric model (ring, rod, ring with rod) 7 341 … 223 112 nodes 0.20 … 2.0 s
    fem, 3-D model (AP1 station) 1.2 … 1.4 million nodes 75 … 140 s, 4.3 GB

    Measured as described below; the fem numbers are those of the fem page.

    Where the cost is¶

    A solve of the image family has three parts:

    1. Reaction matrix. For \(N\) segments the kernel is evaluated for every pair: \(O(N^2)\) time and, if the matrix is kept, \(8N^2\) bytes. In two-layer soil every pair carries the Tagg / Sunde series, so the cost grows with the number of image terms, which depends only on the contrast \(\lvert K\rvert\) (20 terms at \(\lvert K\rvert = 0.5\), 277 at \(\lvert K\rvert = 0.94\) for the default tolerance; table on the image_2layer page). An electrode through the layer interface switches every pair to the spectral kernel of ADR-0007, which is slower and needs more memory per depth group.
    2. Network solve. The unknowns are one current per owner (each straight wire, and its part in each soil layer for a wire through the interface), one potential per node and one current per finite branch — a small dense system. A purely galvanic world is solved once and reused for every frequency; with inductive conductors one complex solve per frequency follows, which is cheap against the assembly.
    3. Earth-return assembly of inductive conductors: one integral per pair of conductor sub-segments, the dominant cost of inductive worlds with the default sommerfeld model.

    The mom family solves for one current per segment: its dense LU is \(O(N^3)\) and dominates for large \(N\). mom_sommerfeld adds one quadrature per distinct pair argument.

    fem meshes the soil instead. Its cost is set by the node count of the finer of its two mesh levels, not by \(N\): about 4 GB per million nodes for the axisymmetric model and 2.9 GB per million nodes for the 3-D model, whose conjugate gradients run once per port and level. Both refuse a mesh above FemOptions.max_nodes / max_nodes_3d (2 million nodes) before it is built (ResourceLimitError); the fem page has the measurements.

    Memory model¶

    The kernels never build (N, N, 3) difference tensors. The reaction matrix is materialised once per solve while it fits a budget of 1 GiB — \(8N^2\) bytes, i.e. up to \(N\) = 11 585 segments in homogeneous soil, \(16N^2\) and \(N\) = 8 192 for the two-layer series — and applied with the same BLAS calls as before, which keeps results bit-identical. Beyond the budget the kernels work in row blocks of 4 MiB with \(O(N\cdot\text{block})\) memory; results then differ at round-off (about \(10^{-15}\)). Field evaluation (FieldResult.potential) runs in pieces of at most 16 MiB, so a 200 × 200 grid of field points costs memory proportional to the grid, not to grid times sources.

    The resource limit of the engine, Engine.max_segments (default diagnostics.HARD_LIMIT, 20 000 segments or nodal unknowns), stops a world before anything of size \(N\) is allocated (ResourceLimitError); raise it per engine to solve a larger world on purpose. SOFT_LIMIT (5 000) marks the size where solves take minutes rather than seconds on a typical laptop; every Engine.solve at or above it emits a UserWarning that says so (solve_mutual_matrix and solve_mutual_field check the limit but emit no notice; fem emits it from the count of its display segments, which is not its problem size — that is its mesh). The size of that notice belongs to the engine as well, Engine.warn_segments (default SOFT_LIMIT, read at solve time), so a workstation can move it up together with max_segments.

    Measurements and audit trail

    Up to 0.15.0 the difference tensors needed 96 bytes per segment pair, 1.25 GB for a 30-house network and a MemoryError at 80 houses (finding 10.56). The resource limit is finding 10.06, the notice's entry points are audit D2A-09, and Engine.warn_segments is user decision Q31.

    What to change first¶

    In order of effect on a typical study:

    1. Earth-return model of inductive conductors. The Sommerfeld assembly dominates inductive studies; keep the default Engine.sommerfeld_assembly="tabulated", the faster and, by the measurements below, more accurate choice ("exact" integrates every segment pair and costs 17–25 times as much on the TN networks below). Leave conductors without an inductance_model where their inductance is not the question — then no assembly runs at all.
    2. Segment length. Cost and memory grow with \(N^2\). With the line kernel straight wires are exact at any segment length, so ds only needs to resolve the geometry (corners, rings, the distance between neighbouring wires and to a layer interface); a convergence study (convergence_study) shows when it does.
    3. Parallel cells of a sweep or Monte Carlo study (sweep(n_jobs=...)).
    4. numba (extra fast) for the line kernel's far field and the two-layer series; for catalogue sweeps in high-contrast two-layer soil Engine.image_far_field = "point" (point-form far images, 3e-6 … 4.3e-4 on networks).
    5. Contrast and electrodes through the interface set the cost of two-layer worlds; the series length follows from \(\lvert K\rvert\) alone.
    6. fem only where it is needed as a reference: the axisymmetric model takes seconds, the 3-D model minutes and gigabytes per solve.

    Measured in this release¶

    How the numbers were measured: synthetic AP1-like networks of the review's performance study (R8 workloads) — a substation with a 4 × 4 m ring and three rods, 10 to 80 houses with their foundations on both sides of four streets, PEN cables with service drops, 100 Ω·m or 500/100 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 5 m, ds = 0.5 m — built with the layout builder. The release work-streams ran v0.15.0 and the release candidate back to back in separate processes, one BLAS thread, on a shared machine with two cores; times are wall-clock (best of two for the short ones), memory is the tracemalloc peak, which is deterministic. Wall-clock ratios vary with the machine load; memory ratios do not. The scripts belong to the release evidence; the memory gates are part of the test suite (tests/test_kernel_performance.py), the timing gates run with --run-benchmark.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The gains come from component-wise row-block kernels instead of tensors, one reaction-matrix assembly per solve instead of two, the two-layer series summed into one matrix before the product (optionally compiled with numba, bit-identical) and the direct assembly of \(Z_G\) instead of applying the kernel to an identity matrix (\(O(N^3)\) per image term; findings 10.56–10.59, 10.62). The W2 peak was the grouped spectral correction of the cross-layer path; since wave 3 of the 2026.11 work a large depth group is planned once from the range of its distances and evaluated in row chunks (bit-identical), and beyond the budget the path works in row blocks like the series path (see Electrodes through the layer interface below).

    The default line kernel¶

    The exact line kernel (kernels, ADR-0015) adds the exact near-pair integrals and the quadrupole far field over all pairs. Its cost relative to the point kernel, measured on the release state:

    Workload \(N\) point: time / memory line: time / memory ratio (time) \(Z_\text{in}\), line vs point
    16 house grids, homogeneous 1920 0.043 s / 38.8 MiB 0.165 s / 47.7 MiB 3.8 (5.8 without numba) —
    16 house grids, 500 / 100 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m 1920 0.332 s / 64.4 MiB 0.519 s / 69.7 MiB 1.6 (1.4 without numba) —
    W1: 30 houses, homogeneous 3668 0.83 s / 113.4 MiB 1.55 s / 155.7 MiB 1.9 +0.35 %
    W1-80: 80 houses, homogeneous 9668 6.19 s / 758.3 MiB 6.94 s / 856.2 MiB 1.1 +0.27 %

    Install the extra fast (numba) for the compiled far field; kernel="point" reproduces the old numbers and cost.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The 16-house rows are the release state (best of three rounds of fresh processes, interleaved, with other agents on the machine, load 6 … 13); the homogeneous one is the case documented with Engine.kernel. The W1 rows were measured under a machine load of about 5.6 on two cores (numba installed), so their absolute times are about three times those of the galvanic table above; the ratio and the memory are the meaningful columns. The networks differ slightly from the v0.15.0 table (\(N\) = 3668 instead of 3687) because the layout builder now joins PEN cables at junction nodes instead of 5 cm rods.

    Far images in two-layer soil¶

    In two-layer soil every Tagg/Sunde image within reach carries the far-field term of the line kernel. Its cost follows the image density: high contrasts and thin top layers put hundreds of images within reach, and with long pieces below a thin top layer the Galerkin integrals of their near image pairs add to it (each long piece is within 7.5 segment lengths of every short segment's image; the near-pair criterion of the kernel). Engine.image_far_field = "point" keeps far images as point sources and leaves out their far-field term; the near image pairs stay Galerkin integrals on both paths, so on the cross-layer path it takes 0.49 … 0.94 of the default's time. Headline numbers (line kernel, ds = 0.5 m, 50 Hz, numba installed; † the cross-layer path):

    world \(N\) default "point"
    16 house grids, 500 / 100 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m 1920 0.54 s 0.48 s
    AP1 station, 40 % penetration † 2711 1.35 s 0.96 s
    TN network, 40 houses, 1000 / 30 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 1.5 m † 4877 4.03 s 1.99 s
    the same without numba † 4877 9.46 s 2.33 s

    "point" moves networks by 3e-6 … 4.3e-4 against the default: a choice for catalogue sweeps, not for fields near the interface.

    Electrodes through the layer interface¶

    The ADR-0007 path of image_2layer (wires at or below \(h_1\)) costs one spectral evaluation per pair of segment depths; large depth groups are planned once and evaluated in row chunks. Its peak memory is that of the series path: 16 house grids entirely below \(h_1\) = 0.5 m need 21.9 bytes per segment pair (the series path 18.3), and beyond the matrix budget the path works in row blocks with memory linear in \(N\) (36 house grids, \(N\) = 4320: 34.8 MiB in block mode). On the AP1 station of the roadmap with 40 % penetration (\(N\) = 2711) it takes 1.35 s with the line kernel (2.17 s without numba) and 0.96 s with image_far_field="point"; the same world with 4 m rods that stay in the top layer solves on the series path in 1.27 s.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The AP1 station of the roadmap — a 4 × 4 m ring at 0.6 m and three 6 m rods from 0.6 m through \(h_1\) = 5 m (500 / 100 Ω·m), 60 houses with foundations (10 × 10 m, 2 × 2 meshes at 0.8 m) at 5 … 40 % penetration, PEN cables, ds = 0.5 m, 50 Hz — before and after wave 3 of the 2026.11 work (best of three, numba installed, a shared machine; memory: tracemalloc peak):

    penetration \(N\) line kernel before → after point kernel before → after peak before → after the same world with 4 m rods (series path)
    5 % 311 0.82 → 0.43 s — 19.4 → 20.8 MiB 0.06 s, 8.5 MiB
    10 % 671 0.84 → 0.50 s 0.85 → 0.42 s 27.8 → 28.5 MiB 0.11 s, 18.7 MiB
    20 % 1031 0.89 → 0.62 s — 42.5 → 39.6 MiB 0.22 s, 25.0 MiB
    40 % 2711 1.28 → 1.65 s 1.14 → 0.70 s 278.3 → 136.6 MiB 1.27 s, 127.1 MiB

    The spectral amplitudes are eliminated in closed form (one LAPACK solve per quadrature node before, half the time of the path): the point kernel, which sees only that change, is 1.6 … 2.0 times faster. The line kernel carries the layered correction in line form now (a rod through the interface no longer depends on the segment length); since audit W3A its far-field terms cost about what the closed-form amplitudes saved (1.35 s at 40 % penetration, 1.29 s before wave 3; 2.17 s without numba). Before wave 3, 16 house grids entirely below \(h_1\) = 0.5 m needed 41.2 bytes per segment pair, and the 36 house grids 1008 MiB without row blocks (finding 10.56).

    Inductive conductors¶

    Workload v0.15.0 2026.11, sommerfeld_assembly="exact" 2026.11, "tabulated" (default)
    W3: 2 houses, Neumann PEN, Sommerfeld earth return, 11 f 35.3 s 1.9–2.1 s 1.2–1.5 s
    W3: 30 houses, same ≈ 3600 s (estimate) 242 s 7.8–9.1 s
    W3c: 30 houses, carson_series 16.4 s 3.3 s —
    TN generator, 8 houses, Neumann PEN (78 inductive branches), 4 f — 16.6 s 1.0 s
    TN generator, 30 houses, same (338 inductive branches) — 306 s 12.2 s

    The tabulated assembly shares one table of the spectral integral per pair of depth groups and frequency, and agrees with a converged reference better than the per-pair rule (substation impedance \(5\cdot 10^{-10}\) against \(1.1\cdot 10^{-8}\) on W3; input impedance of the 8-house TN network \(2\cdot 10^{-10}\) against \(1.2\cdot 10^{-8}\)). carson_series is an approximation for long parallel lines, not a replacement of sommerfeld (earth return), and no faster than necessary to be a check.

    Measurements and audit trail

    CPU times of the W3 rows measured by the coupling work-stream, of the TN rows by the stream that made the tabulated assembly the default (user decision Q16; TnNetworkGenerator, homogeneous 100 Ω·m, ds = 1 m, 50/250/550/1000 Hz). The per-pair path gains from one multi-frequency assembly per solve (geometry, λ grid and Bessel tables once per pair; bit-identical) and the resolved λ grid (finding 10.60). carson_series moved by its own fix (F32).

    Other measurements¶

    • mom_sommerfeld, three layers. The depth-group tabulation of the reflected kernel (default) took a 40 m grid in 1000/30/200 Ω·m soil at ds = 1.25 m from 66.6 s to 0.30 s and a village of eleven rings (\(N\) = 273) from 34.1 s to 1.25 s, within \(10^{-6}\) of the quadrature (benchmark B12).
    • sweep(n_jobs=...). Parameter cells run in a process pool with one BLAS thread per worker; the result is bit-identical to n_jobs=1 when the parent also runs a single BLAS thread. The review measured 1.98 times the throughput on two workers. The LU factorisation is 0.2–1.5 % of a solve, so BLAS threads are not the lever; parallel cells are.
    • Import. import groundfield loads its heavy dependencies lazily: 223 ms instead of 798 ms, 346 instead of 1169 modules.
    • fem. The axisymmetric model takes 0.20 s, 0.58 s and 2.0 s for a ring, a 3 m rod and a ring with a central rod in two layers (7 341 / 28 934, 18 868 / 74 871 and 56 079 / 223 112 nodes at levels 0 / 1). The 3-D model takes 26 s and 1.8 GB for a ring of 12.6 m (600 000 nodes at the finer level) and 75 … 140 s and 4.3 GB for the AP1 station (28 m of wire, 1.2 … 1.4 million nodes); FemOptions.max_nodes_3d (2 million nodes) refuses meshes of more than about 6 GB before they are built. Details and conditions on the fem page.
    Measurements and audit trail

    The tabulation of mom_sommerfeld is finding 10.61, the parallel sweep finding 10.64, the lazy import finding 10.72.
