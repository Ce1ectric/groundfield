    Assess touch voltages¶

    This guide computes the touch voltages of an installation at its accessible metal parts and compares them with the permissible values of EN 50522. Read it when you verify an earthing system for an earth fault: a station, a transferred potential at a house, an open-air grid.

    EN 50522 assesses the touch voltage \(U_T\) between an accessible conductive part and the feet of a person who touches it, standing 1 m away from the part, against the permissible touch voltage \(U_{Tp}(t_f)\) of the fault duration \(t_f\). groundfield returns the prospective (open-circuit) value

    \[ U_T = U_\text{part} - \varphi(\text{feet}), \]

    the potential of the touched part less the soil potential at the feet, as a phasor in V for the source current of the solve.

    Prerequisites: the basic workflow of tutorial T01. The steps define the touch points, evaluate a transferred potential, sample the meshes of an open-air grid and compare the results with the permissible values.

    1. Define the touch points¶

    The touched parts are the metal parts above ground: the housing or the door of a station, a mast, a fence post, a cable cabinet, a house connection box. gf.touch_voltage_at_part takes one part — its horizontal position and the electrode or junction it is bonded to — and puts the feet on the circle of radius distance (1 m) around it. exclude removes the foot positions inside outlines where nobody can stand (the housing itself); directions keeps only the given sides, for example the outside of a fence.

    A compact substation: a housing of 3 m × 2.5 m inside a ring earth of 2.5 m radius, 100 A of earth-fault current into the station earth at 50 Hz, 100 Ω·m.

    import math

import groundfield as gf

world = gf.create_world(soil=gf.HomogeneousSoil(rho=100.0))
gf.create_electrode(world, "ring", name="station", center=(0.0, 0.0, 0.6), radius=2.5)
gf.create_source(world, attached_to="station", magnitude=100.0)
result = gf.create_engine(frequencies=[50.0]).solve(world)

housing = [(-1.5, -1.25), (1.5, -1.25), (1.5, 1.25), (-1.5, 1.25)]  # outline in m
angles, u_door, feet = gf.touch_voltage_at_part(
    result, world, position=(0.0, -1.25), bonded_to="station",  # door in the south wall
    exclude=[housing], return_points=True,
)
_, u_fence = gf.touch_voltage_at_part(
    result, world, position=(0.0, -4.0), bonded_to="station",  # fence post, bonded
    directions=[(0.0, -1.0)],  # people stand outside the fence
)
u_e = abs(result.electrode_potentials["station"][0])
print(f"earth potential rise {u_e:.0f} V")
print(f"door: {abs(u_door).max():.0f} V (largest of {len(u_door)} foot positions)")
print(f"fence post: {abs(u_fence[0]):.0f} V")

    Of the 24 positions around the door, the 11 inside the housing are dropped; the two on the wall line are kept. The door is inside the ring, where the surface potential is high, so its touch voltage (447 V) is lower than 1 m outside the ring; the bonded fence post outside the ring has 784 V.

    2. Evaluate a transferred potential over the PEN¶

    A part bonded to a node far from the faulted earth carries that node's potential into an area of low potential rise (EN 50522, 3.6). A house 40 m away without an earth electrode of its own is connected to the station by the PEN conductor of the low-voltage cable, which ends on a junction at the service connection box:

    gf.create_junction(world, name="service_box", position=(40.0, 0.0, 0.7))
gf.create_conductor(
    world, name="pen", start="station", end="service_box",
    resistivity=2.83e-8, cross_section=150e-6,  # aluminium, 150 mm²
)
result = gf.create_engine(frequencies=[50.0]).solve(world)

house = [(40.0, -4.0), (50.0, -4.0), (50.0, 4.0), (40.0, 4.0)]
_, u_box = gf.touch_voltage_at_part(
    result, world, position=(40.0, 0.0), bonded_to="service_box", exclude=[house]
)
print(f"service connection box: {abs(u_box).max():.0f} V of {u_e:.0f} V")

    No current flows on the PEN, so the box carries the station potential, while the soil at the house is almost at remote earth: 96 % of the earth potential rise is a touch voltage there.

    3. Sample the meshes of an open-air grid¶

    touch_voltage_envelope puts the feet 1 m from the nearest wire of an electrode, all around it — a proxy where the touched parts are not known. It walks 24 equally spaced directions from the centre and, by default (corners=True), nine more feet on the arc around every corner of the convex hull of its wires, where the touch voltage is largest: equally spaced directions alone miss the corners of a non-square grid (a 60 m × 40 m grid read 12 … 28 % low, case study E03). By default it samples nothing inside the footprint, because there stands the station, the house, the steel structure or the mast. Over an open-air grid, such as a substation yard, people walk over the grid and the surface potential sags inside the meshes: interior="meshes" adds the centre of every mesh (the mesh voltage of IEEE Std 80), of a ring, and of the cells of a foundation built from bonded wires; interior=True also the inside of a closed polyline. Use the modes only where the inside is accessible open ground.

    Near grids the image backend's uniform current is not field-grade (UniformCurrentFieldWarning), so the yard is solved with mom:

    yard = gf.create_world(soil=gf.HomogeneousSoil(rho=100.0))
gf.create_electrode(
    yard, "grid_mesh", name="grid", corner=(0.0, 0.0, 0.5), size=(20.0, 20.0), n_x=2, n_y=2
)
gf.create_source(yard, attached_to="grid", magnitude=100.0)
yard_result = gf.create_engine(backend="mom", segment_length=0.5, frequencies=[50.0]).solve(yard)

_, outside = gf.touch_voltage_envelope(yard_result, yard, electrode="grid")
# corners=False: the 24 equally spaced feet first, then the mesh centres
_, inside = gf.touch_voltage_envelope(
    yard_result, yard, electrode="grid", interior="meshes", corners=False
)
print(f"1 m outside: {abs(outside).max():.1f} V; mesh centres: {abs(inside[24:]).max():.1f} V")

    The value to assess is the largest of both, here 1 m outside a corner.

    The feet stand 1 m from the nearest wire of the whole foundation (anchor="footprint", the default). anchor="connection_point" puts them on a circle round the electrode's connection point instead. That point is the centre of a hand-built grid mesh, but a corner of a rectangular foundation of the generators — the start of its local −y side, the electrode named after the foundation — so the circle goes round that corner: 100 A into a generated 20 m × 5 m ring at 0.8 m in 100 Ω·m give 229.71 V there against 161.76 V round its centre (default engine, 0.5 m segments). Keep the default for generated foundations.

    Background and audit trail

    anchor="connection_point" keeps the rule of 0.15.0. The connection point of a generated foundation is a corner since 2026.11: for meshes since audit A2G-04, for rectangles without cross-braces since user decision Q38. With 0.15.0, round the centre of one grid mesh, the same case gave 156.07 V (audits D3A-03, R4B-09).

    4. Compare with the permissible touch voltage¶

    permissible_touch_voltage_en50522(t_f) follows Figure 8 / Table B.4 of EN 50522:2022 up to 10 s and returns 80 V beyond, the value the standard allows for current-flow durations considerably longer than 10 s. Pass math.inf for an earth fault that is not cleared, as in a resonant-earthed network. With footwear or a resistive surface layer, permissible_prospective_touch_voltage_en50522 adds the body current times the additional resistances (Annex B.4):

    u_tp_cleared = gf.permissible_touch_voltage_en50522(0.5)  # cleared after 0.5 s
u_tp_uncleared = gf.permissible_touch_voltage_en50522(math.inf)
u_vtp = gf.permissible_prospective_touch_voltage_en50522(math.inf, R_F1=1000.0, rho_s=100.0)
for part, u in (("door", abs(u_door).max()), ("service connection box", abs(u_box).max())):
    print(f"{part}: {u:.0f} V against U_Tp = {u_tp_cleared:.0f} V (0.5 s), "
          f"{u_tp_uncleared:.0f} V (not cleared), U_vTp = {u_vtp:.1f} V (1000 Ω shoes, not cleared)")

    What you get: each part's largest touch voltage against \(U_{Tp}\) for a cleared and an uncleared fault and against \(U_{vTp}\) with footwear. Both parts exceed the limits by far at 100 A: the earthing system of this example needs more than a ring (EN 50522, 5.4.2 and Annex E, describe the verification steps and the measures).
