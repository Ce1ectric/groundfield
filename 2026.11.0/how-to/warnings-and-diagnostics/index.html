    Read and act on warnings¶

    This guide checks a world for problems before a long run with gf.diagnose, explains the warnings you will meet most often and what to do about each, and shows how to handle warnings in scripts and tests. Read it when a solve warns, or before you start a large study.

    groundfield warns when a result is computed but may not mean what you think: a discretisation that is too coarse somewhere, a model applied outside its validity, a setting that has no effect, a deprecated name. Each warning names its cause and a remedy. Every warning and error class, with its import path, is described in the warnings catalogue.

    1. Check a world before you solve¶

    gf.diagnose(world, engine) collects the pre-solve checks in one report: resolution (features shorter than the segment length), segment budget, thin wires, wires of different electrodes that are too close or interpenetrate, inductive conductors drawn on top of each other, and settings that have no effect. Pass result= as well to add post-solve checks such as a truncated image series.

    import warnings

import groundfield as gf

world = gf.create_world(soil=gf.HomogeneousSoil(rho=100.0))
gf.create_electrode(world, "rod", name="rod_a", position=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), length=3.0, wire_radius=0.008)
gf.create_electrode(world, "rod", name="rod_b", position=(0.018, 0.0, 0.0), length=3.0, wire_radius=0.008)
gf.create_conductor(world, name="bond", start="rod_a", end="rod_b")  # two rods 2 mm apart
gf.create_electrode(world, "rod", name="short_rod", position=(10.0, 0.0, 0.5), length=0.4)
gf.create_conductor(world, name="link", start="rod_a", end="short_rod")
gf.create_source(world, attached_to="rod_a", magnitude=1.0)

report = gf.diagnose(world, gf.create_engine(segment_length=1.0, kernel="point"))
print(report)
for finding in report:
    print(finding.category, finding.severity, finding.subjects)

    The report prints one line per cause. Acting on it here means: refine the segment length (or accept the coarse short rod), and either merge the two rods into one electrode or use the default line-source kernel, for which close wires are only information:

    report_line = gf.diagnose(world, gf.create_engine(segment_length=0.2))
print(report_line or "no findings")

    What you get: with the point kernel a resolution finding for the short rod and a proximity warning for the two rods 2 mm apart; with the default line kernel and 0.2 m segments no warning is left (the hidden checks assert both). report.warn() emits the findings as one DiagnosticsWarning, for pipelines that collect warnings.

    2. Check insulated cables for overlaps¶

    Insulated cables with an inductance model are checked as well: two conductors drawn beside each other closer than touching over a stretch (category overlap, a warning) are evaluated by the inductance matrix as touching wires, so their coupling follows their radii instead of the drawn spacing. A typical cause is a second cable or a service drop drawn on the axis of another:

    cables = gf.create_world(soil=gf.HomogeneousSoil(rho=100.0))
gf.create_electrode(cables, "rod", name="a", position=(0.0, 0.0, 0.5), length=1.5)
gf.create_electrode(cables, "rod", name="b", position=(50.0, 0.0, 0.5), length=1.5)
for name, radius in (("cable_1", 0.006), ("cable_2", 0.004)):
    gf.create_conductor(
        cables, name=name, start="a", end="b", wire_radius=radius,
        coupling_to_soil="isolated", inductance_model="neumann",
    )
overlap = [f for f in gf.diagnose(cables) if f.category == "overlap"]
print(overlap[0].message)   # 'cable_1'–'cable_2' beside each other over 50 m, ...

    Draw the cables at their real spacing (a few centimetres in one trench), or model a concentric cable with conductor_type="cable_shield" around its core, which is not an overlap.

    What is reported, and what is not:

    • The stretch must exceed ten touching distances and 5 % of the shorter conductor, so a branch leaving its main at more than about half a degree, a crossing and a T-contact are not reported.
    • Cables drawn exactly at the touching distance are not reported either: within 1e-9 of it, or within 16 units in the last place of the coordinates where that is more (round-off, also at projected coordinates).
    • The generated networks report none, also with placement jitter (star and radial networks of 5 … 200 houses, jitter_m 0 … 0.1 m). The service drops that leave one tap in one direction — the drops of houses behind each other in the radial networks and in star networks such as the 10-house star (five drops from the substation) — lie side by side at the touching distance, so that only neighbours touch. Drops that are not exactly parallel (jitter) are laid along one common normal, and a drop that would lie closer than touching to a drop of a row joins the row.

    With a bare PEN (coupling_to_soil="galvanic") the drops of a row are close wires (proximity "close", 1 … 4 cm apart), and a drop of a row starts up to (n − 1)/2 touching distances off its tap (2 cm in a row of five), next to the wire it leaves there. Without an engine diagnose() warns about those starts as "interpenetrating" against the substation ring (the 10-house radial network: 9 pairs at axis distances of 0.5 … 5.0 mm) and about the neighbours of a row as "close"; with the default engine (the image family's line kernel, which integrates near pairs) all of them are information.

    Background and audit trail
    • The tolerance at the touching distance (1e-9, or 16 units in the last place) dates from audits W3B-03 and W3R2B-02.
    • The drops of a row lie side by side at the touching distance since decision D9. Up to D9 they lay on one axis and every pair was reported (radial networks of 5 / 10 / 80 / 200 houses: 10 / 20 / 31 / 247 pairs, the 10-house star 10, the 80-house star with commercial sites 2; audit W3B-05). Up to audit W3R2B-01 a jitter of 1 nm put them back on one axis (the 10-house radial network: 19 pairs); since then jittered drops are laid along one common normal and a drop too close to a row joins the row.
    • With the drops on one axis (before D9) the bare-PEN case gave 5 "interpenetrating" pairs at 5.0 mm in the 10-house radial network. That the default engine reports all of these pairs as information is the finding of audit W3R2B-07.

    3. Know the warnings you will meet most often¶

    Warning Meaning What to do
    UserWarning "resolution: … shorter than segment_length" an electrode feature is shorter than the segment length refine segment_length, or accept it for small parts
    UserWarning "image_2layer: deepest segment ends … above the layer interface" point kernel: the deepest segment ends less than two segment lengths above the interface refine near the interface, or use the line kernel
    ShallowSegmentWarning segments longer than four times their depth (point kernel, mom family) the default line kernel, or shorter segments
    SeriesTruncationWarning the two-layer image series stopped at image_max_terms raise Engine(image_max_terms=...)
    UniformCurrentFieldWarning touch or step voltages of the image family near grids, stars, strips, bonded clusters backend="mom" for field quantities
    ExperimentalBackendWarning the first solve with the experimental fem backend use fem as a reference next to a production engine
    InterfaceContactWarning a rod end or a horizontal wire very close to a layer interface keep wires a few radii from the interface
    BackendCapabilityWarning the backend simplifies the world (for example lumped conductors) choose a backend that supports the feature
    InertConfigurationWarning a setting that no backend reads was changed remove it
    RhoFStandardQualityWarning, VectorFitQualityWarning a rho-f fit misses its samples by more than the tolerance narrow the range, add poles, or fit per resistivity
    NonPassiveFitWarning an exported formula has a negative resistance in the band refit; require_passive=True refuses such formulas
    CrossSectionUnitWarning a cross section above 0.01 m², probably mm² passed as m² pass m² (50e-6 for 50 mm²)
    DeprecationWarning a name or parameter is deprecated switch to the replacement it names

    Details of some rows:

    • The image-series warnings. SeriesTruncationWarning appears for strong contrasts, \(\lvert K\rvert \to 1\) with the reflection coefficient \(K = (\rho_2 - \rho_1)/(\rho_2 + \rho_1)\), where the series needs more terms than image_max_terms to reach its tolerance. The "deepest segment ends" warning comes from image_2layer with kernel="point".
    • UniformCurrentFieldWarning: the image family carries one uniform current per straight wire, which is right for impedances but not for the local field next to grids, meshes, stars, strips or bonded clusters. Tutorial T08 shows the assessment with mom.
    • ExperimentalBackendWarning is emitted once per process; filter the category when you use fem on purpose. FemReductionWarning, the warning of the former equivalent-hemisphere reduction of fem, is no longer emitted since 2026.11.
    • InterfaceContactWarning: a rod end within one wire radius of a layer interface, or a horizontal wire within a few wire radii of a contrasting one — 3.9 radii over a layer 33 times more conductive, 2.2 radii under one 33 times more resistive, one radius from a contrast of 1.3 for a wire the interface cuts. The thin-wire model deviates from the solid wire by 1 % or more there. The image family, mom, mom_sommerfeld and the 3-D fem warn, the axisymmetric fem does not: check a ring with fem's axisymmetric model (the reach of the interface is tabulated in the warnings reference).
    • InertConfigurationWarning covers boundary conditions and the relative permittivity; neither has an effect below 1 kHz.
    • The rho-f warnings: tutorial T04 fits and checks rho-f models.
    • DeprecationWarning: deprecated names are removed in 2027.5 at the earliest.

    Errors stop the solve before anything expensive happens: BackendCapabilityError (the backend cannot represent the world; the message names backends that can), ResourceLimitError (more segments or nodal unknowns than the engine's limit, Engine(max_segments=...), by default groundfield.diagnostics.HARD_LIMIT; the notice that a solve may take minutes moves with Engine(warn_segments=...)), and ValueError / KeyError for invalid input with the closest valid names.

    4. Handle warnings in scripts and tests¶

    Warnings point at the line of your code that caused them. Do not silence them globally; catch the ones you have understood, where they occur:

    engine = gf.create_engine(segment_length=1.0)
with warnings.catch_warnings(record=True) as caught:
    warnings.simplefilter("always")
    result = engine.solve(world)
for w in caught:
    print(f"{w.category.__name__}: {str(w.message)[:90]} ...")

    with warnings.catch_warnings():
    warnings.simplefilter("error", DeprecationWarning)  # make deprecations fail here
    try:
        gf.HomogeneousSoil(resistivity=100.0)  # the pre-2026.11 name
    except DeprecationWarning as exc:
        print("DeprecationWarning:", exc)

    In a test suite, turn groundfield's deprecations into failures so that an upgrade cannot hide them, e.g. with pytest in pyproject.toml:

    [tool.pytest.ini_options]
filterwarnings = ["error::DeprecationWarning"]
