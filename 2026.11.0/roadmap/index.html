    Roadmap¶

    This page collects work that is planned or possible. The first section lists what is scheduled for the release after 2026.11.0; everything below it is not scheduled. Up to 0.15.0 the roadmap was the last section of the changelog; it moved here in 2026.11.0, when the release notes were condensed (see the changelog). Ideas that a review pass recorded as roadmap candidates stay on the page of that pass in the audit history.

    Scheduled for the next release¶

    Nothing is scheduled yet. The items decided on 2026-11-08 for the release after 2026.11.0 (the questions and answers are listed on the pass-10 page) were moved into 2026.11.0 itself on 2026-11-14 and are done.

    Left open by 2026.11.0¶

    The work of release 2026.11.0 left these items open on purpose. Where an item limits a result, the reference documentation states the limit.

    • A current that varies along each wire in the image family. Since user decision Q30 every straight wire of an electrode carries one uniform current per unit length. The arms of a star carry equal currents by symmetry, so the split left a star alone as it was: +1.8 % (3 arms), +3.4 % (4), +10.4 % (8 arms of 10 m) above one current per segment in 100 Ω·m, +12.3 % in 1000 over 100 Ω·m — the current crowds to the tips. Grids are within +0.36 … +0.70 %. A current varying along each wire (linear or per sub-piece, as mom solves it) would close the gap; until then backend="mom" gives the per-segment solution.
    • Group rule for wide groups. A group of about 180° is served by one main to its far-end building; a street-row rule or a minimum of two groups for buildings around the substation would serve it better (a decision).
    • One cabinet around the substation. Ten houses in a row through the substation get their only cabinet at one end; the other half is fed from the substation.
    • A cabinet that cannot keep its clearance raises; moving it on along the ray past the blocking site would always place it (dense random layouts only so far).
    • A continuous cabinet rule (user decision D7: not in 2026.11). The 20 m threshold between the centroid ray and the far-end ray is a step: a group that crosses it moves its cabinet by up to the width of the group (141.8 m in the 50-house grid for a 0.2 m change of the house spacing, X at 1 kHz about −20 %). A rule that moves the cabinet continuously with the group's geometry would remove the step; the plan's cabinet_rule / centroid_ray_gap_m show meanwhile where a study crosses it.
    • Line form of the field of lower-layer sources. FieldResult.potential and solve_mutual_field evaluate sources in the lower layer with the spectral point kernel; the reaction matrix uses the line form since 2026.11 (wave 3).
    • A faster far-image kernel. The compiled far-image term is not vectorised by numba on arm64 (nor is the series kernel); a hand-vectorised or fused point + quadrupole kernel would cut the +60 … +90 % of two-layer line solves.
    • Cost of carson_series on large networks. The Sommerfeld fallback of the oblique pairs takes 72 % of a 100-house sweep and the Python loop of the Carson pair plan 15 % of a 40-house sweep. Compiled Carson integrals were measured and not built (5 … 13 % of an inductive sweep after memoisation, below the 20 % threshold; 10.63).
    • Spacing of service drops in a trench. The drops of one tap and direction lie side by side at the touching distance (user decision D9); a spacing parameter of the generator would cover wider trenches (5 cm moves the 5-house radial network's X at 1 kHz by −12.5 / −14.6 %, carson_series / sommerfeld).
    • Galerkin near image pairs of long pieces. A long piece below a thin top layer is within 7.5 segment lengths of the image of every short segment (1.15 M pairs, 5 times the solve before wave 3); integrating them with the shorter segment as observer and mirroring the result (the corrections are symmetric) would remove it. Since audit W3R2A-01 the point form keeps them too: 16 house grids with eight 9.4 m pieces below a 0.5 m top layer solve in 1.44 s instead of 0.39 s with image_far_field="point", 4.5 times the solve before wave 3.
    • Far term per image family (audit W3A-01, proposal 2). Thin top layers and high contrasts put hundreds of images within reach (3.6 … 4.5 times the solve before wave 3 with the default far-image form).
    • The soil-surface image at the transverse distance. Image pairs use the transverse distance since audit W3A-02; the soil-surface image keeps the reduced kernel so that homogeneous worlds stay as they are in 2026.11 (applied to it, homogeneous house grids would move by +3.9e-7 at 0.8 m, +8.8e-7 at 0.5 m and +1.7e-5 at 0.1 m).
    • A finer 3-D fem for groups. The 3-D model refuses the house mesh at its defaults (max_nodes_3d) and the solve adds about 2.9 GB per million nodes; a third level with the observed order of convergence (instead of the assumed second order) would tighten its 2e-3, and per-feature grading or a matrix-free multigrid would reach foundations and groups at the defaults.
    • Ties of the star topology under placement jitter. The cabinet placement and the contact rule of the star topology have ties that a jitter of 1e-7 … 1e-6 m flips (the 10-house star: −0.24 % and −11.8 % X at 1 kHz); making them robust, or documenting the realisations as discrete, would serve Monte Carlo studies.
    • The point form at the interface. A wire on the interface differs by up to 3e-4 between the series and the cross-layer path with image_far_field="point", because the far terms are split differently (the default: 1.3e-6); a consistent split was not attempted.
    • Reciprocity of cross-layer worlds. solve_mutual_matrix(symmetrize=False) shows an asymmetry of 2.3e-8 on a cross-layer world (a strip below h₁, a rod through it, a grid above), in both far-image forms.
    • A 3-D fem preconditioner for high layer contrast. The multigrid of the stiffness stalls above a contrast of about 300 (the coupling diagonal does not help, stream fw4fem3d); such worlds are refused.
    • A source radius per wire in the 3-D fem. One source radius serves the whole world, so the closest pair of wires that run beside each other refines the whole mesh (nodes grow as \(1/R\)): one PEN 5 cm beside a strip triples the station's nodes (audits W4R2B-03, W4R2A-01). A per-wire radius needs a per-wire disc quadrature, energy radius and mesh size. diagnose() does not report such wires yet; the solve refuses the closest ones before meshing.
    • The transfer estimate in the axisymmetric fem. fem_error_estimate_transfer exists in the 3-D model only; the same estimate would give both models one metadata schema.
    • The reach of the interface warning beyond rings. The reach of InterfaceContactWarning for horizontal wires is measured on rings with \(r_0/a \ge 18\) against the solid torus (audit W4R2A-04); straight wires against a solid reference and thicker wires on smaller rings are not covered. The conductive nodes come from the thin wire at one segment length; converged in it, the measured distances lie 0.004 … 0.17 a inside the table (the documented rule on converged values would add 0.1 a at contrasts 5.5 … 100).
    • Converged Q40 junction numbers. A third level of the prism faces (more than 3 million nodes) would settle the sign of the junction effect, today a few cm of wire at most (audit W4B-05).
    • A proximity correction for horizontal wires next to an interface in the image family: the exact 2-D equipotential-cylinder correction (tests/benchmarks/references.wire_proximity_correction) brings the thin ring to 5e-4 of the torus down to 1.1 radii (a decision; today the solve warns, audit W4A-07).

    Roadmap as carried in the changelog up to 0.15.0¶

    The list below is the Roadmap section of CHANGELOG.md as it stood before 2026.11.0, moved verbatim (reST roles rendered as code); its references to the [Unreleased] block are to that time.

    Backlog of feature ideas that are not yet scheduled. Updated as work package 1 progresses.

    Performance¶

    • Vectorise the ADR-0007 cross-layer Sommerfeld path. After caching the Gauss–Legendre nodes (see [Unreleased]), the remaining cost of the cross-layer fallback is the genuine \(O(N^2)\) per-segment-pair work in coupling/layered_green.py::two_layer_layered_correction_real_space / _spectral_amplitudes: many small np.linalg.solve calls (one spectral-amplitude system per pair) plus per-pair quadrature. Batching the per-pair spectral solves into a single vectorised np.linalg.solve over the pair axis, and evaluating the Bessel/exponential kernels on the shared quadrature grid in one array op, should give another ~2–5×. Motivation: AP1 catalogues two-layer soils with a thin upper layer (h_1 = 5 m) where a deep station rod can cross the interface — currently the dominant per-station cost even after the leggauss cache (~155 s vs ~3 s within-layer at 5 % penetration). Cost scales \(O(N^2)\) in segment count, so larger / higher-penetration geometries benefit most.

    Core functionality¶

    • Exact buried two-layer inductive kernel → n ≥ 3 layers. The 0.13.0 fix (coupling/sommerfeld_inductance.py::_two_layer_reflected_weight) solves the exact boundary-value problem for a conductor buried in the upper layer of a two-layer earth, validated against Tsiamitros (2005) Eq. (8) across 50 Hz – 1 MHz. For n ≥ 3 layers a buried source still falls back to the approximate single-air-image correction (the surface reflection cascade reflection_coefficient_layered is already exact for any n); a general n-layer buried spectral kernel would extend the exact inductive earth-return to arbitrary multilayer soils. The n ≥ 3 buried path is currently pinned only by the homogeneous and bounding checks — there is no exact multilayer reference for it yet. Galvanic multilayer solves are unaffected (they use layered_green).
    • FEM support for distributed conductors. The current FEM backend falls back to a lumped branch and logs a warning when it sees a distributed conductor — the equivalent-hemisphere reduction does not resolve the midpoint pseudo-electrodes. A full-3-D FEM (deferred below) would lift this restriction.
    • Full-3-D FEM backend (via scikit-fem or comparable) replacing the equivalent-hemisphere reduction in the current fem engine. Deferred — explicitly not on the AP1 roadmap. The target AP1 geometries (~20 houses with foundation electrodes plus a transformer station with ring + driven rods plus driven rods at cable cabinets) already push the integral solvers towards the ~1000-segment regime where the dense \(O(N^3)\) LU solve dominates; a 3-D volume FEM on the same configuration would be one to two orders of magnitude more expensive without adding physical insight. This task is therefore reserved for follow-up work where a multi-cluster volume computation is genuinely needed.
    • Scaling the integral solvers to ~10 000-segment networks: replace the dense LU solve in mom, bem and cim by a preconditioned iterative solver (GMRES with a block-diagonal or fast-multipole preconditioner). Unlocks the full AP1 parameter space (large TN-Ortsnetz with foundation electrodes, drives, and cable cabinets) without giving up the per-segment current resolution. Concrete prerequisites: ACA (adaptive cross approximation) for the dense reaction matrix to bring memory from \(O(N^2)\) down to \(O(N \log N)\) and a block-Jacobi preconditioner per cluster.

    Studies and tooling¶

    • ParameterSweep API — wrap a world_factory(**params) plus a parameter grid into a parallel sweep (joblib-driven, target 12 cores) that returns a pandas.DataFrame of cluster impedances, EPRs and per-electrode currents. Drives the AP1 statistical studies (soil layering, electrode count and position, Monte Carlo realisations).

    • TN-Ortsnetz topology generator — implemented 2026-05-18 in the [Unreleased] block above. The radial-trunk PEN topology (RadialTrunkTopology) and the integration with OsmBuildingPlacement are live; what remains as a v2 enhancement is the corner-routed service drop (tap → axis- aligned corner → building), which currently runs as a single straight line between anchors. For typical street-aligned plots the corner offset is small compared with the full feeder length and the residual error in the longitudinal branch impedance stays well below 1 % at 50 Hz.

    • penetration depth Calculate the depth of the earth current as it is used in Carson integrals. It should be possible to create the earth current depth of any soild multilayer problem to use an equivilent for the typical formulas for calculating the self and coupling impedances of a cable or overheadline with earth return part.

    Integration¶

    • Direct export of BusType and BranchType into groundinsight databases.
    • Import of measurement geometries from groundmeas (positions, lengths, auxiliary-electrode set-up).

    Documentation and typing¶

    • Reference-case library for AP1: clusters of single-family houses (5 / 10 / 30 / 80 / 200), small and medium commercial buildings, cable cabinets.
    • Notebook suite that covers the full parameter space described in the dissertation proposal.
