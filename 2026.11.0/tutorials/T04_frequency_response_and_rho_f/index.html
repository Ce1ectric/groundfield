    T04 — Frequency response and the rho-f model¶

    Between 0 Hz and 1 kHz the impedance of a networked grounding system changes with frequency, and it changes with the soil resistivity. A planner who works with a network model (for example groundinsight) does not want to rerun a field solution for every soil and every harmonic; he needs a compact formula \(Z(\rho, f)\) — the rho-f model. This tutorial computes the frequency response of a small LV network, shows how much the earth-return model matters, and reduces the results to a rho-f formula in two ways: the six-coefficient standard form over \(\rho\) and \(f\), and a vector fit over \(f\) at one resistivity. Every fit is checked on data it has not seen.

    You will learn

    • where the frequency dependence below 1 kHz comes from, and how to switch it on (inductance_model="neumann");
    • the three earth-return models and when to use which;
    • how to fit, evaluate and validate the standard rho-f form and a vector fit, and how to read their quality figures.

    Runtime: about 10–20 s.

    Where the frequency dependence comes from¶

    Below 1 kHz the soil itself stays quasi-static: the displacement current is negligible and an earth electrode is a resistance. What changes with frequency are the conductors that connect the electrodes. A PEN cable of length \(l\) has the series impedance

    \[ Z_\mathrm{PEN}(\omega) = R + j\omega L + \Delta Z_\mathrm{earth}(\omega), \]

    where \(L\) follows from the Neumann double integral over the cable segments, \(M_{ij} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int_i \int_j \frac{\mathrm d\mathbf l \cdot \mathrm d\mathbf l'}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\), and \(\Delta Z_\mathrm{earth}\) is the effect of the current that returns through the conducting earth. As the frequency rises, the cable reactance grows and the remote houses contribute less: the network impedance seen from the substation rises.

    groundfield offers three earth-return models (Engine.earth_inductive_model):

    Model Physics Use
    "sommerfeld" (default) Sommerfeld/Pollaczek integral of the conducting half-space or layered earth over the real segment geometry reference whenever \(Z(f)\) matters
    "carson_series" Carson's (1926) per-metre earth-return correction for long parallel wires on the anti-parallel image fast approximation for long parallel cables in homogeneous earth
    "perfect_mirror" the earth as a perfect magnetic mirror, frequency independent diagnostic baseline only

            world, name=f"bond_{k}", start="station", end=f"station_rod_{k}"

            "grid_mesh",

            inductance_model="neumann",  # self and mutual inductance of the cable

    At 0 Hz all three models give the same, purely resistive value. Above, they disagree markedly: the earth-return model is not a detail. perfect_mirror ignores the finite conductivity of the earth and is kept only as a fast diagnostic baseline; carson_series is derived for infinitely long parallel wires; sommerfeld integrates the field of the conducting earth over the actual segment geometry and is the default. We continue with sommerfeld.

    The standard rho-f form¶

    The research standard form describes the impedance with six real coefficients,

    \[ Z(\rho, f) = k_0 + k_1 \rho + (k_2 + j k_3)\, f + (k_4 + j k_5)\, f \rho . \]

    \(k_0 + k_1\rho\) is the resistive part at 0 Hz, \(k_3 f\) and \(k_5 f \rho\) the reactance, \(k_2 f\) and \(k_4 f\rho\) the frequency-dependent resistance. The fit minimises the error relative to \(|Z|\) (weights \(1/|Z|\)) over all samples and reports the worst pointwise relative error max_relative next to the rms value. rho_f_standard_from_results takes solved results and their resistivities; by default it fits the input impedance.

    Know the limits of the standard form¶

    The standard form is linear in \(\rho\) at every frequency. That holds for an earth electrode alone, whose resistance is proportional to \(\rho\), but not for a network: the cable resistance does not scale with the soil, so at low resistivities it takes a larger share of the impedance. Extending the fit down to \(\rho = 30\ \Omega\,\mathrm m\) therefore degrades it, and the fit says so with a RhoFStandardQualityWarning when the worst sample exceeds 5 %:

    Vector fitting at one resistivity¶

    Vector fitting (Gustavsen and Semlyen, 1999) approximates the frequency response by a rational function of \(s = j\omega\),

    \[ Z(s) = R_\infty + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{r_k}{s - p_k}, \]

    with poles \(p_k\) and residues \(r_k\) in conjugate pairs, so that the model is real at \(s = 0\) and causal. A physically sound grounding model must also be passive: \(\mathrm{Re}\,Z \geq 0\) at every frequency, also between the samples. check_passivity scans the band from 0 Hz to twice the highest fitted frequency.

    We fit with one to three poles and, as an independent check, fit again on the even-indexed frequencies only and predict the others.

    Summary¶

    • The frequency dependence below 1 kHz comes from the conductors; switch it on with inductance_model="neumann" and keep the default earth-return model sommerfeld for quantitative work.
    • The standard rho-f form gives one formula for a range of soils; check max_relative and predictions at resistivities outside the fit. Vector fits describe one soil very accurately; check rms_relative and passivity.
    • Fit the impedance you need: by default the helpers fit the input impedance (quantity="input"), the impedance a network model connects to.
