    ADR-0004: Inductive coupling between conductor segments¶
    Status Accepted — amended 2026.11 (review pass 10: image conventions, touching pairs, lumped conductors, skin effect); see the amendment at the end of this document
    Date 2026-05-04
    Deciders Project maintainers
    Scope groundfield

    Context¶

    ADR-0003 introduced the distributed-conductor model. Each conductor is split into \(n\) longitudinal sub-segments, and the resulting nodal-analysis system carries one branch per segment with series impedance

    \[ Z_\text{long}^{(k)} \;=\; R^{(k)} \;+\; j\omega\, L^{(k)}. \]

    ADR-0003 deferred \(L^{(k)}\) to a follow-up step. With \(L^{(k)} = 0\) the system is real and frequency-independent, and the engine.frequencies list returns the same DC solution for every entry. This is fine for DC-near grounding-impedance studies but not for analysing inductive coupling between the measurement lead and the current injection — the very effect that forced the distributed-conductor refactor in the first place.

    This ADR settles how the inductive part is added on top of the distributed model.

    Decision¶

    Physical model¶

    Self- and mutual-inductance of conductor segments are computed from Neumann's double-line integral (Grover 1946, Sunde 1968 ch. 7, Paul 2010 ch. 5):

    \[ M_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \oint_{C_i} \oint_{C_j} \frac{d\vec{\ell}_i \cdot d\vec{\ell}_j}{r_{ij}}. \]

    For two straight segments of equal length \(\ell\) that share the same axis direction at perpendicular distance \(d\), the closed form is

    \[ M_\parallel(\ell, d) \;=\; \frac{\mu_0\,\ell}{2\pi} \Bigl[\ln\!\Bigl(\frac{\ell + \sqrt{\ell^2 + d^2}}{d}\Bigr) - \frac{\sqrt{\ell^2 + d^2} - d}{\ell}\Bigr]. \]

    For arbitrary 3-D orientations and unequal lengths the implementation falls back to a two-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature of the double integral on each segment pair — sufficient because \(1/r_{ij}\) varies slowly over a single sub-segment, and the quadrature is cheap (constant cost per pair, total cost \(\mathcal{O}(M^2)\) for \(M\) segments). For the segment with itself we use the thin-wire approximation

    \[ L_\text{self} \;\approx\; \frac{\mu_0\,\ell}{2\pi} \Bigl[\ln\!\Bigl(\frac{2\ell}{a}\Bigr) - 1\Bigr], \]

    with \(a\) the wire radius. This formula is bounded between \(\ell/a > 10\) (thin-wire regime), which holds comfortably for the typical cable runs (\(\ell\) ≈ 1–5 m, \(a\) ≈ 4 mm).

    Earth return ("ideal earth")¶

    In this ADR the earth is treated as a perfect mirror for the magnetic field (\(\sigma_\text{earth} \to \infty\)): the image of a segment at depth \(z_s\) contributes its own Neumann integral against all other segments' real and image positions, exactly as in the electric image-charge sum used by the existing solvers. This is the same approximation already used by Sunde 1968 ch. 7.4 for buried power lines below 1 kHz; the finite earth conductivity correction (Carson) is the subject of a follow-up ADR.

    Schema¶

    Conductor gains one new field:

    inductance_model: Literal[None, "neumann"]   # default None
    • None (default) — backwards-compatible: \(L^{(k)} = 0\), the longitudinal branch is purely resistive, and the system is real per frequency (existing behaviour).
    • "neumann" — the segments contribute self- and mutual-inductance to the branch impedance via the formulas above. The longitudinal block of the augmented system becomes complex:
    \[ Z_b(\omega) \;=\; R + j\omega\, L \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times M}, \]

    with \(L\) a dense, symmetric, positive-definite matrix over the distributed-conductor branches. Mutual coupling exists between every pair of segments — not only within the same conductor — so that a measurement lead routed parallel to the current-injection lead automatically picks up its mutual inductance.

    Linear system¶

    The block structure of the augmented system is unchanged from ADR-0003:

    \[ \begin{bmatrix} Z_g & -C_s & 0 \\ C_s^{\top} & 0 & B^{\top} \\ 0 & B & -Z_b(\omega) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_\text{leak} \\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}_n \\ \mathbf{I}_\text{long} \end{bmatrix} \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{I}_\text{in} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}. \]

    The earth Green's function block \(Z_g\) stays real and frequency-independent (quasi-static, \(f < 1\,\mathrm{kHz}\)) — the finite-frequency correction would have to come from a magnetic Green's function on the layered soil, which is again Carson territory (ADR-0005). The \(Z_b(\omega)\) block carries all the frequency-dependent physics introduced here.

    Frequency loop¶

    When at least one conductor has inductance_model == "neumann", the solver:

    1. Discretises the geometry once and assembles \(Z_g\), the resistance vector \(R\), and the inductance matrix \(L\) — all frequency-independent.
    2. Loops over engine.frequencies. For each \(f_k\) the \(\omega = 2\pi f_k\) is plugged into \(Z_b(\omega)\) and the full complex linear system is solved with numpy.linalg.solve on the complex augmented matrix.
    3. Returns one complex potential / current entry per frequency in FieldResult.electrode_potentials[name][k] etc. — the FieldResult shape is unchanged; entries that used to be real per frequency are now genuinely complex.

    When all conductors have inductance_model is None (the default), the solver keeps the historic real-only fast path: one linalg.solve over the real augmented matrix, the result broadcast across engine.frequencies. Quasi-static DC studies stay bit-exact and at the same cost.

    Self-inductance and shielded conductors¶

    The thin-wire self-inductance assumes a bare conductor. For a cable with a conductor inside an outer sheath the relevant self-inductance is geometrically smaller; this distinction matters for shielded MV cables but not for the bare-copper / PEN typical study. We keep the model simple: inductance_model == "neumann" always uses the bare-conductor self formula; refinements come with the cable-shield work later.

    Validation¶

    • Single isolated segment — the segment's self-L matches the closed-form Grover formula above to within 0.5 % (we sweep \(\ell/a\) from 10 to 1000).
    • Two parallel coaxial segments — the Neumann quadrature reproduces \(M_\parallel(\ell, d)\) to within 1 % for separations \(d / \ell \in [0.1, 10]\).
    • Two perpendicular segments crossing in the middle — the Neumann integral evaluates to zero by symmetry (test tolerance \(10^{-9}\)).
    • DC reproducibility — at \(\omega = 0\) the inductive system collapses bit-exact to the resistive system from ADR-0003 (regression).
    • Cross-engine at \(f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}\) — image, mom, cim, bem agree on the cluster impedance of a galvanic distributed conductor with the inductive model active to within 5 %.
    • Loop coupling — two parallel galvanic conductors (one current injection, one open-circuit measurement lead) show the expected finite open-circuit voltage at 50 Hz that scales linearly with the source current and with \(\omega\).

    Consequences¶

    Positive¶

    • Covers inductive coupling between the measurement lead and the current injection. The model is now physically complete for \(f < 1\,\mathrm{kHz}\) except for the Carson earth-return correction.
    • The Neumann integral is geometric only — it works on top of any of the existing electric Green's-function backends without duplicating their kernel logic.
    • Frequency-resolved FieldResults without API change: the same electrode_potentials[name][k] shape now actually depends on \(f_k\).

    Negative¶

    • The system becomes complex when the inductive model is enabled, doubling the per-frequency LU factorisation cost. A frequency sweep with \(N_f\) frequencies costs \(N_f\) complex solves (vs. one real solve in the resistive-only case). For a typical sweep (\(N_f \le 20\), \(N \le 5\,000\) segments) this is still well inside the 32-GB / 12-core workstation budget.
    • The mutual-inductance matrix is dense and symmetric; the same ACA/iterative roadmap that applies to the electric Z-matrix applies here. Both will be tackled together.

    Neutral¶

    • Default behaviour (inductance_model is None) is unchanged. Existing notebooks and tests continue to produce DC results at identical cost.
    • The earth is still treated as a perfect mirror for the magnetic field — Carson finite-conductivity corrections are the subject of ADR-0005.

    References¶

    • Grover, F. W. (1946). Inductance Calculations: Working Formulas and Tables. Dover (reprint 2004).
    • Sunde, E. D. (1968). Earth Conduction Effects in Transmission Systems, Dover, ch. 7.
    • Paul, C. R. (2010). Inductance: Loop and Partial. Wiley.
    • Carson, J. R. (1926). Wave propagation in overhead wires with ground return. Bell Syst. Tech. J. 5(4) — referenced for the follow-up ADR-0005.

    Amendment 2026.11 — image conventions, exact touching pairs, lumped conductors, skin effect (review pass 10)¶

    Findings 10.20, 10.24, 10.37, 10.44, 10.46 and 10.60. The physical model of this ADR (Neumann partial inductances, complex branch block) stands; the following decisions refine it.

    1. The image is part of the earth model. The perfect-mirror image of section Earth return ("ideal earth") is one of three image conventions of build_inductance_matrix(image=...) (ImageConvention: "none", "additive", "antiparallel"). The solver builds the inductance matrix with the image of the selected earth_inductive_model: perfect_mirror → additive (this ADR), carson_series → anti-parallel (ADR-0005), sommerfeld → none (ADR-0006). An earth-return correction is defined against one image and must not be added to another (10.20). perfect_mirror has not been the default since 0.13.0.
    2. Pair integrals. Parallel, skew and perpendicular segment pairs use closed forms; pairs that share an end point (chain neighbours, bends) use Grover's vertex formula and the coaxial gap formula by default (touching="exact"), so a straight conductor keeps the inductance of the unsplit wire for every discretisation (10.46). touching="quadrature" reproduces 0.15.0.
    3. Internal inductance. Every self term carries the DC internal inductance \(\mu_0\ell/(8\pi)\) of a solid round conductor, for distributed and lumped branches alike (include_internal=True), except a cable shield (conductor_type="cable_shield"), a thin-walled tube without one (item 11, user decision Q33).
    4. Lumped and single-segment conductors. A conductor with an inductance_model that the topology keeps as one branch is one inductive branch from start to end, coupled to all others; up to 0.15.0 its inductance was dropped silently (10.24).
    5. Skin effect (opt-in). Conductor.resistance_model="skin" adds the exact internal impedance of a solid round conductor relative to the DC convention (resistance \(R_\text{ac}(f)\), internal inductance \(L_\text{int}(f)\)); the default "dc" keeps the numbers (10.37). The bare-conductor restriction of section Self-inductance and shielded conductors stands.
    6. Frequency loop. The earth-return correction is assembled once per solve for the whole frequency set (bit-identical to single-frequency assemblies, bounded cache; 10.60); the real fast path of purely galvanic worlds is unchanged.
    7. Close wires. Coincident conductors have no finite mutual inductance; find_close_wires and diagnose() report crossing, touching, interpenetrating and close wires (10.44).
    8. Wires closer than the sum of their radii (second adversarial audit). Two near-parallel segments whose axes stay closer than \(r_1 + r_2\) over a common length (coincident leads, a service drop along its PEN main) are evaluated as touching wires at the axis distance \(r_1 + r_2\), in every image convention; before, the quadrature stopped at its 1e-6 m floor (two coincident 50 m leads: 1.87 H). Close pairs that miss the closed forms — nearly parallel, crossing, skew pairs and T-junctions — are integrated semi-analytically (inner integral in closed form, graded outer panels), with touching="exact"; touching="quadrature" keeps the 0.15.0 rule. The coaxial closed forms are evaluated in one vectorised call per segment (audit A2C-5). The "5e-11" stated here holds for directions that differ by more than 1e-6 rad; see item 9 for the measured worst cases.
    9. Wires beside each other, tiny angles (third adversarial audit, A3C-1, A3C-2, A3C-4, A3C-5). The touching rule of item 8 switched a whole pair, and the decision depended on which segment was the row: a V pair of 1 m and 0.5 m at 1.5° read 0.362 or 0.503 µH, the generator's lumped in-line leads 0.134 or 0.127 Ω of measured reactance (50 Hz); tilted V and X pairs whose far ends lay just outside the cylinder kept a filament value above the self inductance (an indefinite matrix, a two-wire loop with a negative reactance). The rule now acts pointwise inside the Neumann integral: where a point of one wire lies beside the other wire (its foot on the other axis falls inside that wire) closer than \(r_1 + r_2\), that distance is used — touching wires — and where the thinner wire runs inside the thicker one (\(\rho + r_\text{thin} \le r_\text{thick}\), a core inside a cable shield) the thicker radius: a filament inside a thin tube couples to it as at the tube radius, which gives the coaxial loop inductance \(\frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}(\ln\frac{r_s}{r_c} + \frac14)\) per metre when the shield carries no internal inductance (+36 % before; since user decision Q32 the reading needs the shield to be marked, item 11). Both one-sided integrals (inner integral in closed form) are averaged, on end points in a fixed order, so every order and direction of the segments gives the same value bit for bit; the value is continuous in the geometry (4e-15 against an independent quadrature), chain neighbours and bends turning by less than 90° keep their closed forms, and 2000 random close configurations gave no indefinite matrix (700 before). Equal radii never count as one wire inside the other; for unequal radii the model steps from "inside" to "side by side" where the thinner axis leaves the thicker wire (the two describe different cables). Filaments from one vertex below 0.81° use a cancellation-free form of Grover's vertex formula (16 % off at 1.5e-6 rad before), and the close-pair rule takes the crossing point of nearly parallel axes from a cross product (9.5e-4 at 1e-8 rad before). Measured against mpmath: closed forms to round-off, vertex pairs to 4e-10 in a general orientation, close pairs 5e-11 above 1e-6 rad and 1.0e-9 at 1e-8 rad; pairs inside the parallel tolerance use the parallel closed form at the start offset (4.2e-9 for wires 1 cm apart tilted by 1e-10 rad, 1.7e-5 at 1 µm).
    10. Pairs across the surface (user decision Q27). Section Earth return ("ideal earth") applies the image to every pair, also to one segment above the soil surface (\(z < 0\), an overhead lead) and one below it (a buried PEN), for which a magnetic mirror between them has no physical basis. The numbers stay — perfect_mirror is a diagnostic baseline — but the solve emits one UserWarning per inductive assembly with the number of such pairs and the conductors on each side, pointing to sommerfeld; carson_series and sommerfeld couple such pairs through the field transmitted into the soil and do not warn. For two parallel 1 km wires, one 1 m above the surface and one 0.7 m deep and 1 m beside it, in 100 Ω·m, the additive image gives 2.28 times (50 Hz) and 2.77 times (1 kHz) the mutual reactance of sommerfeld; carson_series gives 1.12 and 1.03 times.
    11. Concentric cables by an explicit marker (user decisions Q32 and Q33). Item 9 read every thinner wire on the axis of a thicker one as a core inside a cable shield, so two separate cables of different radii drawn on one axis were one concentric cable. The reading now needs the thicker conductor to carry conductor_type="cable_shield" (the existing type tag, which selected only a plot colour before); two conductors of any other types on one axis are separate conductors and touch, as every pair lying side by side (build_inductance_matrix(..., shields=...) marks the segments at the low level). A shield is a thin-walled tube: its self terms carry no internal inductance of a solid wire, and Conductor.internal_impedance_per_length of a "dc" shield has no inductive part; the solid-round skin model (resistance_model="skin") is rejected for a shield. A 100 m coaxial pair (core 5 mm, shield 15 mm, 0.7 m deep) reads a loop inductance of 26.966 µH in a solve (perfect_mirror and sommerfeld; 26.970 µH with carson_series), whatever the sub-pieces of the two conductors (item 12), against the analytic 26.972 µH of the infinitely long coax, instead of 31.966 µH with the internal inductance of a solid shield; unmarked, the two cables read 43.472 µH (43.476 µH with carson_series), the 43.48 µH of two touching solid wires. A shield without a core loses \(\mu_0\ell/(8\pi)\) = 50 nH/m of self inductance against the same conductor without the tag (buried_lead() of the measurement generator builds its lead as a shield: the two-pole reading of a three-house network with buried feed and probe leads −2.5 % in reactance at 50 Hz and 1 kHz, the three-pole reading unchanged to 1e-6).
    12. Pieces of conductors lying beside each other (wave-1 audit of the calendar release, W1A-02). Item 9 decides per segment whether a point lies beside the partner, so a pair of sub-pieces of two conductors on one axis that do not overlap coupled as two filaments on the axis (distance 0) while the overlapping pairs coupled at the shield radius or at \(r_1 + r_2\): the pieces did not add up to the lumped pair. The 100 m coax read 26.912 / 26.852 / 26.374 / 24.608 µH in 10 / 5 / 1 / 0.25 m pieces (−0.2 … −8.7 %; every joint of the pieces cost about 6 nH), the unmarked pair 43.400 … 40.343 µH, the drops routed back along the PEN main of a golden case 3.9e-4 low in reactance at 1 kHz. Two rules now act on parallel pairs closer than touching. A thinner wire inside a cable shield (\(\delta + r_\text{thin} \le r_s\), \(\delta\) the lateral distance of the axes) couples at the shield radius at every axial offset — overlapping, end to end and apart — which is exact for a filament on the axis of a thin tube (every point of the tube is \(\sqrt{x^2 + r_s^2}\) away), so a shield made of several conductors, or a core and shield in sections, add up too. For two other conductors lying beside each other (the rule of item 9 acts on a parallel pair of their pieces), a point lies beside the partner when its foot falls inside the partner's whole conductor, not only inside the partner segment; the stretches have Grover's parallel-filament closed form. The solver passes one label per conductor (build_inductance_matrix(..., conductors=...); without labels every segment counts as a conductor of its own, the rule of item 9 in closed form). The pieces now add up to the lumped loop to 3e-12 (coax, marked and unmarked, 0.25 … 20 m pieces, all three earth-return models) and the drops along the main to 3e-15; the value stays continuous in the geometry, the same for every order and direction of the segments, and pieces of one conductor or conductors that only meet end to end on one axis (a main split at its taps) keep the filament integral (every other golden case bit-identical). The assembly of a 1 km coax in 2 m pieces takes 6.4 s instead of 5.8 s. Remaining: two unmarked cables in sections on one axis (a section of one beside a section of the other that it does not overlap) couple as filaments on the axis between the sections that do not overlap.
    13. Nearly parallel pieces (second wave-1 audit of the calendar release, W1R2-01, W1R2-03). The closed form of item 12 needs the pieces parallel within \(|\cos\varphi| \ge 1 - 10^{-9}\), about 4.5e-5 rad. A core tilted by more against its shield kept the rule of item 9 per segment, so the drift of item 12 came back in full, with a step at the tolerance: the 100 m coax read 26.966 µH in 0.25 m pieces at 4.4e-5 rad and 24.966 µH at 4.5e-5 rad (−7.4 %; −0.17 / −0.36 / −1.86 % in 10 / 5 / 1 m pieces). Pieces of two conductors that are nearly parallel — the lateral distance changes by at most the touching distance \(r_1 + r_2\) along the longer piece, and \(|\cos\varphi| \ge 0.99\); both bounds were dropped by item 14 — now get the two readings of item 12 against the partner's whole conductor with the stretches found exactly: each piece is split where the foot of its points passes an end of the partner's conductor and where the distance \(\rho(s)\) from the partner's axis crosses \(r_1 + r_2\) or \(r_\text{thick} - r_\text{thin}\) (from the closest approach of the two lines). On the stretches inside the shield and beside the partner the effective distance is constant and the integral has a closed form in the foot coordinate (\(du = \cos\varphi\,ds\)); a side that is entirely free takes the filament value of the pair, and a side with free and clamped stretches (a piece where the core leaves the touching distance, or passes an end of the partner) is integrated by the quadrature of item 9 with the conductor extents. The pieces add up to the lumped pair for tilts 0 … 1e-3 rad in 10 / 5 / 1 / 0.25 m pieces, in the matrix within 6.4e-12 (marked) and 3.1e-10 (unmarked; the largest value is the parallel closed form just below its tolerance, at 4.4e-5 rad) and in the solver (perfect_mirror and sommerfeld within 3.2e-11 marked, 3.1e-10 unmarked; under carson_series the Carson correction of one lumped pair, tiled at its mid-point distance, differed from the pieces by 1.7e-5 at 3e-4 rad and 1.3e-4 at 1e-3 rad, independent of the piece length — the correction's own tiling, not the rule; integrated per stretch since audit W1R3-07, ADR-0005 item 7). The literal proposal, the closed form at the mean lateral distance of each pair, missed by up to 1.35 % at 1.5e-4 … 7e-4 rad, where the core leaves the shield's tube inside a piece. Beyond the gate the rule of item 9 acted per segment. Where the core leaves the axis at the shield's start the touching stretch is then about one piece long, and the pieces differed from the lumped pair by 1.5e-7 (10 m pieces, 2.01e-3 rad), 6.6e-7 (1 m, 2.01e-2 rad) and 1.4e-6 (0.25 m, 8.01e-2 rad); in general the error was not small (item 14). The generator's overhead measurement leads are such a pair (the probe lead starts 10 mm beside the feed lead and ends on its axis, 2e-4 rad): their mutual in the default 5 m pieces was +1.0e-4 off the lumped one (1 m pieces +5.4e-4), now 4e-16; the probe junction potential moves by 6.5e-5 … 1.1e-4, the feed potentials and every electrode result not at all. Without labels (conductors=None) unmarked touching pieces stay per segment, while the inside reading of a marked shield needs no labels, also tilted. The pairs of two bundled conductors are evaluated in chunks of 4096 (W1R2-03; element-wise, so bit-identical): a 600 m pair in 1 m pieces peaked at 292 MiB with all pairs at once, now 30 MiB, the level before item 12.
    14. No length gate and no angular cap (third wave-1 audit of the calendar release, W1R3-01, W1R3-02). The two bounds of item 13 mixed two readings within one pair of conductors, and between a lumped pair and its pieces. Where the core crosses the shield axis at mid-length the touching stretch is two pieces long: just beyond the length gate the loop in 10 / 5 / 1 / 0.25 m pieces read −8.6e-5 / −6.4e-5 / −4.0e-5 / −3.0e-5 off the lumped pair (solver: X −7.6e-5 / −5.6e-5 in 10 / 5 m pieces under perfect_mirror and sommerfeld, homogeneous, two- and three-layer soil). Long pieces at small tilts read −1.7e-4 (2 × 50 m, 5e-4 rad), shield pieces of 15 / 1 m at 2e-3 rad −1.6e-3, and a core running 5 m beyond its shield +2.9e-5, its lumped mutual 4.4e-6 off the independent quadrature (the lumped loop stepped by −3.3e-5 at the gate). At the cap, two 2 m wires leaving one junction in 0.02 m pieces read 0 at 8.0° and −1.55e-3 at 8.2°. Decision of the coordinator: the rule acts on every pair of pieces of two conductors with a hit of the pointwise rule that is neither parallel within the closed form's tolerance nor orthogonal, at any angle and for pieces of any length; the classification of item 13 is exact at any angle. Every case above now adds up to the lumped pair within 2.2e-12, every lumped mutual equals the independent quadrature within 4.4e-16, the loop is smooth in the tilt (no step at the old gate, none at 8.1°; wires leaving a junction add up at 1° … 90° in 0.1 / 0.05 / 0.02 m pieces), and the matrices stay symmetric and positive definite. In the solver the pieces equal the lumped pair within 1.1e-15 under perfect_mirror; under sommerfeld within 1.6e-8 at 1 kHz (3.2e-7 at 10 × the old gate in 5 m pieces), the correction's own piece dependence, the same inside the old gate; under carson_series the correction of a tilted pair was tiled at the mid-point distance of each pair and the pieces differed from the lumped pair by 4.2e-4 (10 m pieces, 2e-3 rad), 1.4e-3 (5 m, 4e-3 rad), 1.9e-2 (10 m, 2e-2 rad) — the tiling, not this rule; per stretch since audit W1R3-07 (within 7.8e-6, ADR-0005 item 7). Only pairs with a piece that comes within the touching distance of the other's axis are classified (the others keep the filament value either way; bit-identical): a 600 m coax crossing at 0.05 / 0.3 rad in 1 m pieces assembles in 3.95 / 3.82 s against 3.73 / 3.66 s on the snapshot, where the gates skipped these pairs. Generated networks are bit-identical, with and without an inductive PEN.
