    ADR-0007: Cross-layer electrodes and conductors¶
    Status Accepted — step 1 (interface split), layer-aware post-processing and the mom lift implemented 2026-07-09 (audit WP-D; see the amendment at the end); amended 2026.11 (review pass 10: one owner per layer, slivers, reciprocity)
    Date 2026-05-07
    Deciders Project maintainers
    Scope groundfield

    Context¶

    Every layered-soil backend currently rejects worlds in which any electrode segment crosses a layer interface:

    ValueError: image_2layer: a segment lies below the layer interface
(z_max = 2.500 m, h_1 = 1.000 m). The current backend assumes all
electrodes sit in the upper layer.

    This precondition exists in image_2layer, mom_sommerfeld, cim and bem. The reason is purely numerical: the Tagg/Sunde image series and the Sommerfeld kernel as currently implemented both assume source and observer in the same upper layer. The mathematical Green's function for two-layer earth supports arbitrary layer combinations, the implementation just hasn't been extended yet.

    For typical applications this is a blocking limitation. Realistic geometries that we must be able to compute include:

    • Driven rods (Tiefenerder) of 1.5–3 m length at house connections that pass through the typical \(h_1 = 0.5\,\)m to \(5\,\)m upper layer into the lower one.
    • Foundation electrodes at depths around 0.8–1.5 m that may straddle a thin top layer.
    • MV cable shields routed at 1.0–1.5 m depth, often below the resistive top layer of dry-fill / paving.
    • Cable cabinets and substations with deep grounding stars.

    Without cross-layer support the user has to either truncate the geometry artificially (wrong physics) or thicken the upper layer beyond physical reality (wrong soil model). Neither option is acceptable for typical cases.

    Decision¶

    Physical model¶

    For a 2-layer earth (upper layer \(\rho_1\) thick \(h_1\), lower layer \(\rho_2\) semi-infinite), the electric Green's function for a source at depth \(z_s\) observed at depth \(z\) has four pair-type cases that depend on which layer the source and the observer inhabit:

    \[ G(\vec r, \vec r')\;=\;\begin{cases} G_{uu}(\vec r,\vec r') & 0\le z, z_s\le h_1 \quad\text{(both upper)} \\ G_{ul}(\vec r,\vec r') & 0\le z\le h_1,\; z_s>h_1 \\ G_{lu}(\vec r,\vec r') & z>h_1,\; 0\le z_s\le h_1 \\ G_{ll}(\vec r,\vec r') & z, z_s>h_1 \quad\text{(both lower)} \end{cases} \]

    With \(G_{ul} = G_{lu}\) by reciprocity. Each of the four kernels has a closed-form Tagg/Sunde image-series representation and a direct Sommerfeld-quadrature representation, both documented in Sunde 1968 ch. 3 and Tagg 1964. In the limit \(\rho_2 = \rho_1\) all four collapse to the homogeneous kernel (K=0); in the limit \(\rho_2 \to \infty\) they collapse to "PEC at \(z = h_1\)" (no current can enter the lower layer).

    Implementation strategy — three phases¶

    This ADR ships Phase A in the same release. Phase B and C are explicit follow-up work documented here so the API contract is stable.

    Phase A — Discretiser split + uniform numerical kernel¶

    1. Discretiser split. _discretize_electrode and _discretize_conductor get a new optional argument layer_interfaces=[h_1, h_2, ...]. When a segment would cross a layer boundary at depth \(h_k\), it is split at that depth into two segments. After this step every segment lies fully in exactly one layer; a new _Segment.layer_index field records which.
    2. Layered Green's-function kernel. A new coupling.layered_green module evaluates \(G_{ij}(\vec r, \vec r';\,\rho_1,\rho_2,h_1)\) for any pair \((i, j) \in \{u, l\}^2\) via direct Hankel-transform numerical integration of the spectral solution to the 2-layer matching problem. The kernel is consumed by all four layered backends.
    3. Lift the preconditions. z_max < h_1 is replaced by a discretiser-split call. Every segment-pair entry in the reaction matrix uses the appropriate kernel \(G_{uu}, G_{ul}, G_{ll}\) depending on the layer indices.
    4. Image-series fast path (Tagg/Sunde) is preserved for the common \(G_{uu}\) case; the slower spectral-quadrature path is used for \(G_{ul}\), \(G_{lu}\), \(G_{ll}\).

    Phase B — Closed-form image series for ll and ul (planned)¶

    The Tagg/Sunde image series for \(G_{ll}\) uses the lower-to-upper reflection coefficient \(-K\) and an image structure analogous to the \(uu\) series; the \(ul\) case carries a transmission factor \((1 + K)\) and progressively deeper "transmitted-then-reflected" images. Both are derivable in closed form (Sunde 1968, eqs. 3.32–3.34) and will replace the slower spectral-quadrature path in _layered_green_kernel for typical worlds with many cross-layer segments.

    Speedup target: Phase A's geometric Sommerfeld quadrature is \(\mathcal{O}(N_\lambda)\) per pair (≈ 128 spectral evaluations per pair × 9 line-line samples = 1 152 evaluations per diagonal entry, 128 per off-diagonal). Phase B's truncated image series truncates at \(|K|^n < \text{tol}\), typically 10–30 terms, with each term being a cheap \(1/r\) — speedup factor ≈ 30–100x.

    API contract: no public API change. The dispatcher in _two_layer_self_kernel_factory(allow_cross_layer=True) will automatically pick the closed-form path when both \(z_i, z_j \le h_1\) + Tagg/Sunde-uu (already implemented), and the new closed-form \(ll\) and \(ul\) helpers when one or both segments are in the lower layer. The Sommerfeld quadrature stays as a sanity check.

    Validation programme for Phase B (when implemented):

    1. ll vs Phase-A Sommerfeld at \(f = 0\) Hz, ρ_2/ρ_1 = 10, h_1 = 1 m, z = z_s = 2 m, s = 0.1 m → ≤ 0.5 % rel difference.
    2. ul vs Phase-A Sommerfeld in the symmetric configuration (same parameters, different z, z_s) → reciprocity check ≤ 1e-9 abs.
    3. ρ_1 = ρ_2 limit: every term beyond \(K^0\) vanishes, the series reduces to the homogeneous free-surface image — bit-exact.
    4. End-to-end: 1 km PEN on a 2-layer world with rod tip 1 m below h_1 — Phase B vs Phase A on the cluster impedance ≤ 1 % rel, wall-clock speedup ≥ 30x.

    Status: deferred to a focused follow-up where the derivation can be cross-checked against Sunde 1968 and Tagg 1964 worked examples. For production-grade single-frequency runs at \(\lesssim 500\) segments, the Phase A path is acceptable (a few seconds per solve); Phase B becomes essential for the parameter-sweep phase of large parameter studies (many EFH counts × multiple soil models × multiple frequencies).

    Phase C — n \ge 3 layers (deferred)¶

    The MultiLayerSoil case generalises the 2-layer image series to a recursive Tagg-Sunde stack with reflection coefficients \(\Gamma_k(\lambda)\) at each interface. This is consistent with the existing image_nlayer dispatcher and the Sommerfeld formulation in solver/cim.py / solver/bem.py. Phase C is a straightforward extension once Phase A is stable.

    API impact¶

    • Engine schema is unchanged.
    • World / Conductor / Electrode are unchanged.
    • _Segment gains a layer_index: int field.
    • _discretize_electrode, _discretize_conductor accept layer_interfaces and split there.
    • All four layered backends drop the if z_max >= h_1: raise check and dispatch the kernel by (layer_i, layer_j).
    • The user-visible behaviour is strictly additive: every geometry that worked before continues to produce the same result; geometries that were rejected before now produce a meaningful FieldResult.

    Validation¶

    tests/test_cross_layer.py:

    1. Homogeneous limit (\(\rho_1 = \rho_2\)): for every backend and every layer-pair case, the cross-layer kernel reproduces the existing homogeneous kernel to within \(10^{-9}\).
    2. PEC limit (\(\rho_2 \to \infty\)): for source in upper and \(\rho_2 / \rho_1 = 10^6\), the spreading resistance approaches the upper-layer-only hemispherical asymptote.
    3. Sink limit (\(\rho_2 \to 0\)): for source in upper and \(\rho_2 / \rho_1 = 10^{-6}\), spreading resistance drops by the textbook factor.
    4. Discretiser split: a 3-m rod at \(z = 0.5\) over a 2-layer soil with \(h_1 = 1.5\) m is split into 2 segments inside layer 1 and 1 segment inside layer 2; segment count and per-segment layer indices are checked explicitly.
    5. Cross-engine consistency at 50 Hz: image_2layer, mom_sommerfeld, cim, bem agree on the cluster impedance of a rod-through-interface within 5 %.
    6. driven rod: 3-m rod, \(h_1 = 1\) m, \(\rho_1 = 300\), \(\rho_2 = 50\,\Omega\,\mathrm{m}\) — sanity check on the cluster impedance against the Dwight 1936 formula adapted for 2-layer (Dwight + correction factor).
    7. Notebook 16 layered sweep runs through every \(h_1 \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0\}\) without error.
    8. DC reproducibility regression: every old test that uses z_max < h_1 continues to pass bit-exact.

    Consequences¶

    Positive¶

    • realistic geometries run. Driven rods, foundation electrodes, deep meshes — all supported on every layered backend.
    • The Sommerfeld kernel in mom_sommerfeld becomes more general; this also lifts ADR-0006's Pillar B layered Sommerfeld coupling for cross-layer wires.
    • Layered earth no longer needs a UserWarning anywhere in the stack. Carson series remains as the asymptotic option but is honest about its limitation: per-meter formula × length, top layer only.

    Negative¶

    • Phase A's spectral-quadrature path for cross-layer pairs is slower than the closed-form image series. For typical worlds with ≲ 1000 segments and a single-frequency or short-frequency sweep this is acceptable; for very large worlds Phase B is the optimisation lever.
    • Discretiser splits add segments at layer interfaces; the total segment count grows by ~\(O(n_\text{cross})\) where \(n_\text{cross}\) is the number of conductors crossing interfaces. For typical this is small.

    Neutral¶

    • Default behaviour unchanged for all geometries that previously worked.
    • The new _Segment.layer_index field is exposed but not documented as a public API — internal solver detail.

    References¶

    • Sunde, E. D. (1968). Earth Conduction Effects in Transmission Systems, Dover. Chapter 3, §3.5–3.7.
    • Tagg, G. F. (1964). Earth Resistances, George Newnes. Two-layer image series for source/observer in either layer.
    • Wait, J. R. (1972). Electromagnetic Waves in Stratified Media, Pergamon. General reflection-coefficient algebra.
    • Tleis, N. D. (2008). Power Systems Modelling and Fault Analysis, Newnes. Modern transmission-line interpretation of the layered Green's function.

    Amendment 2026-07-09 — WP-D (audit 2026-07-08)¶

    Three gaps between this ADR and the implementation were closed:

    1. Step 1 (interface-split discretiser) is now implemented. The original phase plan promised segments that never straddle the interface; the discretiser had ignored layer_interfaces and assigned straddling segments by midpoint (mis-assignment up to ds/2). Rods and sloped strips now split at h_1 in every layered backend; a margin warning fires when the deepest segment ends within 2·segment_length above h_1.
    2. Post-solve field evaluation dispatches by layer. FieldResult.potential and the solve_mutual_* probe kernels used the upper-layer image series for all points (measured +7 % at z = 7 m, +25 % at z = 12 m for K = +0.818); layer-2 pairs now use the spectral kernel (two_layer_probe_matrix).
    3. mom no longer rejects cross-layer worlds — the action item "lift the remaining backends" is done for the n = 2 family (mom/cim/bem and the mom_sommerfeld diagonal all dispatch the cross-layer kernel; n ≥ 3 cross-layer raises everywhere).

    Performance note: the cross-layer reaction-matrix correction is now grouped by depth pairs (one spectral BVP solve per (z_i, z_j) group instead of per matrix entry) and cached across the kernel calls of one solve — a 3-rod cross-layer world dropped from 33.7 s to 4.6 s on the audit sandbox with identical results.

    Amendment 2026.11 — one owner per layer, slivers, reciprocity, line kernel (review pass 10)¶

    Findings 10.15 (F02), 10.25, 10.26, 10.28, 10.32 and 10.50 (F43).

    1. Current per layer. The image family carries one uniform current per owner. An electrode that crosses an interface is now one owner per layer it reaches, all in its cluster — the model of two rods bonded at \(h_1\) (10.15). With one owner across the interface a 12 m rod through \(h_1 = 5\) m in 1000 / 30 Ω·m soil was 566 % above the per-segment mom solution; it is now within the ansatz error of mom. solve_mutual_matrix / solve_mutual_field follow the same split, and the diagonal of the mutual matrix is the length-weighted cluster impedance over all owners (10.26).
    2. Slivers. The point kernel takes the Galerkin self term below \(L/a = 2.99\) (10.32). Every interface cut is kept, however short the piece beyond it. (A first version dropped cuts that would leave a piece shorter than the wire diameter, SLIVER_RADII = 2; that ignored the contact with the other layer — a 4.5 m rod in 1000 / 30 Ω·m jumped from 195.0 to 128.6 Ω between 15.9 and 16.1 mm of tip penetration — and was replaced before the release, second adversarial audit A2S-01. The Galerkin self term and the line kernel's parallel-pair integral are evaluated without cancellation for such pieces.) The diagonal evaluates the closed-form \(1/R\) terms of the layered correction at the wire radius instead of their default clamp \(h_1/200\), which took 68–84 % of the correction of every piece within \(h_1/400\) of the interface (a ring at \(h_1\) = 5 m read −5.29 Ω; third adversarial audit A3S-01); off the diagonal they are regularised at the 1 mm of the point forms. What remains at a rod end is a limit of the thin-wire model, which has no end face: +21 % with the tip exactly at \(h_1\) against a solid-rod reference, −4.6 % 1 mm past it; such ends warn (InterfaceContactWarning, A3S-05).
    3. Reciprocity and the line kernel. With the exact line kernel (ADR-0015) the direct term of a pair in different layers carries the transmitted resistivity \(2\rho_1\rho_2/(\rho_1+\rho_2)\) and the near-singular interface image of the layered correction is a Galerkin integral for near pairs; the reaction matrix is reciprocal to round-off and the impedance of a rod through the interface changes by less than \(10^{-4}\) per halving of the segment length.
    4. Point form on both sides. The point-form guard of a horizontal wire within 0.387 (error) or 0.6 (warning) segment lengths of \(h_1\) applies above the interface too, where the series takes the image of the segment in the interface as a point source: a ring 1 µm above \(h_1\) read −1931 Ω with the point kernel and the mom family (A3S-02).
    5. Series bookkeeping. Cross-layer solves report series_used = False and no false SeriesTruncationWarning (10.50); the post-solve field evaluation uses the series controls of the solve (10.25).
    6. fem raises for an electrode through an interface its equivalent hemisphere does not reach (ADR-0002 amendment 2026.11).

    Amendment 2026.11 (wave 3) — line form, memory, speed¶

    Finding 10.28 (the line kernel on the cross-layer path), 10.56 (memory), the roadmap row Vectorise the ADR-0007 cross-layer Sommerfeld path.

    1. Line form of the layered correction. With the line kernel the layered correction was evaluated between segment midpoints, except the interface image of near pairs in one layer. In two-layer soil it is an exact superposition of images in every layer combination (the generations of layered_green._image_generation): with \(K = (\rho_2 - \rho_1)/(\rho_2 + \rho_1)\), \(c = 2\rho_1\rho_2/(\rho_1 + \rho_2)\), \(q = 2h_1\) and relative to the baseline of the source layer, \(\rho_1 K^m\) at the four Tagg/Sunde images for two points in layer 1; \(-\rho_2 K\) at \(q - z_s\), \(-\rho_2 K^2\) at \(-z_s\) and \(\rho_2(1 - K^2)K^m\) at \(-z_s - mq\) in layer 2; \(cK^m\) at \(z_s \pm mq\) and \(-z_s - mq\) (\(m \ge 1\)) across the interface. The spectral values stay the point form; each image adds its line-kernel correction as on the series path (far quadrupole term, blended Galerkin integrals of near pairs, Galerkin self images). A rod through the interface no longer depends on the segment length (within 9e-7 from 2 m to 0.125 m, before 1.3e-4 … 2.4e-4) and the path reproduces the series for wires in the upper layer to 1.7e-7 (before 6.2e-4). The field of sources in the lower layer (FieldResult.potential, solve_mutual_field) stays in point form.
    2. Memory. A depth group larger than one block is planned once from the range of its distances and evaluated in row chunks (bit-identical), and beyond the matrix budget the path works in row blocks like the series path: the AP1 station at 40 % penetration (\(N\) = 2711) needs 134 instead of 278 MiB (the within-layer path 126 MiB).
    3. Speed. The path was grouped by depth since the 2026-07-08 audit; what remained were one LAPACK solve of a 3 × 3 system per quadrature node, twice per group — half the time of a cross-layer solve, and batching them into one call gains nothing. The system is eliminated in closed form, vectorised over the nodes: with the point kernel, which sees only that change, the AP1 station solves 1.6 … 2.0 times faster (5 … 40 % penetration); results move by round-off (the elimination is at least as accurate as LAPACK against a 50-digit reference). With the line kernel the far terms of point 1 cost about as much again at 40 % (performance page).

    Amendment 2026.11 (wave 3, audit W3A) — image pairs, cost, the point form¶

    Audit W3A and the author's decision of 2026-11-15 (stream fw3xl; ADR-0015, amendment W3A).

    1. The images of the layered correction take the transverse distance of the wire from the image (ADR-0015); a ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface is within 0.004 % of its exact thin ring (+1.60 % before), the rod through the interface within 1.4e-6 of the auditor's exact two-owner value, and the two paths agree to 1.8e-7 where both apply.
    2. A segment's own image on the diagonal follows the rule of every pair: only where the segment reaches the image (before, a short segment took every far image of the world's longest segment); the far terms are evaluated on the upper half of the symmetric correction. The AP1 station at 40 % penetration solves in 1.35 s again (1.86 s at wave 3, 1.34 s before it; 2.35 s without numba), 16 house grids below \(h_1\) = 0.5 m in 0.54 s (0.91 s, 0.29 s), with image_far_field="point" in 0.93 and 0.32 s (performance page).
    3. With Engine.image_far_field = "point" the layered correction is in line form for the interface image of pairs in one layer only — near pairs blended, the diagonal at every distance — as before wave 3.
