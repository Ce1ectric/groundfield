    Concrete-encased electrodes¶

    This page explains how groundfield models an earth electrode embedded in concrete — the foundation earth electrode of a building above all: the shell coefficient, how each engine uses it, how far the current model of the image family is from a per-segment reference for such electrodes, and what changed against 0.15.0. Read it before you set concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m or a generator's concrete_rho_ohm_m, or when foundation results differ from those of 0.15.0.

    A foundation earth electrode (Fundamenterder, DIN 18014) lies inside the concrete of the strip foundation: every ampere it leaks has to cross the concrete before it reaches the soil. Concrete conducts like soil — wet concrete at tens of Ω·m, dry concrete at thousands — so the encasement adds a series resistance that can be negligible or dominant depending on the moisture.

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    Around a wire of radius \(r_a\) the concrete forms a cylindrical shell of thickness \(t\) (outer radius \(r_b = r_a + t\)) and resistivity \(\rho_c\); outside lies the soil. A wire that leaks the current \(I'\) per metre drives it radially through the shell: at the radius \(r\) the current density is \(I'/(2\pi r)\) and the field \(\rho_c I'/(2\pi r)\), so the voltage across the shell is \(\int_{r_a}^{r_b}\rho_c I'/(2\pi r)\,\mathrm dr\). In this cylindrically symmetric limit the shell and the soil add in series along the radius (Sunde 1968, §4.4), and per metre of wire the shell contributes

    \[ R'_\text{shell} = \frac{\rho_c}{2\pi}\,\ln\frac{r_b}{r_a} \equiv C \qquad\text{(in } \Omega\cdot\text{m)}, \]

    the radial resistance of one metre of shell, while the soil sees a bare wire of the enlarged radius \(r_b\). The decomposition is exact for an infinite straight wire and a good approximation for a finite electrode as long as the leakage current varies slowly along the wire on the scale of the shell — always the case below 1 kHz.

    Concrete around the wire is local: modelling it as a horizontal soil layer would put concrete under the whole neighbourhood, which is why groundfield refuses the planar interpretation (ADR-0012). Fagan & Lee (1970) and Ufer (1964) measured such concrete-encased electrodes; their low resistance in moist concrete is the reason foundation electrodes work at all.

    One mechanism: the shell coefficient¶

    Every electrode kind (rod, ring, strip, polyline, star, mesh, grid mesh) has the field concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m — the coefficient \(C\) above. The wire radius of an encased wire is the outer radius \(r_b\). The image family, solve_mutual_matrix / solve_mutual_field, mom and mom_sommerfeld consume it per segment: a segment of length \(\Delta s\) adds \(C/\Delta s\) to its own diagonal entry of the reaction matrix — the voltage drop of its share of the leakage current across its piece of shell — and nothing to the off-diagonal entries, because shells of different segments do not couple.

    Under the uniform current of an owner (one straight wire, or its part in one soil layer) of length \(L\), segment \(k\) carries the share \(I\,\Delta s_k/L\) of the current \(I\), and the per-segment terms add up to \(\sum_k (C/\Delta s_k)(\Delta s_k/L)^2 = C/L\):

    \[ Z(C) - Z(0) = \frac{C}{L}, \]

    the lumped series resistance of the whole shell, for every electrode kind, homogeneous or layered soil, in Engine.solve and in solve_mutual_matrix alike (tests/test_concrete_shell_coefficient.py). fem consumes \(C\) as well: its axisymmetric model as the surface conductance \(1/(2\pi r_b C)\), its three-dimensional model as the radial resistance \(C\) per length between the metal and its smoothed line source (fem).

    Measurements and audit trail

    mom and mom_sommerfeld rejected encased electrodes up to 2026.11; they consume the shell per segment since then (next section).

    In the moment method¶

    mom and mom_sommerfeld solve one pulse current \(I_k\) per segment, all segments of a node at one potential (point matching). A segment of length \(\Delta s_k\) drops \(C I_k/\Delta s_k\) across its piece of shell, so the segment system reads

    \[ \bigl(K + S\bigr)\,\mathbf I = \varphi\,\mathbf 1, \qquad S = \operatorname{diag}(C_k/\Delta s_k), \qquad \sum_k I_k = I , \]

    every segment at the potential \(\varphi\) of the metal, with \(K\) the soil reaction matrix (in Ω) of the wire of outer radius \(r_b\) and \(C_k\) the coefficient of segment \(k\). The reported segment potentials, and with them the node potential, are those of the metal, and the field in the soil is that of the leakage currents.

    Unlike the uniform current of an owner, the current redistributes. The equipotential solution minimises the dissipation \(\mathbf I^\mathsf{T}(K + S)\mathbf I\) over \(\sum_k I_k = I\). The shell term alone is smallest for a current uniform per length, where it equals \(C I^2/L\), so the shell raises \(Z\) by at least \(C/L\); and by at most \(C/L\) plus the excess of the uniform current over the equipotential one. A dry shell evens the current out: \(Z - C/L\) is within \(5\cdot10^{-6}\) of the uniform-current value of the bare wire at \(C = 3.8\cdot10^{5}\) Ω·m for a 3 m rod, an 8 m × 6 m grid and a three-arm star.

    Two methods check the implementation:

    • Same matrix physics. The image family with one owner per segment (point kernel, every segment its own uniform-current owner, all bonded) solves the same system and agrees with mom to \(7\cdot10^{-16}\). mom_sommerfeld reproduces mom to about \(10^{-8}\) where neither engine warns, and the shell increments \(Z(C) - Z(0)\) of the two engines agree to \(1.4\cdot10^{-10}\).
    • Closed forms. The segments of a ring are equivalent (its matrix is circulant), so its current stays uniform and the shell adds exactly \(C/(2\pi r)\): mom and mom_sommerfeld give it to \(10^{-14}\) in homogeneous, two-layer and three-layer soil for \(C\) = 11 … 382 Ω·m. For a 3 m rod from the surface in 100 Ω·m the reference is Sunde's decomposition with Dwight's rod formula, \(R = C/L + \frac{\rho}{2\pi L}\bigl(\ln\frac{4L}{r_b} - 1\bigr)\): the image family's uniform current (line kernel) lies 0.14 … 0.20 % above it and rises by exactly \(C/L\); mom lies below it by the point kernel's deficit (kernels), 0.8 … 2.7 % for the encased rod (details below).

    What the generators build¶

    Both concrete_model settings of the generators produce the same representation:

    1. every wire of an encased foundation carries \(C\) and the radius \(r_b\) — the perimeter and, for a mesh foundation, the cross-braces (see Cross-braces of mesh foundations);
    2. rods and the other electrodes of a site stay bare (\(r_a\), \(C = 0\));
    3. no shell resistance on PEN service drops (it would count twice);
    4. substation and cabinet electrodes get \(C\) when their specification is encased;
    5. every rectangle without cross-braces (style="ring", or "mesh" with n_x = n_y = 1) is one owner per side at every orientation (user decision Q38, see Owners of a rectangle).

    concrete_model therefore has no effect and is deprecated (removal no earlier than 2027.5): "distributed" still loads and warns, the default "lumped" and the files of 0.15.0 load silently; drop the argument (see Deprecations). World.concrete_shell_corrections is deprecated as well (it still loads and warns at solve; removal no earlier than 2027.5).

    Why one mechanism (findings 10.12–10.14)

    Up to 0.15.0 the two variants of ADR-0012 travelled different roads. The distributed variant put the shell on the wires; the lumped variant recorded a total shell resistance in the world registry World.concrete_shell_corrections, which only the TN generator turned into a series resistance on the PEN service drop. Every other path — Engine.solve of any other world, solve_mutual_matrix, solve_mutual_field, the layout builder — ignored the registry but kept the inflated wire radius. For the AP1 catalogue, which builds its foundation matrix with solve_mutual_matrix, the concrete therefore lowered the impedance instead of raising it at low soil resistivity. A lumped shell spread over a mixed site also pushed rod and brace current through the foundation's shell, and mesh braces declared unencased got the inflated radius. Up to user decision Q38 "lumped" made an axis-aligned rectangle without cross-braces a single grid-mesh owner.

    The golden case mutual_foundations_lumped_concrete records the effect on a foundation matrix: its diagonal rises because the lumped shell now reaches solve_mutual_matrix; the release notes give the magnitudes for AP1-type sites. Since user decision Q38 its two square rings are one owner per side as well, so the passive one carries equalising currents among its sides: the matrix moves by a further 5.2e-4, and the field at the edges of the passive foundation by +5.5 / −4.7 % (the default line kernel at 2 m and 1 m segments, the point kernel at 1 m; the golden engine's point kernel at 2 m segments gives +5.9 / −5.1 %, an effect of the coarse segments, not of the kernel; audit R4B-13), 1.6 % of the largest value of the case's field (the norm-wise measure of the golden harness; audit D3B-02).

    Cross-braces of mesh foundations¶

    A mesh foundation (FoundationElectrodeSpec(style="mesh"), the generators' default for buildings) joins its perimeter with cross-braces. DIN 18014 lays the foundation earth electrode in the concrete together with its connections, so the braces are encased like the perimeter: every wire of an encased mesh carries \(r_b\) and \(C\) (user decision of 2026.11). The shell resistance of the whole foundation is \(C/L\) over every encased wire, the per-wire shells \(C/L_j\) in parallel; concrete_shell(spec, encased_length_m=...) in groundfield.generators.materialise reports it.

    The rule matters as soon as the concrete is drier than the soil. Bare braces are a bare electrode inside the concrete: however dry the concrete, the foundation could not exceed the spreading resistance of its braces alone (9.60 Ω for the house mesh below in 100 Ω·m). Encased, the shell term grows with \(\rho_c\); for \(\rho_c \gg \rho\) the current spreads over the wires in proportion to their length and \(Z \to C/L + Z_\text{soil}\), with \(Z_\text{soil}\) = 4.105 Ω for a uniform current over the 60 m of the house mesh.

    The table gives the change of the input impedance when the braces are encased as well, against the bare braces (\(r_a\), no \(C\)) of the 2026.11 development line — a rule that was never released but isolates the braces; the change against 0.15.0 is in Against 0.15.0:

    Mesh, soil \(\rho_c\) = 50 100 150 1 000 5 000 Ω·m
    house, 100 Ω·m −1.1 % −0.03 % +1.5 % +47 % +307 %
    house, 30 / 100 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m +1.3 % +6.2 % +12 % +154 % +861 %
    medium_industry, 100 Ω·m −1.3 % −0.03 % +1.6 % +46 % +298 %

    House mesh: the generators' default (10 m, 2 × 2 cells, 0.8 m deep, \(r_a\) = 5 mm, \(t\) = 5 cm); medium_industry: 20 m, 3 × 3 cells, same wire and cover. Default engine (image family, line kernel, 0.5 m segments). Concrete wetter than the soil lowers \(Z\) a little (the wire grows to \(r_b\)); concrete as conductive as the soil changes nothing but the line kernel's self term.     Model uncertainty. The table rests on the owner model of the image family, one uniform current per wire. With one current per segment, all segments at one potential (the moment-method reference), encasing the braces of the house mesh in 100 Ω·m changes \(Z\) by +49.6 / +321.2 % (mom, 0.25 m segments) instead of +46.9 / +307 % at \(\rho_c\) = 1 000 / 5 000 Ω·m (medium_industry: +49.5 / +313.7 % instead of +46.2 / +298 %). The owner model is 1.6 / 2.8 % high for the mesh with bare braces (house; medium_industry 2.2 / 3.6 %) and at most 0.6 % high for the encased mesh, whose shell evens out the current over the wires. The three-dimensional model of fem (each wire its own shell, the soil meshed as a volume) gives +49.1 / +318.2 % for encasing the braces of the house mesh at \(\rho_c\) = 1 000 / 5 000 Ω·m — mom +49.6 / +321.3 % and the owner model +46.9 / +307.0 % on the same world; against it the owner model is +1.6 / +2.8 % high with bare braces and +0.14 / +0.03 % with encased ones.

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Earlier brace rules. In 0.15.0 every wire of an encased mesh had the radius \(r_b\), but the braces carried no \(C\): with "distributed" only the perimeter wires had the shell, with "lumped" (the default) no wire had it, and the lumped shell sat in World.concrete_shell_corrections, which Engine.solve of a house never read. The 2026.11 development line, between review pass 10 and the user decision, kept the braces bare (\(r_a\), no \(C\)).
    • Checks of the table. Engine.solve of the generated world and a hand assembly of its wires agree to 4e-16: the wires as separate shell-free electrodes of radius \(r_b\), their mutual matrix from solve_mutual_matrix, \(C/L_j\) added to its diagonal, the wires bonded (\(1/(\mathbf{1}^\mathsf{T} Z^{-1}\mathbf{1})\)). The assembly uses the solver's kernel and owner model, so it checks how the generator wires \(r_b\) and \(C\), not the numbers. An independent thin-wire boundary-element solution (its own line-source kernel and image series, audit D2B) reproduces every entry of the table to 0.02 percentage points.
    • Per-segment values. groundfield's per-segment assembly with the line kernel gives +49.2 / +318.5 % (medium_industry +49.2 / +312 %), and the boundary-element solution agrees with it to 0.02 points; an own point-matching solve of six hand-built wires (own segments, own kernel, Sunde's \(C\) by hand) reproduces mom on the generated mesh to \(4\cdot10^{-16}\) in \(Z\) (audit W2B-06; evidence of stream fw2mom). With the point kernel against mom the owner model is 1.5 / 2.7 % (house) and 2.1 / 3.5 % (medium_industry) high with bare braces; for the encased house mesh in 100 Ω·m it is 0.43 % high at 50 Ω·m and 0.004 % at 5 000 Ω·m. These owner-model numbers come from groundfield's per-segment assembly with the line kernel at 0.25 m segments; at 0.5 m segments it gives 0.42 %, and mom with its point kernel puts the image family's point kernel 0.38 % above it (see In the moment method).
    • The 3-D model of fem: 1.5 million nodes (evidence of stream w4fem3d); it confirms the per-segment value (+49.2 / +318.5 %) to 0.1 / 0.3 points, and mom lies 0.5 / 0.8 % and 0.2 / 0.06 % below it (re-measured after audit W4B-01, evidence of stream fw4fem3d).

    Overlapping shells at junctions¶

    Each wire carries its own radial shell; where wires meet, the shells overlap within about \(r_b\) of the junction and each wire counts the shared concrete as its own. The three-dimensional model of fem can resolve the concrete instead (FemOptions.concrete_resistivity): a square prism of conformal radius \(r_b\) around every straight encased wire, their union the concrete, on the same mesh as the per-wire shells. The square keeps the shell's resistance to the soil, but in the shell-dominated limit its concrete is 3.8 % less resistive (its inner conformal radius is 0.914 \(r_b\)), and its faces and edges converge at first order: it reads the conductance per metre 2.8 … 4.6 % high at the default levels. So the junctions are read from differences between configurations of the same length and the same free ends — a straight 4 m bar against an L of two 2 m arms (the corner), straight 4 m and 8 m bars against a T and a crossing (user decision Q40). For a 5 mm bar with 5 cm cover, 0.8 m deep in 100 Ω·m, each junction expressed as the equivalent length of wire at the default prism faces (negative where the shared concrete leaks less than the overlapping shells):

    Junction (not converged) \(\rho_c\) = 1 000 Ω·m 5 000 Ω·m
    corner +3.4 cm +5.7 cm
    crossing −5.1 cm −1.9 cm
    T-joint +0.6 cm +3.7 cm

    These numbers are not converged: one refinement of the prism faces moved the corner to −1.5 / −1.4 cm (−4.9 / −7.1 cm), the crossing and the T-joint were not refined, and the impedance form of the same runs gives −4.0 … −6.3 cm for every junction. A junction acts like a few cm of wire, at most about \(2\,r_b\), sign not settled. With each wire's own shell an L of 4 m reads 0.35 % low, and the house mesh (60 m: 4 corners, 4 T-joints, 1 crossing) changes by about 1 % or less (0.35 … 0.85 % with every sign aligned). The image family, mom and the 3-D model agree on the per-wire shell of these configurations to 0.01 … 0.3 %. No correction is applied: the effect lies below the uncertainty of the concrete resistivity and of the resolved model.

    Evidence of streams w4fem3d and fw4fem3d, after audit W4B-01. The inner conformal radius of the square prism is audit W4B-04, the unconverged junction numbers audit W4B-05.

    Against 0.15.0¶

    100 Ω·m "distributed" +2.2 % +4.2 % +6.6 % +67.9 % +399 %

    Measurements and audit trail

    The causes are findings 10.12–10.14 (the shell of "lumped"), 10.39 (the owners) and 10.28 (the line kernel). The line kernel contributes +0.78 % without a shell and +0.09 % at 5 000 Ω·m (100 Ω·m); with the point kernel on both versions the changes are +6.2 … +781 % ("lumped") and +1.5 … +398 % ("distributed") in 100 Ω·m. In a TN network the 0.15.0 lumped shell sat on the PEN service drop of the house: the default TN network with four such houses (TnNetworkGenerator, seed 0, 100 Ω·m, input impedance at the substation) moves by −3.8 / −5.2 / −6.5 / −13.8 / −10.7 % with "lumped" and by −0.3 / +1.0 / +2.5 / +34.5 / +120.9 % with "distributed" at \(\rho_c\) = 50 … 5 000 Ω·m (−1.7 % without a shell, from other findings). The 0.15.0 models rebuilt by hand in the release code reproduce the 0.15.0 numbers to round-off with the point kernel, and the boundary-element solution reproduces the changes to 0.08 percentage points.

    Owners of a rectangle without cross-braces¶

    A rectangle without cross-braces (style="ring", or "mesh" with n_x = n_y = 1) is one uniform-current owner per side: one strip per side, bonded to the local \(-y\) side (the anchor), at every orientation, bare or encased (user decision of 2026.11, Q38). concrete_model has no effect on it (see Deprecations). A building outline (polygon_xy_m) stays one closed polyline; since the user decision Q44 (with Q30) the image family gives it one owner per side as well, so a rectangular outline equals the rectangle given by its size.

    The change of the input impedance, one owner per side over one owner minus one, is never positive. The four bonded owners carry the split of the current among the sides that dissipates least (their owner matrix is symmetric and positive definite), and the single owner's split, in proportion to the side lengths, is one of those they can take; for a square the two splits coincide and nothing changes. The decrease is largest for narrow rectangles, conductive upper layers over resistive ones, and thick, wet shells. Over the 39 soils of the AP1 grid, for a 5 mm wire at 0.8 m (the generator defaults) and a short side of at least 1 m, the largest decreases found are:

    Scope aspect ratio ≤ 4, sides ≤ 80 m aspect ratio ≤ 50, sides ≤ 150 m
    bare, homogeneous soil −0.089 % (7 m × 1.75 m) −0.330 % (25 m × 1 m)
    bare or shell up to 5 cm (the default), \(\rho_c\) = 30 … 1 000 Ω·m −0.320 % (80 m × 20 m, 5 cm at 30 Ω·m, 30 / 200 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m) −0.907 % (150 m × 7.75 m, bare, same soil)
    bare or shell up to 0.2 m, \(\rho_c\) = 30 … 1 000 Ω·m −0.686 % (4 m × 1 m, 0.2 m at 30 Ω·m, 1 000 / 30 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m) −1.529 % (16 m × 1 m, same shell, \(h_1\) = 10 m)

    The field description and the generators' module docstring quote a bound with a margin over these values: per side the input impedance is lower by less than 1 % up to aspect ratio 4 (short side at least 1 m, sides up to 80 m) and 2 % up to aspect ratio 50 (short side at least 1 m, sides up to 150 m); bare or with the default 5 cm shell by less than 0.4 % and 1 %. No case of the scan gives a positive change beyond round-off (\(4\cdot10^{-16}\), for a square). Every case decreases most for the wettest concrete of the scope (30 Ω·m), and the largest cases grow with the shell thickness; drier concrete brings the two models together (−0.04 % at 5 000 Ω·m for the 16 m × 1 m case), a perfectly conducting shell separates them up to −1.66 %. A foundation at 0.5 … 1.5 m or a wire of 2.5 … 10 mm deepens the largest decreases by at most 0.10 percentage points (−0.78 % for 4 m × 1 m at 0.5 m, −0.99 % for 150 m × 7.75 m with a 10 mm wire). Below a short side of 1 m the decrease keeps growing (2 m × 0.5 m: −1.01 %, 10 m × 0.5 m: −2.22 % with the 0.2 m shell; 1.2 m × 0.3 m: −0.33 % with the default shell), which is why the scope names the short side.

    At the AP1 station (the ring of 4 m × 4 m at 0.6 m with three bonded 4 m rods) the change is −0.0009 … −0.0026 % over the 39 AP1 soils, at 2 m and at 0.5 m segments: the square ring alone would not change, the rods break its symmetry. The houses of the AP1 catalogue are building outlines and did not change by Q38; one owner per side of an outline (user decision Q44) moves the twelve outlines of the tutorial T06 layout by at most −0.004 % each and the layout with the AP1 station by −0.03 … −0.40 % (5 cm of 100 Ω·m concrete or bare, four soils).

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Before user decision Q38 the default concrete_model="lumped" made an axis-aligned rectangle — also one turned by a multiple of 90°, built as the axis-aligned rectangle with swapped sides — a single owner: one GridMeshElectrode, the same current density on all four sides; only "distributed" or a rotation by another angle split it (audit D3B-04). Until the decision the numbers of the table, as one owner over one per side minus one (0.089 … 1.553 %), bounded the difference between the two values of concrete_model. Before user decision Q44 a building outline was one owner, and a rectangular outline read like the single owner did.
    • The scan behind the table: 30 rectangles with short sides of 1 to 37.5 m and aspect ratios 1 to 50, bare and with 5 cm and 0.2 m shells, 61 values of \(\rho_c\), refined around each maximum, 13 524 owner matrices in all (audit R2B-01, re-run for the decision). Engine.solve of the generated rectangles (both styles) against one GridMeshElectrode with the same radius and shell reproduces the scan to the printed digit; the line kernel at 0.25 m or 1 m segments and the point kernel stay within the bound (−0.51 … −0.69 % and −1.38 … −1.53 % for the two largest cases).
    • The AP1 station: Engine.solve and the owner algebra of the seven wires agree to \(10^{-15}\). The outlines: evidence of stream w1sol.

    Model uncertainty. Against one current per segment, all segments at one potential (the moment-method reference), one owner per side is the closer model, which is why the decision kept it: over the whole scope one owner per side is at most 1.4 % high, the single owner up to 2.2 % (2.16 % for 16 m × 1 m), and one current per segment is never above one owner per side nor one owner per side above one owner:

    Scope (short side ≥ 1 m) aspect ratio ≤ 4, sides ≤ 80 m aspect ratio ≤ 50, sides ≤ 150 m
    bare +0.230 % (80 m × 20 m, 30 / 200 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m) +1.336 % (150 m × 3 m, same soil)
    bare or shell up to 5 cm, \(\rho_c\) = 30 … 1 000 Ω·m +0.231 % (80 m × 20 m, 5 cm of 30 Ω·m) +1.339 % (150 m × 3 m, 5 cm of 30 Ω·m)
    bare or shell up to 0.2 m, \(\rho_c\) = 30 … 1 000 Ω·m +0.253 % (6 m × 1.5 m, 0.2 m of 30 Ω·m, 1 000 / 30 Ω·m, \(h_1\) = 5 m) +1.347 % (150 m × 3 m, 0.2 m of 30 Ω·m)

    The largest values sit at the corner of the scope, the longest and narrowest rectangle (145 m × 2.9 m: +1.335 %, 125 m × 2.5 m: +1.323 %); narrower ones than the scope allows lie higher (150 m × 1 m: +1.65 %). The values for the 0.2 m shell are uncertain by about 0.2 points, because its outer radius of 0.205 m is not thin against the segments (at the default 0.5 m, segment length over wire radius is 2.4, below the thin-wire limit of 5, and the engine warns).

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Over the whole scope (audit D3B-01): 80 rectangles with short sides of 1 to 150 m, the 39 AP1 soils, bare and with 5 cm and 0.2 m shells, 61 values of \(\rho_c\), 9 360 segment matrices; segments of 0.5 m up to sides of 50 m, 1 m beyond; groundfield's per-segment assembly at 0.25 m segments. At the largest decreases of the first table one owner was 0.55 % (80 m × 20 m, bare) to 2.16 % (16 m × 1 m) high, one per side 0.23 … 0.90 % high; for the bare cases the boundary-element solution agrees to 0.001 points. 150 m × 2.9 m (aspect ratio 51.7, outside the scope) gives +1.350 % (audit R4B-06); 150 m × 1 m is audit D3B.
    • The independent boundary-element solution of audits R2B and D3B averages the potential over the wire surface. It gives the same bare and 5 cm values to 0.001 points, at the scan's segments and at half of them. For the 0.2 m shell it gives −0.594 % and −1.447 % instead of −0.686 % and −1.529 % in the first table, the same at 0.25, 0.125 and 0.083 m segments. In the second table the two solutions agree at 150 m × 3 m (+1.346 / +1.347 %); at 6 m × 1.5 m the boundary-element value grows from +0.253 % to +0.334 % when its segments are halved to 0.25 m; for the cases of the first table it lies up to 0.23 points lower at 0.25 m segments, and refining its segments to 0.083 m — shorter than the radius, which groundfield refuses — moves it by up to 0.17 points (4 m × 1 m: one owner +0.85 → +1.01 %, one per side +0.25 → +0.41 %).

    Converting between the conventions¶

    The helpers in groundfield.geometry.electrodes convert between the material description, the coefficient and a lumped resistance:

    Helper Formula Use
    concrete_shell_coefficient(rho_c, wire_radius, thickness) \(C = \frac{\rho_c}{2\pi}\ln\frac{r_a + t}{r_a}\) from concrete resistivity and cover
    shell_coefficient_from_resistance(electrode, R) \(C = R\,L\) from a lumped shell resistance of the whole electrode
    shell_resistance_from_coefficient(electrode, C=None) \(R = C/L\) the lumped resistance a coefficient represents

    \(L\) is electrode.total_length(), all wires of the electrode. A lumped resistance of a site belongs to its encased wires only — every wire of the foundation, braces included, but not the bonded rods; spreading it over the rods was the error behind the mixed-site deviations of finding 10.13. With \(\rho_c = 100\) Ω·m, 5 cm cover and a 5 mm wire, \(C = (100/2\pi)\ln 11 \approx 38.2\) Ω·m, i.e. 3.8 Ω for 10 m of encased wire.

    Validity¶

    Condition Statement
    Geometry thin shell around a thin wire, not overlapping a neighbouring wire's shell along its length (at junctions the shells share a region of the size of \(r_b\), see above)
    Current radial through the shell; the series decomposition holds below 1 kHz for foundation sizes
    Concrete homogeneous \(\rho_c\) per electrode; about 30 Ω·m (wet) to tens of kΩ·m (dry), see ADR-0012
    Field quantities the surface potential is that of the bare wire of radius \(r_b\) fed through the shell; the shell itself is a series element
    Current model against one current per segment the owner model of the image family is +0.3 … +0.9 % high for grids, at most 0.6 % for encased meshes, 1.4 % for rectangles without braces, 3.6 % for meshes with bare braces and +19.7 % for stars (table)
    Backends image, image_2layer, solve_mutual_*, mom, mom_sommerfeld and fem
