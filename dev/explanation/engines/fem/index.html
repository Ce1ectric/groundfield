    fem — conforming finite elements, axisymmetric and 3-D (experimental)¶

    This page explains how the fem engine computes the current field in the soil with finite elements, how accurate its two models are, what they cost and where they must not be used. Read it when you use fem as the reference for the integral engines (image, image_2layer, mom, mom_sommerfeld), or when you need to know why it refused a world.

    At a glance
    Soil any number of horizontal layers
    Electrodes every kind, bare or encased (ADR-0012)
    Axisymmetric model rods on one vertical axis and rings around it, meshed as the solid cylinders and tori they are
    3-D model every other world (optional fem3d extra); thin wires as smoothed line sources
    Accuracy axisymmetric about 1e-4; 3-D about 2e-3 away from layer interfaces and other wires
    Far field exact monopole Robin condition on a box 1000 lengths away
    Extrapolation Richardson over two nested mesh levels, with a reported error estimate
    Network the shared pipeline of the integral engines
    Status experimental (ExperimentalBackendWarning once per process), a reference backend

    In short

    • Two models. With FemOptions.model="auto" (the default) fem takes the axisymmetric model for any set of rods on one vertical axis and horizontal rings centred on it (a rod, a ring, a ring with a central rod) and the three-dimensional model for every other world: strips, polylines, stars, meshes and grids, rods and rings side by side, several clusters, galvanic distributed conductors. model="axisymmetric" refuses the other worlds, model="3d" forces the 3-D model.
    • Axisymmetric accuracy. The port resistance lies within about 1e-4 of independent references at the default settings, in any horizontally layered soil and at any contrast; within 5.1e-5 for a ring wire within 1.05 radii of an interface, up to a layer contrast of 1000.
    • 3-D accuracy. Within about 2e-3 of the axisymmetric model away from layer interfaces and other wires. Wires that run beside each other are kept at least \(1.5\,R\) apart (\(R\) = source_radius, 6 cm by default; a smaller \(R\) for closer wires, BackendCapabilityError where none fits). Next to a strongly contrasting interface the thin-wire error of the integral engines applies (+9.2e-3 three wire radii above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface). Layer contrasts are tested up to about 300.
    • Cost. Axisymmetric: 0.2 … 2 s for a ring, a rod or a ring with a central rod. 3-D: seconds to a few minutes and up to 4.3 GB in the measured worlds (a 12.6 m ring 26 s and 1.8 GB, the AP1 station 75 … 140 s and 4.3 GB).
    • Use it as the independent reference where the integral engines approximate: the uniform current of the image family, the point kernel of mom, the thin wire next to an interface, each wire's own concrete shell at the junctions of a foundation.
    • Do not use it for networks (by default the 3-D model takes at most 100 ports and 2 million nodes), for layer contrasts above about 300 in the 3-D model, or for wires beside each other closer than 3.3 radii of the thickest bare wire (refused). The full list is under Validity range.

    What it solves¶

    fem solves the volume form of the stationary current-flow problem: it meshes the soil around the electrodes and computes the potential in the whole soil volume, instead of integrating a Green's function over the wires as the integral engines do. Its two models share nothing with the integral engines but the network solve. That makes fem the independent reference for the image family, mom and mom_sommerfeld where they make their approximations: the uniform current of the image family, the point kernel of mom, the thin wire of both next to an interface, each wire's own concrete shell at the junctions of a foundation (ADR-0016).

    The physical model and its assumptions:

    • Quasi-static soil. The soil carries a stationary conduction current; the earth part of the solve is the same at every frequency of the engine. The conductor branches of the network (resistance, inductance, skin effect, earth return) are evaluated per frequency, as in the integral engines.
    • Horizontally layered soil. Any number of layers, each with a constant resistivity, an insulating soil surface, and the potential zero far away.
    • Electrodes are perfect conductors, each at its own potential. The axisymmetric model takes a rod, a ring and a ring with a central rod — any set of rods on one vertical axis and rings around it — as the solid bodies they are: a rod is the solid cylinder of its wire_radius with flat end faces, a ring the solid torus of its wire_radius. The current density along and around them follows from the field (more at the rod ends, more on the side of a ring that faces a conductive layer). The three-dimensional model represents every wire by a smoothed line source with an analytic correction for its radius.
    • Concrete. An encased wire carries its own thin concrete shell (the coefficient \(C\) of ADR-0012) in both models; the 3-D model can resolve the concrete as a volume instead (FemOptions.concrete_resistivity).

    The options are the fields of FemOptions:

    Option Default Meaning
    model "auto" "axisymmetric", "3d", or the automatic choice
    refinement 0 level of the coarser of the two meshes
    extrapolate True solve two levels and extrapolate
    r_far_factor 1000 size of the truncation box in characteristic lengths
    far_field "robin" monopole Robin condition; "dirichlet" grounds the box
    max_nodes 2 000 000 node limit of the axisymmetric model
    max_nodes_3d 2 000 000 node limit of the 3-D model

    growth 0.5 growth of the 3-D element size away from the wires
    concrete_resistivity None resolve the concrete as a volume (3-D)

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    The field equation, the ports, the concrete shells and the network solve hold for both models; the discretisation below is that of the axisymmetric model, and the three-dimensional model has its own section (below).

    The field equation¶

    The stationary current field obeys

    \[ -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\sigma(z)\,\nabla\varphi\bigr) = 0 \quad\text{in the soil},\qquad \frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=0} = 0,\qquad \varphi \to 0 \text{ far away}, \]

    with the potential \(\varphi\), the depth \(z\) (positive downwards) and the conductivity \(\sigma = 1/\rho\), piecewise constant in depth. The first equation is the conservation of charge for the conduction current \(\mathbf J = -\sigma\nabla\varphi\), the second says that no current crosses the soil surface into the air, and the third puts the reference at remote earth.

    For a body of revolution about a vertical axis the field is the same in every meridian plane, and the problem reduces to the meridian half-plane \((s, z)\), \(s\) the distance from the axis:

    \[ -\frac{1}{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigl(s\,\sigma\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial s}\Bigr) -\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Bigl(\sigma\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z}\Bigr) = 0 . \]

    It is the same conservation law in cylindrical coordinates; the factor \(s\) weights each point of the half-plane with the circumference \(2\pi s\) of the circle it stands for.

    Ports and the port conductance matrix¶

    Every electrode is a perfect conductor at its own potential \(U_k\) — a port. The port conductance matrix \(G\) relates the port currents to the port potentials,

    \[ \mathbf I = G\,\mathbf U , \]

    and is the earth part of the solve: symmetric (reciprocity) and positive definite (the soil dissipates power for every non-zero \(\mathbf U\)). By the minimum-energy principle the discrete \(G\) of a mesh that holds the electrodes exactly (a rod is a rectangle of mesh lines) is larger than the exact one, so its resistance lies below the exact one: the true potential minimises the dissipated power among all potentials with the port values, and the finite elements search a smaller set. A ring's O-grid inscribes a polygon in the wire's circle, so for rings this is observed, not guaranteed (see Numerics).

    Encased electrodes¶

    An encased electrode (concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m \(= C\) in Ω·m, concrete shells) keeps its metal at \(U_k\) and leaks through its shell surface \(r_b\) = wire_radius: every metre adds the radial resistance \(C\), applied locally as the surface conductance \(1/(2\pi r_b C)\) per unit area between the metal and the soil at each point of the surface. That is Sunde's cylindrical shell, exact for a shell thin against the scale on which the leakage current varies; the current may redistribute, so the shell adds at least \(C/L\) to a wire of length \(L\) (for a ring \(C/(2\pi r)\)), as in mom.

    Discretisation of the axisymmetric model¶

    • Graded tensor mesh. Vertical lines \(s = s_i\) and horizontal lines \(z = z_j\) through every feature — the rod surfaces and end faces, the layer interfaces, the soil surface, the axis and the truncation box — graded away from each feature, \(h(x) = \min_f (h_f + 0.2\,|x - x_f|)\) (\(h\) the line spacing at the coordinate \(x\), \(h_f\) the spacing at the feature \(x_f\)). The cells inside a rod are removed, so the rod is a rectangle of mesh lines. The edges of its end faces are re-entrant corners of the conductor; with a spacing of \(0.002\,a\) there (\(a\) the wire radius) the convergence stays of second order, also for a rod tip on an interface. Each cell is split into two linear triangles; the weight \(2\pi s\) is integrated exactly.
    • Coincident features. Mesh features closer than 1e-9 of max(1 m, x) are one line (\(x\) the coordinate of the feature): a run keeps the coordinate of its physical feature (an interface, a rod end, the soil surface), so only mesh lines move; an interface is cut at its own line, and a triangle of zero area is refused as an internal error.
    • O-grid for the ring wire. A square block of up to three wire radii around the wire's cross section is filled by an O-grid that conforms to the tensor lines on its boundary and is inscribed in the wire's circle (32 chords at level 0). The block keeps clear of the soil surface, the axis, the other electrodes and of every interface more than \(1.05\,a\) from the wire axis; a nearer interface — a ring on it or within its radius — is cut into the block's elements, so every element lies in one layer.
    • Ring wires next to an interface. A ring wire within \(1.05\,a\) of an interface gets a finer block: the gap between the wire and the interface, and the narrow wedge where the interface cuts the wire near its edge, make the field vary over \(\sqrt{2a\,|d - a|}\) (\(0.14\,a\) at \(d = 1.01\,a\), \(d\) the distance of the wire axis from the interface). The interface cuts the wire at an edge of the conductor on a material interface, or touches it in a contact zone that shrinks with the contrast. The O-grid of such a block is therefore graded towards the points where the interface cuts the wire at a narrow angle (0.7 a or farther from the axis) or comes closest to it — the rays geometrically in angle, the first layers geometrically in radius, from cells of 1e-3 wire radii, both bisected with the level — and the block has 32 intervals per side. The default lies within 5.1e-5 of independent boundary elements of the solid torus at contrasts 3 … 1000 on either side, cut or touching (60 cases; 3.2e-5 at 100), refinement=1 within 1.9e-5, and the error estimate (3.9e-4 … 1.5e-3) lies above the error in all 113 cases of the sweep at both refinements. A ring touching a 1000 times more conductive layer takes 38 317 / 151 807 nodes, 1.8 s and 0.76 GB (refinement=1: 604 307 nodes, 11 s, 2.8 GB). A ring on the interface (\(d = 0\)) reads −1.2e-5 against the same model.
    • Truncation. A box at r_far_factor = 1000 characteristic lengths (the electrodes' depth and radius, or the depth of the deepest interface times the layer contrast \(\max\rho/\min\rho\) if larger, since a resistive bottom keeps the current in the layers above it over that distance) with the Robin condition \(\partial_n\varphi = -(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r/r^2)\,\varphi\), the exact Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of the monopole far field on any boundary: far away the potential is that of a point source, \(\varphi \propto 1/r\) (\(r\) the distance from the electrodes, \(\mathbf n\) the outward normal of the box), for which this relation holds exactly on any surface. The graded mesh reaches the box with a few dozen extra lines.
    • Ports. The surface nodes of a bare electrode are one unknown; \(G\) is the Schur complement of the stiffness matrix onto the ports, one sparse LU factorisation for all of them.

    Network, sources and results¶

    The soil model hands an EarthPorts record (port conductance matrix, display segments with their leakage per port volt) to the network solve of the integral backends (ADR-0014), with \(Z = G^{-1}\) as the multi-port matrix. Lumped and isolated distributed conductors, inductive branches with their earth-return models (earth_inductive_model), the skin effect, junctions, return_to and voltage sources with their internal impedance are honoured exactly as there, at every frequency of the engine; the soil part is quasi-static and the same at every frequency. Floating electrodes take their transfer potential.

    The result reports every electrode's potential and current, the port resistance matrix (metadata["fem_port_resistance"], ports in world order metadata["fem_ports"], and per level), the mesh size and time per level (fem_mesh) and the box size (fem_r_far). Its point sources are the display segments of the engine's segment_length (rods cut at the interfaces) with the model's leakage distribution, so FieldResult.potential, the touch-voltage helpers and the exports work as for the other backends (homogeneous and two-layer soil). The field of those point sources is the exact field of the model's leakage, and the nodal field of the mesh converges to it (at most 0.96 %, 0.21 %, 0.062 % at levels 0, 1, 2 along the surface 1 … 100 m from a 3 m rod). Next to a rod the equipotential current distribution differs from the uniform one of the image family: the surface potential 1 … 3 m from the rod lies 0.08 … 0.36 % below that of image, the touch voltage at 1 m (per ampere) 1.4 % below. The solve runs the coordinate check (MixedCoordinatesWarning) and solves projected coordinates in a local frame.

    The three-dimensional model¶

    The three-dimensional model needs the optional fem3d extra (scikit-fem, BSD-3; pyamg, MIT; see Install the optional extras). It meshes the soil in tetrahedra and represents every wire by a smoothed line source, so the mesh never has to resolve a wire radius of a few millimetres; what the mesh does not resolve is added analytically. Layer interfaces, and resolved concrete, enter as partially penalised immersed elements instead of mesh planes.

    Wires as smoothed line sources¶

    Every electrode is a chain of straight pieces along its wire axis (a ring a polygon of 1° chords), every leaking piece of a galvanic distributed conductor one more. A piece leaks the current density \(j(s)\) in A/m (\(s\) the position along the wire), spread over the disc of radius \(R\) (FemOptions.source_radius, default 6 cm) perpendicular to the axis with the bump density

    \[ \eta(r) = \frac{3}{\pi R^2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\Bigr)^2 , \]

    \(r\) the distance from the wire axis: a smooth profile of unit integral over the disc, so the current leaves the wire as if from a soft cylinder of radius \(R\). The wire sees the \(\eta\)-weighted disc average \(\Pi(s)\) of the potential. Its field is smooth, so the mesh needs no resolution of the wire radius \(a\). What lies inside the disc is added analytically as a radial resistance per unit length between the metal and that average,

    \[ U - \Pi(s) = \bigl(C + \Delta C(s)\bigr)\,j(s), \qquad \Delta C = \frac{\rho}{4\pi}\sum_k \bigl[\Phi_k(s; a) - \Phi_k(s; r_\eta)\bigr], \]

    with the shell coefficient \(C\) of an encased wire, \(\Phi_k(s; c)\) the integral of the reduced kernel \(1/\sqrt{d^2 + c^2}\) over piece \(k\) (closed form; \(d\) the distance from the point \(s\) to the points of the piece) and \(r_\eta = 0.5442\,R\) the energy radius of the bump. The first equation is Ohm's law per metre of wire: the metal at \(U\) lies above the disc average by the current density times the shell and the soil inside the disc; the second is that soil's resistance, the potential the pieces produce on the wire surface minus what the smoothed source already gives. The sum runs over what the mesh does not resolve:

    • the observer's own wire whole: its polyline as far as it has turned by at most 90° along the wire (a ring's polygon, a strip, the corners of a loop), and every piece on the same axis line that belongs to the observer's port or to a wire that meets it — the next piece of a galvanic conductor, a rod cut into touching pieces, the surface image of a rod whose head reaches the surface;
    • of the rest of its electrode — a part of the own polyline that has turned by 150° or more along the wire (an arm folded back, the far side of a narrow loop or a small ring, the other arm of a narrow V, the next arm of a meander or the next winding of a spiral, which run the same way again after a full turn), the other wires of a mesh or a star — and of every other surface image only the part within \(1.5\,R\) of the point; between 90° and 150° of turn the own polyline counts by the weight \((150° - T)/60°\) whole and by the rest of the weight within \(1.5\,R\);
    • of a wire of another port the part within \(1.5\,R\) (on the observer's axis line the whole piece) only where the two wires meet, that is, where their surfaces touch: a rod on a ring, a T-joint, a crossing, a galvanic conductor and the electrode it is attached to. Wires that touch are one conductor and carry about the same current where they meet. Where two wires have just stopped touching, this window term fades out linearly with the gap between their surfaces, from 1 at contact to 0 at a gap of \(a_1 + a_2\), so the correction is continuous. Any other wire of another port carries its own current, which the observer's correction cannot know, and counts nothing.

    The turn \(T\) is the sum of the angles at the corners passed along the chain from the observer's piece, along a closed chain the shorter way round. The blend between 90° and 150° makes the correction continuous in the turn: a V whose turn passes 120°, a meander arm tilted by a fraction of a degree and a spiral wound as one polyline or as bonded windings all agree with mom and image (measurements in the note below).

    Each wire reaches its radius from its axis perpendicular to it, up to a flat end. Two wires that cross therefore touch where their axes come within \(a_1 + a_2\) (the window fades out between \(a_1 + a_2\) and \(2(a_1 + a_2)\)); a wire that ends at another touches it where its end face reaches the other's surface; two wires end to end on one axis touch only where the gap between their ends is zero (to 1e-9 m). An encased wire reaches its outer shell radius \(r_b\), which is what the soil sees: two encased wires of different ports whose shells touch count as one conductor in the radius correction, so their separate port entries are not resolved.

    Farther than \(1.5\,R\) the disc average of a smoothed line is its line field (the 2-D mean-value property of the radial bump), so the mesh already gives the exact field: for two parallel wires the exact correction is below \(10^{-3}\) of the self term from \(1.5\,R\) on, and the surface image of a horizontal wire (\(2z \ge 2.5\,R\) away) never counts. Far inside a straight wire \(\Delta C\) is Sunde's radial resistance \(\frac{\rho}{2\pi}\ln(r_\eta/a)\) of the soil between the wire and the disc; near an end, a corner or a junction it carries the 3-D geometry. The squared distance from a piece's axis is taken from the cross product, which stays accurate on long pieces off the coordinate axes.

    Wires beside each other¶

    The disc of a smoothed source reaches \(R\) from its axis. Where another wire runs within \(1.5\,R\) of it, the mesh no longer gives that wire's exact field and the radius correction has no term for it. The 3-D model therefore keeps wires that run beside each other at least \(1.5\,R\) apart, as it keeps every disc in the soil and in one layer: it takes \(R\) = (their axis distance) / 1.5 for the whole world (wires end to end: their gap / 5). Two wires run beside each other where

    • a point of one lies within \(1.5\,R\) of the side of the other (its nearest point is not an end of that wire) and the two run within 30° of parallel: wires side by side, concentric or diagonal rings, an arm of the same polyline folded back beside the point, a galvanic conductor in the trench of an earthing strip; or
    • the whole of one electrode's wire lies within \(1.5\,R\) of another wire it does not meet (a small ring around a rod's head); or
    • two wires that do not meet lie in line, end to end — the gap runs within 30° of both axes and within \(a_1 + a_2\) of both axis lines — with a gap below \(5\,R\): stacked rods of two electrodes.

    Wires end to end keep their gap at \(5\,R\), not \(1.5\,R\), because the measured error of their transfer entry falls with \(R\) / gap: at \(1.5\,R\) it read 1.5 … 2.0 times its estimate, at \(3\,R\) still 1.15 … 1.18 times for 8 mm rods 10 … 18 cm apart, at \(5\,R\) it lies within the estimate in every measured case. Stacked 8 mm rods up to 8.8 cm apart are therefore refused; from 10 cm on every port entry, the transfer entry included, and the bonded value lie within their estimates, and from a gap of 30 cm on \(R\) stays 6 cm.

    Mesh, layers and concrete¶

    • Isotropic mesh. A grid of \(8\times8\times4\) cubes on the truncation box, six Kuhn tetrahedra each, refined by a deterministic conforming longest-edge bisection until every element is below \(1.5\,(R/2 + g\max(0, d - R))\), \(d\) the distance from the nearest wire and \(g\) = FemOptions.growth (0.5): fine around the wires, growing linearly with the distance beyond the disc. The elements keep an aspect ratio of at most 2. (A graded tensor grid carries its fine lines to the box as slabs; the multigrid stalls on them.)
    • Layer interfaces are no mesh planes. An element that an interface cuts is an immersed element: its shape functions are linear on either side of the plane, continuous across it and carry the flux \(\sigma\,\partial_z\varphi\) across it; the faces the interface crosses get the consistency and penalty terms of the partially penalised method (Lin, Lin & Zhang 2015), so a layered field is reproduced exactly. Nodes within 30 % of their shortest edge of an interface are moved onto it. Elements four times larger than the layer they cut (far from the electrodes) take the laminate conductivity.
    • Concrete shells. By default every encased wire carries its own coefficient \(C\) — the model of the image family and mom, so fem compares with them directly. With FemOptions.concrete_resistivity \(= \rho_c\) the concrete is a volume: a square prism of conformal radius \(r_b\) = wire_radius (side \(s = r_b/0.59017\), the same resistance to the soil as a circular shell) around every straight encased wire along x, y or z, the metal radius \(r_a\) following from \(C = \frac{\rho_c}{2\pi}\ln(r_b/r_a)\), the radial resistance of a cylindrical shell. Where wires meet, the prisms share their concrete (see Concrete shells). The square keeps the resistance to the soil, but in the shell-dominated limit (\(\rho_c \gg \rho\)) its concrete is 3.8 % less resistive than the circular shell: the concrete between the bar and a nearly equipotential prism surface is set by the square's inner conformal radius at its centre, \(0.5394\,s = 0.914\,r_b\) (for a 5 mm bar with 5 cm cover \(\ln(50.3/5)/\ln(55/5) = 0.962\); Schwarz–Christoffel and a least-squares Green's function agree). One square side cannot match both radii.

    Solve¶

    The disc averages couple all nodes of a disc, so the coupling is never formed: conjugate gradients run on the operator, preconditioned by the smoothed-aggregation multigrid of the soil stiffness (pyamg, set up under a fixed seed of numpy's global random state, which is restored afterwards, so a result does not depend on the user's seed), one solve per port. The iterations grow with the layer contrast: 16 … 115 up to the contrast 33 of the AP1 soils, 384 … 530 at 100 and 1 225 … 1 264 at 333 at the finer level for a rod through the interface (both orders of the layers, each within its error estimate of the axisymmetric model). At 1 000 they do not converge in 3 000 iterations and the solve raises BackendCapabilityError naming the contrast, the axisymmetric model (any contrast) where it takes the world, and the integral engines (a multigrid of the stiffness plus the diagonal of the wire coupling gives the same counts). Without a layer contrast the refusal names its circumstances instead — resolved concrete and its contrast to the soil, or homogeneous soil, where 19 … 27 iterations are the rule.

    Numerics¶

    Both models solve two nested mesh levels and extrapolate. With the ratio \(r\) of the errors of the two levels (coarser over finer) and the error \(e_1\) of the finer level, second-order extrapolation errs by \(e_1(4 - r)/3\) and the reported estimate is \(e_1(r - 1)/3\), so the estimate bounds the error while \(r \ge 2.5\) (both levels on the same side of the converged value). At exact second order \(r = 4\): the extrapolated value is exact and the estimate equals the finer level's error.

    Axisymmetric model¶

    Refinement and extrapolation. Level \(\ell\) bisects every interval of the level-0 lines \(\ell\) times (and doubles the O-grid). The tensor part of the spaces is nested, the O-grid of a ring is not (its layers move with the level, the inscribed polygon grows): one level converges at second order and, in every case tested away from interfaces, from below — observed, not guaranteed. By default fem solves levels 0 and 1 and extrapolates \(G\) with the observed second order (Richardson),

    \[ G = G_1 + \frac{G_1 - G_0}{3} , \]

    which removes the leading error: at second order the error of level 1 is a quarter of that of level 0, so their difference is three times the error left in level 1. metadata["fem_error_estimate"] is the largest relative difference of a port's resistance between the extrapolated and the finer level — about the finer level's error (0.88 … 0.99 of it in 36 cases, 0.94 … 1.07 for a ring that an interface cuts or comes within 1.05 radii of, 60 cases) and 10 … 130 times the error of the extrapolated value, which it bounds while both levels lie on the same side of the converged value and the finer level's error is at most 1/2.5 of the coarser one's (1/3.9 observed; 1/3.9 … 1/4.0 for a ring touching a 100 or 1000 times more conductive layer). FemOptions(refinement=k) starts at level \(k\); extrapolate=False solves one level, usually below the converged value, and reports fem_error_estimate as NaN.

    Convergence. One level converges at an observed order of 1.95 … 2.00 in rods and rings, homogeneous, in two and three layers, through an interface and with a ring 1 cm above or on an interface; level 3 lies within 0.015 % of the reference, and the default extrapolation of levels 0 and 1 within 1.0e-5 … 7.0e-5 (table in the note).

    Three-dimensional model¶

    Levels and error estimates. The finer level is the red refinement of the coarser one (every tetrahedron into eight); the port conductance matrix is extrapolated with the second order, and the relative difference of a diagonal entry to the finer level is metadata["fem_error_estimate"] (3 … 5e-3 at the defaults). The levels are not asymptotic for every geometry (error ratios between the levels of 3.0 … 4.4), so the extrapolated value carries up to about 2e-3. By the rule above the estimate bounds the error while \(r \ge 2.5\): rods run at 3.3 … 4.4 and rings at 3.0 … 3.4, where the estimate is 2.1 … 4.4 times the error.

    metadata["fem_error_estimate_transfer"] is the same for the transfer resistances, the largest relative difference of an off-diagonal entry (NaN for one port or one level). Where the model holds it bounds the error of the transfer entries with a margin of 1.06 … 6.3: two 3 m rods 10 m apart 1.06 against image and 1.8 … 1.9 against mom, 30 m apart 1.7 / 1.8, two coaxial rings 4.5 … 10 cm apart 2.3 … 2.6, and 6.3 on the coaxial analogue of benchmark B8 (rod 0 … 3 m on the axis, ring \(r\) = 2.5 m at 0.8 m, 1000 / 30 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 5 m: 3.1e-3 against an error of \(R_{12}\) of −4.9e-4). In B8 itself it is 2.9e-3, with mom refined to ds = 1/32 m 1.5e-3 above fem and still falling. For two wires end to end the rule keeps the gap at \(5\,R\), where it bounds the error of \(R_{12}\) in every measured case with a margin of 1.24 … 2.6 (see Wires beside each other).

    Resolved concrete converges at first order (\(r \approx 2\)): there the estimate is no bound. Next to a strongly contrasting interface the two levels can lie on either side of the solid wire's value, and the estimate does not see the thin-wire error (see Validation of the 3-D model).

    Cost. A ring of 12.6 m (600 000 nodes at the finer level) takes 26 s and 1.8 GB, the AP1 station (28 m of wire, 1.2 … 1.4 million nodes) 75 … 140 s and 4.3 GB (fresh processes, one solve each, other jobs running). The solve adds about 2.9 GB per million nodes of the finer mesh (3.0 … 3.1 GB with the process itself), so FemOptions.max_nodes_3d (2 million nodes) refuses meshes of more than about 6 GB before they are built (ResourceLimitError). Every port costs one conjugate-gradient solve per level, about 0.9 s per port and level at 600 000 nodes, so max_ports_3d (100) limits the ports. metadata["fem_peak_memory_gb"] is what the solve added to the peak resident memory of the process — in a fresh process the memory the solve needs, less (down to zero) when an earlier computation of the process already needed more — and metadata["fem_process_peak_memory_gb"] that peak itself, the figure /usr/bin/time -l reports. Both are in GiB.

    Accuracy. The axisymmetric model reaches a port resistance within about 1e-4 at the default (within 5.1e-5 for a ring wire within 1.05 radii of an interface, up to a contrast of 1000); raise FemOptions.refinement for more. The 3-D model reaches about 2e-3 away from interfaces and other wires (wires beside each other at least \(1.5\,R\) apart), the error estimate being a bound while the ratio of the two levels' errors is at least 2.5; next to a strongly contrasting interface the thin-wire error applies (see Validation of the 3-D model). For more, take a smaller source_radius or growth with a larger max_nodes_3d (refinement multiplies its nodes by eight: the AP1 station would need about 11.5 million), or one of the integral engines as the second method.

    Guards¶

    What the dispatch and the axisymmetric model check before they solve:

    • The 3-D model instead (FemOptions.model="auto"): an electrode other than a rod or a ring; a rod or a ring off the common axis; a galvanic distributed conductor (it leaks along its route); rods that touch or overlap on the axis, a ring that touches a rod or another ring, a ring wire that reaches within 5 % of its radius to the soil surface (the solid torus must fit), a ring without room for its mesh block (within about a wire radius, diagonally, of a rod's end edge). With FemOptions.model="axisymmetric" these worlds are refused (BackendCapabilityError, naming the backends whose own checks accept the world).
    • The common axis has a tolerance of 1e-3 of the smallest wire radius or surface gap between two electrodes in the meridian plane (at least 1e-12 of the world's size). The resistance is even in the offset \(\delta\), so the axisymmetric reading is off by \(\kappa(\delta/L)^2\): \(|\kappa| \le\) 6e-4 for \(L\) the wire radius and \(\le\) 1.1e-2 for \(L\) the gap (image and mom on the same discretisations, three worlds), below 1e-6 at the tolerance; for coaxial rods end to end with a gap \(g \le a\) the overlap of the end faces changes linearly in \(|\delta|\) in the parallel-plate picture, which with \(d\ln Z/d\ln g\) = 0.02 … 0.10 estimates 6e-6 … 1.3e-5 at the tolerance: below about 1e-5 (an estimate that errs high, the offset being at most 1e-3 g).
    • Error (ValueError, before anything else): an electrode, driven or passive, at or above the soil surface, in the surface plane (\(2z \le a\)) or reaching above it, and a galvanic distributed conductor out of the soil — what every backend refuses. A rod with its head at \(z = 0\) is the ordinary driven rod.
    • Error (ResourceLimitError): the node count of the finer mesh, predicted from its lines before anything is assembled, above FemOptions.max_nodes (2 000 000 nodes, about 8 GB; level 3 of a rod, 2.4 million, needs it raised). The display segments of the point sources count against Engine.max_segments like the segments of every backend (a unit error of segment_length fails before anything is allocated).
    • The remedy of every refusal comes from the other backends' own checks: a backend that a setting of the engine would let solve the world is named with that setting (a larger Engine.max_segments, a finer segment_length), and if none is left the message says that no backend solves the world yet.

    Guards of the 3-D model¶

    • ImportError naming the fem3d extra if it is missing.
    • BackendCapabilityError before meshing: a wire so close to the soil surface or a layer interface that its disc cannot lie in the soil and in one layer; wires that run beside each other closer than 1.5 times the smallest source radius (3.3 radii of the thickest bare wire of the world, axis to axis), or lie end to end without touching closer than 5 times it (see Wires beside each other; wires that meet at less than 10° count as running beside each other), naming the wires, their distance and the integral engines; resolved concrete around a wire that is not straight along x, y or z (rings, oblique strips), whose concrete would reach the surface, or whose cover is so thin that the smoothed source does not reach beyond the metal (a 5 mm bar with 5 mm cover), naming concrete_resistivity=None. After meshing: conjugate gradients that do not converge (layer contrasts above the tested 300, such as a rod through a 1 / 1000 Ω·m interface), naming the contrast, the axisymmetric model where it takes the world, and the integral engines.
    • ResourceLimitError before anything is meshed: the display segments above Engine.max_segments (a unit error of segment_length), more ports than FemOptions.max_ports_3d (100) — every electrode and every leaking piece of a galvanic distributed conductor is a port and costs one conjugate-gradient solve per level, about 0.9 s per port and level at 600 000 nodes, so the message names the estimated time (coarsen the conductor's discretize_segment_length, or raise the limit to solve the world on purpose) — and the predicted finer mesh above FemOptions.max_nodes_3d; after the coarser mesh, its exact refinement above that limit. Where the source radius was limited by wires beside each other, the message says so.
    • ValueError: a source_radius that does not reach beyond the bare wire (it must be at least 2.2 wire radii).
    • InterfaceContactWarning (the solve goes on): a horizontal wire whose axis lies within the reach of a contrasting layer interface (3.9 wire radii at a contrast of 33; the table of the reach is under the warning). The smoothed line source shares the thin-wire error of the integral engines there (+9.2e-3 against the solid torus 3 radii above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface, beyond the error estimate), so the dispatch warns before the 3-D model runs, as the integral engines do; the axisymmetric model solves the wire as a solid torus and does not warn. A bare wire closer than about 2.8 radii to an interface is refused (its disc would not lie in one layer).

    Audit trail¶

    The history of the model and the audits behind the numbers above, kept as provenance.

    History of the model
    • Up to 2026.11 fem reduced every cluster to an equivalent hemisphere; that reduction and its rules are gone (ADR-0016). Since 2026.11 (user decision Q37) it has the two conforming models of this page: the axisymmetric model (part A) and the three-dimensional model (part B). The resolved concrete of the 3-D model, where the prisms of meeting wires share their concrete, is user decision Q40.
    • The coordinate check of the solve (MixedCoordinatesWarning) is user decision D5.
    Audit trail of the axisymmetric model
    • One-sided convergence of rings (audit W4A-09): the O-grid of a ring inscribes a polygon in the wire's circle, so the resistance below the exact one is observed for rings, not guaranteed.
    • Coincident features (audits W4R2A, W4R3A-01): before the rule that features closer than 1e-9 of max(1 m, x) are one line, a ring axis picometres off an interface read +35 … +44 % or failed.
    • Ring wires next to an interface (audits W4A-01, W4R2A-02): with the default block the levels converged at first order and not monotonically there (up to −6.0e-3 next to a layer 33 times more conductive, the error estimate below the error in 7 of 14 cases; audit W4A-01). With 64 intervals per side the default lay within 3.3e-4 at contrast 33, but not beyond it: touching a 1000 times more conductive layer it read −1.6e-2 with an estimate of 1.1e-2, the levels converged at about first order (audit W4R2A-02).
