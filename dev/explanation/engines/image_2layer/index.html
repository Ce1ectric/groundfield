    image_2layer — two-layer soil¶

    This page explains the default engine for two-layer soil: how the layer interface enters the Green's function, how electrodes in, at and through the interface are treated, how accurate the results are and which geometries next to the interface need care. Read the image page first; everything said there about the current model, the network solve and the field-grade limits applies here too.

    At a glance
    Soil TwoLayerSoil, or a MultiLayerSoil whose canonical stack has two layers; backend="image" forwards here
    Green's function Tagg / Sunde image series inside a layer; the two-layer Sommerfeld kernel of ADR-0007 once an electrode reaches the interface
    Current model one uniform leakage current per straight wire and soil layer (user decision Q30)
    Kernel exact line source (default); kernel="point" reproduces 0.15.0
    Accuracy exact for its model; the current-model error of image; thin-wire limits right at the interface
    Status default for two-layer soil

    What it solves¶

    The two-layer soil is an upper layer of resistivity \(\rho_1\) and thickness \(h_1\) over a half-space of resistivity \(\rho_2\). It is the simplest soil that captures the first-order effect of stratification — a dry or frozen top layer over wet ground, weathered soil over rock — without the identification problem of more layers, and it is the soil model of the AP1 catalogue. image_2layer solves it with the classical image series and the uniform-current model of image:

    • Soil. Two homogeneous, isotropic layers with a horizontal interface at depth \(h_1\), insulating air above; depth \(z\) counts positive downwards.
    • Quasi-static field, thin wires and average potential as for image.
    • Current model. One uniform leakage current per straight wire, and per soil layer for a wire that crosses the interface: a rod driven through \(h_1\) is one owner per layer, so its current density may differ above and below the interface.
    • Electrodes anywhere in the soil: in the top layer (image series), at or below the interface (Sommerfeld kernel).

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    Reflection at the interface¶

    The interface reflects the current field with the coefficient

    \[ K = \frac{\rho_2 - \rho_1}{\rho_2 + \rho_1} \in (-1, 1). \]

    \(K < 0\) (a conductive subsoil) draws current downwards and lowers the grounding impedance; \(K > 0\) (a resistive subsoil) pushes current into the top layer and raises it. For \(K = 0\) the soil is homogeneous.

    From the Sommerfeld integral to the image series¶

    For a point current \(I\) at depth \(z_s\) and a field point at depth \(z\) and horizontal distance \(s\), both in the top layer, the potential is the Sommerfeld integral (the static limit of Sommerfeld's half-space problem; the general stack is on the mom_sommerfeld page)

    \[ \varphi = \frac{\rho_1 I}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda\lvert z-z_s\rvert} + e^{-\lambda(z+z_s)} + K\bigl[e^{-\lambda(2h_1-\lvert z-z_s\rvert)} + e^{-\lambda(2h_1-z-z_s)}\bigr]} {1 - K\,e^{-2\lambda h_1}}\,J_0(\lambda s)\,\mathrm d\lambda , \]

    with the spatial frequency \(\lambda\) (in 1/m) and the Bessel function \(J_0\). The numerator holds the direct field, its air image and the first reflection at the interface; the denominator sums the reflections back and forth between the surface and the interface. For two layers the reflection coefficient is the constant \(K\), so three steps give a closed form:

    1. expand the denominator in a geometric series, \(1/(1 - K e^{-2\lambda h_1}) = \sum_{m\ge0} K^m e^{-2m\lambda h_1}\);
    2. multiply out: every term is \(K^n e^{-\lambda d}J_0(\lambda s)\) with a vertical distance \(d\) of \(2nh_1 \pm (z - z_s)\), \(2nh_1 + z + z_s\) or \(2nh_1 - z - z_s\);
    3. integrate each term with Lipschitz' integral \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda d}J_0(\lambda s)\,\mathrm d\lambda = 1/\sqrt{s^2+d^2}\), which is the potential of a point source at distance \(\sqrt{s^2 + d^2}\).

    The result is the image series

    \[ \varphi = \frac{\rho_1 I}{4\pi}\Biggl[\frac{1}{r_0^+} + \frac{1}{r_0^-} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} K^n\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_n^{++}} + \frac{1}{r_n^{+-}} + \frac{1}{r_n^{-+}} + \frac{1}{r_n^{--}}\Bigr)\Biggr], \qquad r_n^{\sigma\tau} = \sqrt{s^2 + (z - \sigma\,2nh_1 - \tau z_s)^2}, \]

    with \(\sigma, \tau \in \{+1, -1\}\) and \(r_0^{\pm} = \sqrt{s^2 + (z \mp z_s)^2}\): the source and its air image, plus four images of weight \(K^n\) per order \(n\) at the depths \(\pm 2nh_1 \pm z_s\) (Stefanescu 1930; Tagg 1964; Sunde 1968). Each order is one more round trip of the current field between the surface and the interface. For \(K = 0\) only the \(n = 0\) pair remains and the result is that of image bit for bit.

    Electrodes in the top layer¶

    Every segment carries the source, the air image and the image series. With the exact line kernel (default) all of them are line sources, treated like the direct pairs of the kernels page:

    • image pairs closer than six segment lengths are exact Galerkin integrals, blended into the far form up to 7.5 segment lengths;
    • beyond, the point form with the quadrupole term of the segment average, tapered off between 60 and 120 segment lengths of vertical distance, where it falls below the remainder of the far form;
    • the exact integral of an image pair takes the image's potential at the transverse distance \(\max(\rho_\perp, a)\) of the wire from the image's axis (\(\rho_\perp\) the distance of the two axes, \(a\) the wire radius): its average over the wire's circumference.

    A straight wire then reproduces its uniform-current resistance at any segment length (Numerics). The point kernel sits about 1–2 % low, as in homogeneous soil.

    Electrodes that reach the interface¶

    A rod driven through \(h_1\), or any wire at or below the interface, needs the Green's function with source and field point in different layers. As soon as one segment reaches the interface, image_2layer evaluates every pair with the two-layer Sommerfeld kernel of groundfield.coupling.layered_green (ADR-0007):

    • segments are first split at \(h_1\);
    • the reaction matrix is the homogeneous kernel scaled with the resistivity of the source layer, plus a layered correction \(\Delta Z\) evaluated by spectral quadrature, grouped by depth;
    • for a pair in different layers the direct term carries the transmitted resistivity \(2\rho_1\rho_2/(\rho_1+\rho_2)\), which keeps the matrix reciprocal;
    • with the line kernel \(\Delta Z\) is in line form as well: in two-layer soil it is an exact superposition of images in every layer combination (Sunde), and its images get the treatment of the Tagg / Sunde images above — the far quadrupole term over all pairs, the Galerkin integral for pairs within six segment lengths blended up to 7.5, the Galerkin self images on the diagonal — on top of the spectral values between midpoints.

    Physically the leakage current density of a rod through the interface scales roughly with the conductivity of the layer it is in. Such an electrode is therefore one owner per layer, all in the electrode's cluster — the same model as two rods bonded at \(h_1\): the current density may differ above and below the interface, and the reported potential is the length-weighted average over the whole electrode. Every interface cut is kept, however short the piece beyond it: the Galerkin self term and the line kernel's parallel-pair integral are evaluated without cancellation for such pieces, so the impedance is continuous in the depth of a rod tip.

    The diagonal entry of a segment is the layered correction at the wire radius, \(s = a\), with the interface image exchanged for its Galerkin integral; its closed-form \(1/R\) terms are evaluated at that radius (the default regularisation of layered_green, a clamp at \(h_1/200\), is meant for coincident points). The diagonal then equals the image solution to \(10^{-13}\), a ring is continuous through \(h_1\) (4.1826 Ω 1 µm above, 4.1805 Ω at \(h_1\)), and pairs closer than \(h_1/200\) are regularised at the 1 mm of the point forms they combine with.

    Numerics¶

    Truncation of the series¶

    The weights decay geometrically, so the neglected tail after order \(n\) is bounded by \(\lvert K\rvert^{n+1}/(1 - \lvert K\rvert)\). The series (one ImageSeries object shared by the solver, FieldResult.potential and solve_mutual_*) stops at the first \(n\) whose tail bound is below Engine.image_series_tol (default \(10^{-6}\)), or at Engine.image_max_terms (default 300) with a SeriesTruncationWarning. The number of terms depends only on \(\lvert K\rvert\), about \(\ln(\text{tol})/\ln\lvert K\rvert\); the generated table in the validation section lists it for typical contrasts — the default cap covers the AP1 corner soil 1000 / 30 Ω·m. metadata["series"] records the controls of the solve, and the post-solve field evaluation uses the same controls.

    Convergence with the segment length¶

    With the line kernel a straight wire reproduces its exact uniform-current resistance at any segment length:

    • a 10 m strip at 0.5 m in 1000 / 50 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1 m: within 3.6e-6 from 2 m to 0.125 m segments, not monotonically; 0.1 m above \(h_1\) within 3.8e-6;
    • a ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface: within 0.004 % of its exact thin ring;
    • the surface potential above the strip: within 8e-6 of the closed-form image sum;
    • a 75 m strip below a 0.5 m top layer: within 1.1e-5 at every segment length from 0.5 to 37.5 m in 500 / 100 and 1000 / 30 Ω·m (within 4.1e-6 from 1 m); over a more resistive lower layer, 100 / 500 Ω·m, +1.2e-4 at 0.5 m and +4.8e-5 at 1 m (the far terms of an image series that does not alternate);
    • a 6 m rod from 0.6 m through \(h_1\) = 5 m in 500 / 100, 1000 / 30 and 30 / 1000 Ω·m: within 6.7e-7 from 2 m to 0.125 m segments, and within 1.4e-6 of the exact two-owner value;
    • for wires in the upper layer, where both paths apply, the cross-layer path reproduces the series to 1.8e-7.

    About 2e-6 of the strip's rest is the reduced kernel that the soil-surface image keeps. The point kernel converges to a value 1–2 % low.

    Far images in point form¶

    Engine(image_far_field="point") (the author's decision of 2026-11-15) leaves out the dense far term: the far Tagg / Sunde images and the far images of the layered correction are point sources, while the near image pairs — within 7.5 segment lengths, a segment's own images included — stay Galerkin integrals blended into the point form, on the series and the cross-layer path alike. It saves the far-image work (cost on the kernels page) and against the default moves

    • networks by 3e-6 … 4.3e-4 (16 house grids 0.4 m above a 1000 / 10 Ω·m interface: 4.3e-4; 16 house grids with a 75 m bare conductor in two pieces below a 0.5 m top layer: within 4e-6),
    • single wires near the interface by up to 4e-4 and the surface potential above them by up to 0.8 %.

    A wire on the interface is up to 3e-4 apart on the two paths in this form (the default: 1.3e-6), because the far terms the point form leaves out are split differently between the two decompositions. The continuous switch and the transverse distance of near image pairs stay, so "point" is not the result of the earlier code: within the upper layer it differs from it by up to 2.3e-4. metadata["series"] records the form, the post-solve field evaluation and solve_mutual_* use it, and any value other than "quadrupole" or "point" raises ValueError.

    Cost¶

    The series path costs the image terms of every pair: their number grows with the contrast (table below). The cross-layer path costs one spectral evaluation per pair of segment depths. Large depth groups (all house grids at one depth, for example) are planned once and evaluated in row chunks, so the path needs the memory of the series path — the reaction matrix, \(16N^2\) bytes, within the budget and row blocks beyond it. The boundary-value system of the spectral amplitudes is eliminated in closed form, vectorised over the quadrature nodes. Timings: kernels and performance.

    Validation¶

    Against mom on the same segments, electrodes in the top layer agree to 0.37 % (B6) and a rod through the interface to 0.38 % (B7): the difference is the uniform-current ansatz (both run with the point kernel in that comparison). For straight wires the line kernel reproduces the exact uniform-current resistance (kernel check at the end of the generated block). The field-grade limits of the ansatz next to grids and stars are those of image. Next to the interface the thin-wire model itself has limits, measured against a solid-rod and a solid-torus model (Validity range).

    The two thin-wire limits at the interface and the point-kernel limit are explained below. What to do instead, and the other limits of the family: known limitations of the current ansatz and kernels and of the soil layers; the warnings and their reach: warnings reference.

    Rod ends at the interface: a limit of the thin-wire model¶

    The thin wire has no end face. A rod that ends exactly at \(h_1\) is a wire entirely in the upper layer; one that reaches past it by a fraction of its radius touches the lower layer through a short piece only. The solid rod is continuous in between, because its flat end face carries the contact. Against an axisymmetric finite-volume model of the solid rod (4.5 m, \(a\) = 8 mm, 1000 / 30 Ω·m) the line kernel reads:

    Tip below \(h_1\) Solid rod Thin wire
    −16 mm 190.2 Ω +3.3 %
    −8 mm 188.9 Ω +3.9 %
    −1 mm 182.8 Ω +7.2 %
    0 161.4 Ω +21.4 %
    +1 mm 156.3 Ω −4.6 %
    +4 mm 148.5 Ω −1.2 %
    +8 mm 141.2 Ω +0.7 %
    +16 mm 130.2 Ω +2.1 %

    The point kernel and mom behave alike (+19.0 … +20.5 % at 0). Every segment backend therefore warns with InterfaceContactWarning when a rod head or tip lies within one wire radius of an interface, and diagnose() lists it under interface_contact. Keep rod ends a few wire radii from an interface, or bracket the result by moving the end by ±2 wire radii; fem models the solid rod.

    Horizontal wires next to the interface¶

    A horizontal wire has the same kind of limit: next to a more conductive layer the solid wire's current crowds to its near side, which the current on the axis cannot follow. A ring (\(a\) = 5 mm) reads +1.2 % at 3 wire radii, +3.6 % at 2 and +8.6 % at 1.5 radii above a layer 33 times more conductive, against the solid torus of the axisymmetric fem. Within the reach of the interface — 3.9 radii at that contrast, tabulated under InterfaceContactWarning in the warnings reference — the solve warns with InterfaceContactWarning as well.

    Point kernel next to the interface¶

    With kernel="point" (and in the mom family, which shares the point form) a horizontal wire just below or above \(h_1\) needs segments much shorter than twice its distance to the interface:

    • 5 cm below \(h_1\), a ring of radius 3 m gives 29.7 Ω at ds = 0.5 m against 54.7 Ω at ds = 0.025 m, while the line kernel gives 55.6–55.7 Ω at every segment length (tests/test_cross_layer_line_kernel.py);
    • above \(h_1\) the point form takes the image of a segment in the interface as a point source: a ring 1 µm / 1 cm above \(h_1 = 5\) m read −1931 / −57.9 Ω (line kernel 4.18 / 15.18 Ω).

    On either side of the interface a wire closer than 0.387 segment lengths therefore raises BackendCapabilityError, and one within 0.6 segment lengths warns with PointKernelInterfaceWarning. The default line kernel needs no such care.

    Audit trail¶

    Line-source images (wave 3 of the 2026.11 work, audit W3A-02)

    Before wave 3 of the 2026.11 work, far images were point sources (which Engine.image_far_field = "point" offers in its own form today), and on the cross-layer path only the interface image of near pairs in one layer was a Galerkin integral. The reduced kernel of direct pairs, applied to images, left out \(a^2/(2d^2)\) of an image \(d\) away; audit W3A-02 replaced it by the transverse distance. Measured before against now:

    • the 10 m strip at 0.5 m in 1000 / 50 Ω·m: up to +5.1e-4 before; 0.1 m above \(h_1\): +6.8e-5 with the reduced kernel on the images;
    • the ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface: +1.60 % against its exact thin ring before (now −0.004 %); the point-kernel example ring 1 cm above \(h_1 = 5\) m: line kernel 15.42 Ω before audit W3A-02, 15.18 Ω now;
    • the surface potential above the strip: up to 0.94 % before (the alternating series of a more conductive lower layer amplifies the error of its far images);
    • the rod through the interface: 1.3e-4 … 2.4e-4 over the segment lengths before; the cross-layer path against the series in the upper layer: up to 6.2e-4 before.
    Point form of the far images (audits W3R2A-01, W3R2A-02, user decision D2)

    Up to audit W3R2A-01 the cross-layer path kept the line form of the interface image only, as before wave 3; the own surface and transmitted images of a segment below the interface were point sources, and the 75 m strip below a 0.5 m top layer read −1.0e-3 at a segment length of 2.5 m and −58 % at 37.5 m (16 house grids with a 75 m bare conductor in two pieces: +1.1 % against the default). The point form keeps the continuous switch of user decision D2, which blends up to 7.5 segment lengths where the base switched at six in one step; hence its difference of up to 2.3e-4 from the code before wave 3. Audit W3R2A-02 added the ValueError for other values of image_far_field.

    Series length and memory (findings 10.25, 10.56)

    Up to 0.15.0 the post-solve field evaluation capped the series at 100 terms (finding 10.25); it now uses the controls of the solve. Before finding 10.56 the cross-layer path needed 41 instead of 18 bytes per segment pair for 16 house grids below \(h_1\), and had no row blocks; one LAPACK solve per quadrature node took half the time of the path before the closed-form elimination.

    Rods through the interface (finding 10.15, audits A2S-01, A3S-01)

    Up to 0.15.0 one uniform density ran across the interface, which put a 12 m rod through \(h_1 = 5\) m in 1000 / 30 Ω·m soil 566 % above the per-segment mom solution (tests/test_cross_layer_owners.py); since 2026.11 the electrode is one owner per layer (finding 10.15).

    Earlier in 2026.11, cuts that would leave a piece shorter than the wire diameter were dropped, which ignored the contact with the other layer: a 4.5 m rod in 1000 / 30 Ω·m read 195.0 Ω with its tip 15.9 mm into the lower layer and 128.6 Ω at 16.1 mm (second adversarial audit, A2S-01).

    The default regularisation of layered_green clamps the closed-form \(1/R\) terms at \(h_1/200\) (25 mm for \(h_1 = 5\) m). On the diagonal it took 68–84 % of the layered correction of every piece within \(h_1/400\) of the interface: a ring of radius 3 m at \(h_1 = 5\) m read −5.29 Ω, and the contact values of a rod tip were 10–32 % low (third adversarial audit, A3S-01). The diagonal is now evaluated at the wire radius.

    Horizontal wires next to the interface (audits W4A-07, W4R2A-04)

    The ring next to a layer 33 times more conductive was measured against the solid torus of the axisymmetric fem in audit W4A-07; the reach of the interface, 3.9 radii at that contrast, comes from audit W4R2A-04.
