    Kernels: point source and exact line source¶

    This page explains how the integral engines turn the current of one wire segment into the potential at another — the kernel — and why the two kernels of groundfield differ by a systematic 1–2 % on grounding resistances. Read it when kernel="point" and kernel="line" disagree, when you compare groundfield with another program, or before you choose a segment length.

    At a glance

    point kernel line kernel
    Source of a segment its current at the midpoint its current spread uniformly along the axis
    Observation at the midpoint of the other segment (collocation) averaged over the other segment (Galerkin)
    Used by mom, mom_sommerfeld; kernel="point" of the image family the image family by default since 2026.11 (ADR-0015)
    Resistance of a straight wire converges to a value about 1–2 % low exact at any segment length
    Cost lowest about 1.1 … 5.8 times the point kernel (Cost)

    Mathematical and physical context¶

    An electrode is cut into straight segments. Segment \(j\) has the length \(L_j\), the wire radius \(a_j\), the unit tangent \(\mathbf t_j\), the midpoint \(\mathbf P_j\) and the axis \(\mathbf x_j(l')\), \(0 \le l' \le L_j\). The image family assumes that it leaks its current \(I_j\) uniformly along its axis, with the line density \(I_j/L_j\) in A/m.

    In homogeneous soil of resistivity \(\rho\) with an insulating surface, a point current \(I\) at \(\mathbf y\) produces at \(\mathbf x\) the potential \(\rho I/(4\pi)\) times \(1/\lvert\mathbf x - \mathbf y\rvert\) plus the same term for the mirror point of \(\mathbf y\) above the surface (the air image, which makes the surface current-free). Integrating the line density of segment \(j\) along its axis and averaging the result over the observing segment \(i\) gives the mean potential of segment \(i\),

    \[ \bar\varphi_i = \frac{\rho}{4\pi}\sum_j K_{ij}\,I_j , \]

    which says that the potential of every segment is a weighted sum of all segment currents; the weights \(K_{ij}\) (in 1/m) are the reaction coefficients. Exactly,

    \[ K_{ij} = \frac{1}{L_i L_j}\int_{0}^{L_i}\!\!\int_{0}^{L_j} \frac{\mathrm dl\,\mathrm dl'}{\sqrt{\lvert\mathbf x_i(l) - \mathbf x_j(l')\rvert^2 + a^2}} \quad(+\text{ the same integral with the image of segment } j), \]

    the mean over both segments of the potential that a unit current on one produces on the other. The reduced kernel \(1/\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}\) puts the source current on the wire axis and the observation point on the wire surface, a distance \(a\) away; this keeps the self term finite. For two different wires \(a^2 = \tfrac12(a_i^2 + a_j^2)\).

    Averaging the potential with the same weights that distribute the current is the Galerkin choice. It makes \(K\) symmetric (\(K_{ij} = K_{ji}\)), so every multiport matrix built from it is reciprocal, and for a straight wire the sum over all segment pairs is the double integral over the whole wire — the result does not depend on how finely the wire is cut. A kernel is an approximation of \(K_{ij}\); the two kernels below differ in how they evaluate it.

    The point kernel¶

    The point kernel (kernel="point", the only kernel of 0.15.0 and of the mom family) replaces both integrals by values at the midpoints:

    • off the diagonal the current of segment \(j\) sits at its midpoint and is observed at the midpoint of segment \(i\): \(K_{ij} \approx 1/r_{ij}\), \(r_{ij} = \lvert\mathbf P_i - \mathbf P_j\rvert\);
    • on the diagonal it takes the collocation self term \(2\ln(L_i/a_i)/L_i\) — the potential of the uniformly loaded segment at its own midpoint, \((2/L)\operatorname{arsinh}(L/2a)\), for \(L \gg a\) — plus the self image \(1/(2z_i)\) as a point image, \(z_i\) the depth of the segment.

    Why it is low. Take a straight wire cut into segments of equal length \(L\) and compare one row of the matrix with the exact Galerkin entries:

    • the segment itself: exact \(K_{ii} \approx 2[\ln(2L/a) - 1]/L\) against \(2\ln(L/a)/L\), so the point kernel over-counts by \(2(1 - \ln 2)/L = +0.61/L\);
    • each adjacent collinear segment: exact \(\frac{1}{L^2}\int_0^L\!\int_L^{2L}\frac{\mathrm dl'\,\mathrm dl}{l' - l} = 2\ln 2/L\) against \(1/L\), so the two neighbours together are under-counted by \(2(2\ln 2 - 1)/L = -0.77/L\);
    • the segment \(k\) places away: exact \([(k+1)\ln(k+1) - 2k\ln k + (k-1)\ln(k-1)]/L\) against \(1/(kL)\), a deficit that falls like \(1/k^3\) and adds about \(-0.07/L\) over both sides.

    The net row-sum error is about \(-0.23/L\). The row sum itself grows like \(2\ln(\ell/a)/L\) for a wire of total length \(\ell\), so the relative error of the resistance — the ratio of the two — depends on \(\ell/a\) and not on the segment length. The point kernel converges, but to a value about 1–2 % below the uniform-current solution, and a convergence study cannot see it. The generated kernel check on the validation page shows it for a rod, a ring and a strip at every segment length.

    Two further consequences of the point form:

    • Short segments. The collocation self term vanishes at \(L = a\) and is too small for short segments, so a short piece acts like a short circuit. The point kernel therefore takes the Galerkin self term below \(L/a = 2.990\), where both coincide (SELF_TERM_CROSSOVER; every segment with \(L/a \ge 2.990\) keeps its value bit for bit). Every interface cut is kept, however short the piece beyond it; the self term of such a piece is evaluated without cancellation.
    • Shallow segments. The self image \(1/(2z)\) is the \(L \ll 4z\) limit of the image line; for segments longer than four times their depth it is biased high (ShallowSegmentWarning).
    Measurements and audit trail

    The row-sum analysis is finding 10.28 of review pass 10. Before the Galerkin self term for short segments (finding 10.32, second adversarial audit A2S-01), a sliver at a layer interface acted as a short: mom gave a rod whose tip is 9 mm below \(h_1\) 27 % less resistance than the same rod 1 mm above it.

    The exact line kernel¶

    The line kernel (kernel="line", default of the image family) evaluates the double integral of \(K_{ij}\). Its inner integral, the potential of a uniformly loaded segment from \(A\) to \(B\) at a point \(X\) with the end-point distances \(R_1 = \lvert X - A\rvert\) and \(R_2 = \lvert X - B\rvert\), has the classical closed form

    \[ \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L \frac{\mathrm dl}{\lvert X - A - l\,\mathbf t\rvert} = \frac{1}{L}\ln\frac{R_1 + R_2 + L}{R_1 + R_2 - L}, \]

    valid for any orientation of the segment: the equipotentials of a uniformly charged segment are ellipsoids with its end points as foci. The code evaluates it without cancellation and with the reduced kernel. The outer average is computed as follows.

    Self term. With \(u = l - l'\) the double integral reduces to one integral, \(K_{ii} = \frac{2}{L^2}\int_0^L \frac{L - u}{\sqrt{u^2 + a^2}}\,\mathrm du\), which is

    \[ K_{ii} = \frac{2}{L}\Bigl[\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{L}{a} - \sqrt{1 + (a/L)^2} + \frac{a}{L}\Bigr], \]

    the mean potential of the segment on its own surface per unit current; for \(L \gg a\) it tends to \(2[\ln(2L/a) - 1]/L\).

    Near pairs — midpoint distance below six times the longer segment (NEAR_FACTOR) — are integrated exactly. Parallel pairs have a closed form (the integral of Neumann's mutual-inductance formula for parallel filaments); touching or crossing pairs use Gauss–Legendre points graded towards the point of closest approach with the exact inner line potential; separated skew pairs a plain eight-point rule.

    Far pairs use the expansion of the segment average. Write a point of segment \(k\) as \(\mathbf P_k + s\,\mathbf t_k\) with \(-L_k/2 \le s \le L_k/2\) and expand \(1/\lvert\mathbf R + s\,\mathbf t\rvert\) to second order in \(s\), \(\mathbf R = \mathbf P_i - \mathbf P_j\), \(R = \lvert\mathbf R\rvert\). The linear term averages to zero and the mean of \(s^2\) is \(L_k^2/12\), which leaves

    \[ \frac{1}{R} + \frac{L_i^2(3c_i^2 - 1) + L_j^2(3c_j^2 - 1) - 12a^2}{24R^3}, \qquad c_k = \frac{\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf t_k}{R}, \]

    the point form \(1/R\), the quadrupole term of each segment's length (\(c_k\) is the cosine between the segment and the line joining the midpoints) and the radius term \(-a^2/(2R^3)\) of \(1/\sqrt{R^2 + a^2}\). The remainder is of order \((L/R)^4\).

    The switch between exact and far form is a blend (user decision D2): pairs between six and 7.5 segment lengths (SWITCH_FACTOR) take \(w\,K_\text{exact} + (1 - w)\,K_\text{far}\), the weight \(w\) falling from 1 to 0 with zero slope at both ends (smoothstep in \(R^2\)). The result is continuous in the geometry: it spreads by at most 1e-13 over local offsets of 0.1 m … 8.8 km, and the solve at UTM coordinates lies as close to the local one as with the point kernel (3e-11 in \(Z\)).

    Images. The air image is a mirrored segment and is treated like a source segment. The Tagg / Sunde images of a two-layer soil lie at least \(2(h_1 - z)\) away (\(h_1\) the thickness of the top layer); they get the treatment of direct pairs — exact near, blended, far form with the quadrupole term — with two differences:

    • Transverse distance. An image line that passes the wire at a transverse distance \(\rho_\perp\) acts with its potential averaged over the wire's circumference. By the two-dimensional mean-value property — the average of \(\ln\lvert x - y\rvert\) over a circle of radius \(a\) around \(x\) is \(\ln\max(\rho, a)\), \(\rho = \lvert x - y\rvert\) — that average is its value on the axis for \(\rho_\perp \ge a\). The exact integral of an image pair therefore uses \(\max(\rho_\perp, a)\) in place of \(\sqrt{\rho_\perp^2 + a^2}\), which keeps the reduced kernel for a collinear image (\(\rho_\perp = 0\), a rod against its own image), and the far form of images has no radius term. A ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface then lies within −0.004 % of its exact thin ring.
    • Reach of the quadrupole term. The quadrupole term of an image is tapered off between 60 and 120 segment lengths of vertical distance between observer and image (IMAGE_QUADRUPOLE_REACH). At 60 segment lengths it is at most \(1/(6\cdot 60^2)\) of the image's point value (\(1/(12\cdot 60^2)\) for horizontal wires), of the order of the far form's remainder at the switch: the image series of a high contrast has hundreds of images, of which few are close. An image is evaluated only on the pairs whose longer segment reaches it, and in the reaction matrix, which is symmetric, on its upper half.

    Engine.image_far_field = "point" keeps far images as point sources (see Cost). On the cross-layer path of image_2layer the layered correction is the exact image expansion of the two-layer Green's function (Sunde) in every layer combination, and its images get the same treatment on top of the spectral point values; pairs in different layers carry the transmitted resistivity \(2\rho_1\rho_2/(\rho_1+\rho_2)\), which keeps the matrix reciprocal.

    Measurements and audit trail
    • Radius term of the far form. Up to wave 3 of the 2026.11 work the far form had no radius term: it was \(a^2/(2R^2)\) — 1.4e-6 at six segment lengths of 0.5 m — away from the exact pairs it joins.
    • One-step switch. Before user decision D2 the form switched at six segment lengths in one step — where the pairs six segments apart on a straight wire sit exactly. Round-off of the coordinates decided their form, and the same world at different positions spread by up to 4.4e-7 in the impedance (a 12 m strip) and, in two-layer soil with an image distance of six segment lengths, by 1e-5 … 2.6e-5 (audit W2B-01/-02).
    • Far Tagg / Sunde images. Up to wave 3 of the 2026.11 work they were point sources beyond six segment lengths, which left a straight strip in two-layer soil up to 5e-4 off and the potential above it up to 1 % off.
    • Transverse distance (audit W3A-02). The reduced kernel on images left out \(a^2/(2d^2)\) of an image \(d\) away, so a ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface read +1.60 % against its exact thin ring.
    • Reach of the far-image work (audit W3A-01). Before, a few long pieces among short segments extended the reach of every pair.
    • Bound of the tapered term. Earlier texts gave \(1/(8\cdot 60^2)\) of the image's point value at the start of the taper, which holds for horizontal wires; the largest size, reached for a vertical wire and its collinear image, is \(1/(6\cdot 60^2)\) (the bound \(\lvert w\rvert(L^2 + 3a^2)/(6\,\mathrm dz^2)\) that the code uses to skip images, IMAGE_QUADRUPOLE_TOL).

    Accuracy of the line kernel¶

    For a straight wire the line kernel is exact at any segment length, in homogeneous and in two-layer soil, up to the \((L/R)^4\) remainder of the far form and the reduced kernel that the soil-surface image keeps: a 10 m strip in 1000 / 50 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1 m is within 3.6e-6 of its closed-form uniform-current resistance (images axis to axis) for segment lengths of 0.125 … 2 m, not monotonically (the same strip in homogeneous soil: within 2.1e-6), and a rod through the layer interface is within 1.4e-6 of its exact two-owner value at every segment length. A ring converges like the square of the segment length, because its chords approximate the circle.

    What remains against the true thin-wire solution is the uniform-current ansatz itself: for the 3 m rod of benchmark B2 the line kernel sits about +0.9 % above the thin-wire value at every segment length (generated numbers on the validation page). The point kernel's −1 … −2 % kernel error partly cancels that ansatz error on impedances, which is why the old numbers looked closer to some references than they were.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The strip and rod numbers are those of audit W3A (4 soils, 3 rods).

    Field points¶

    After the solve, FieldResult.potential evaluates the field with the kernel of the solve. With the line kernel that is the exact line potential near a segment and the point form plus the quadrupole term \(L_j^2(3c_j^2 - 1)/(24R^3)\) farther away, blended like the reaction matrix and for the Tagg/Sunde images as well (sources in the lower layer of a two-layer soil are evaluated in point form by the spectral kernel). Every PointSource of a line-kernel result carries its unit tangent (direction), so a stored result is re-evaluated with line sources; results written by 0.15.0 have no direction and are evaluated with the point kernel.

    Near a wire the difference is large: on the surface of a rod discretised at 0.5 m the point form spikes at every segment midpoint (16 … 177 V around an electrode potential of 32 V), while the line form is smooth and its surface average equals the electrode potential (tests/test_line_kernel.py::test_line_potential_is_equipotential_on_the_wire_surface). Surface potentials next to a wire — touch and step voltages — therefore need the line kernel.

    Measurements and audit trail

    The spikes of the point form on the wire surface are finding 10.33 of review pass 10.

    Cost¶

    The exact near pairs and the quadrupole far field over all \(N^2\) pairs make the line kernel more expensive than the point kernel. A solve of 16 house grids (\(N\) = 1920 segments) takes 3.8 times as long as with the point kernel in homogeneous soil and 1.6 times in two-layer soil (\(h_1\) = 5 m) with numba installed (it is used automatically, extra fast), 5.8 and 1.4 times without; the extra time grows with \(N^2\). The blended switch adds the integrals of the pairs between six and 7.5 segment lengths, and in two-layer soil every image within reach carries the far-field term, evaluated only on the pairs whose longer segment reaches it and, in the reaction matrix, on its upper half, with one division per evaluation. A 40-house network in 1000 / 30 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1.5 m (82 images within reach) takes 1.8 times the solve before wave 3 of the 2026.11 work with numba and 3.6 times without.

    Engine.image_far_field = "point" keeps far images as point sources and leaves out their far-field term; the near image pairs stay Galerkin integrals. It takes 1.1 … 1.3 times the solve before wave 3 on house grids within the upper layer. The 40-house network solves on the cross-layer path (its transformer rods reach below \(h_1\)): 0.91 times the solve before wave 3 and 0.49 times the default with numba. Below thin top layers the near image pairs remain, 1.5 … 2.4 times the solve before wave 3 (0.51 … 0.74 times the default). The performance page has the table. kernel="point" remains available and reproduces the numbers of 0.15.0.

    Measurements and audit trail

    Audit W3A-01 measured the far-image work before its restriction to the pairs that reach an image: a few long pieces among short segments multiplied the work by 5.6, and the 40-house network in 1000 / 30 Ω·m took 4.2 times the solve before wave 3 with numba and 9.7 times without. The near image pairs of "point" are those of audit W3R2A-01.

    Where each kernel is used¶

    Component Kernels Default
    image, image_2layer (and the deprecated names cim, image_nlayer) line, point line
    mom, mom_sommerfeld (and bem) point point
    solve_mutual_matrix, solve_mutual_field line, point the engine's kernel
    FieldResult.potential the kernel of the solve (metadata["kernel"]) —
    fem none (volume mesh; the 3-D model has its own smoothed line source) —

    metadata["kernel"] records the kernel of every result, and metadata["provenance"]["engine"] the request, so a result can be reproduced exactly.
