    Known limitations¶

    This page lists what groundfield does not do, does only approximately, or does only with certain engines, with the size of the effect where it has been measured. Read the entries of your engine and topic before you rely on a number near one of these limits.

    Each entry says what the limit is, how large its effect is, and what to do instead. Measurement lists, audit references and history are collapsed under Details and audit trail. The numbers are recorded in the docstrings of the modules named in each entry (they are the authority and are updated with the code) and in the changelog of the release that measured them. The validation page shows what agrees with independent references and how well, the warnings reference explains every warning named here, and diagnose() reports most geometric limits before a solve.

    Scope of the physics¶

    • Quasi-static, below 1 kHz. Displacement currents in the soil and wave propagation along conductors are neglected; impulse and lightning transients are out of scope.

      How large: the ratio of displacement to conduction current, \(\omega\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\rho\), is below \(10^{-2}\) up to 1 kHz for \(\rho \le 1000\,\Omega\,\mathrm{m}\) and \(\varepsilon_r = 81\), but reaches about \(4.5\cdot10^{-2}\) in rock of 10 kΩ·m (Soil).

      Instead: stay below 1 kHz. Higher frequencies are accepted without a warning but are outside the validated range.

    • Linear, isotropic, horizontally layered soil. No soil ionisation, no lateral changes of resistivity, no anisotropy, no frequency dependence of \(\rho\); soil and metal are non-magnetic.

    • No capacitive coupling between conductors (negligible below 1 kHz for buried conductors).

    • Voltage sources. The internal impedance of a VoltageSource is a lumped \(R + j\omega L\) with constant \(R\) and \(L\). Voltage sources without internal resistance that form a loop (through ideal bonds or remote earth) are rejected, also when one of them has an inductance (it vanishes at \(f = 0\); World.check does not know the frequencies).

      Instead: for a frequency-dependent source impedance, solve once per frequency with its values.

    • Not computed: the inductive reduction factor of cable screens and earth wires; mutual impedances (BranchType) for groundinsight — one BusType describes one terminal.

    Coordinates¶

    • Projected and local coordinates in one world. Projected coordinates are solved in an exactly shifted local frame beyond 10 km from the origin. A world that mixes projected and local coordinates — points within 10 km of the origin and beyond 1000 km from it, typically a return electrode, a cabinet or a cable end entered in a local system in a UTM world — gains nothing from the frame and is solved as given: its two groups lie some 33 000 km apart and a conductor between them is that long.

      How large: a return rod at local (80, 15) m for an outline at UTM coordinates: Z_in +0.43 %; a PEN cable to a cabinet at local coordinates: a 33 118 km conductor, 7494 + j25396 Ω instead of 5.39 + j0.19 Ω with carson_series, and sommerfeld does not finish.

      Instead: give every point in one coordinate system. The solve warns with MixedCoordinatesWarning and diagnose() reports it under coordinates. Field and probe points are never checked.

      Details and audit trail

      Its round-off is that of the UTM world (1.5e-11 instead of 1.0e-11, point kernel). Since 2026.11 the solve warns with MixedCoordinatesWarning and diagnose() reports it under coordinates (user decision D5). A world wholly in projected or wholly in local coordinates is never warned about at the solve; diagnose() reports one wider than 100 km as information. The rule is a point within 10 km of the origin together with one beyond 1000 km: a stray point between 10 and 1000 km, a northing missing a digit, an easting without its zone prefix or a position given in km are silent at the solve (diagnose() reports them as information through their extent, 5 231 / 32 000 / 33 085 km for a rod beside one at UTM coordinates), and field and probe points (FieldResult.potential(points), touch_voltage(position=…), step_voltage, the probe and field points of solve_mutual_*) are never checked (a potential at local (1, 0, 0) m of a UTM world is 4.8e-7 V, without a warning; audit W2R2-11). The measurements of the mixed world are those of audit W2B-04.

    • Straight runs of a polyline at UTM coordinates. The straight runs of a polyline (one owner per run) are found with a round-off allowance of 64 units of the coordinates, 0.46 µm at a UTM easting, and are grown greedily from the first vertex.

      How large: an arc whose vertices lie closer than that to the chord of their neighbours (R = 50 m in 6 mm edges) gets one owner per two edges instead of one per edge: +5.5e-4 in Z against the same arc at local coordinates. The same arc given in reverse order has as many wires, in another partition, and its impedance differs by 2.4e-8 (closed) / 4.9e-8 (open).

      Instead: for such finely divided arcs give the whole world in local coordinates (the reverse-order difference is 4e-15 there).

      Details and audit trail

      The partition depends on the vertex order only where vertices lie within the allowance off the chord (audit W1R3-06).

    Current ansatz and source kernels¶

    • Uniform current per straight wire (image family). image and image_2layer assume a uniform current per unit length on each straight wire of an electrode (and soil layer): a full wire line of a grid, an arm of a star, a straight run of a polyline; rods, strips and rings are one wire. Impedances are close to the per-segment mom solution; surface potentials next to grid corners, star tips, strip ends and bonded multi-electrode clusters are not, because the real current crowds there.

      How large: impedances of rods and rings within about 1 % of the per-segment mom solution, grids +0.36 … +0.70 %, stars +1.8 % (3 arms) … +10.5 % (8 arms of 10 m). Touch / step voltage next to a 20 m × 20 m grid corner +6.4 % / −7.6 %, beyond a 4-arm star tip +8 % / −9 %, between two star arms touch −17 % and step +40 %. Single rings, rods and closed loops are within 2 %, but a bonded cluster is not: a bare ring bonded 1 m outside a house's foundation mesh reads the touch voltage +5 … +16 % above mom with the feet right above the ring (the foundation alone within 2 %). Two-layer soil can make the grid figures much larger, most of all over a resistive bottom: a 60 m × 40 m grid with 5 m meshes in 50 over 300 Ω·m reads the touch voltage 1 m outside +12.5 %, at the mesh centres +45 % and the step voltage at a corner −4.7 % (the unsafe side); in 300 over 50 Ω·m +2.1 %, +7.1 % and +4.5 % (case study E03).

      Instead: for field values near such geometries solve with backend="mom" (or compare both); the safety functions warn with UniformCurrentFieldWarning. For impedances, rho-f samples and far-field potentials the image family is the right tool.

      Details and audit trail

      The current model is that of user decisions Q30 and Q44. Impedances of rods and rings are within about 1 % of the per-segment mom solution, grids +0.36 … +0.70 % above one current per segment with the default line kernel (10 m 2 × 2 … 40 m 8 × 8; +0.43 … +0.89 % in 5 cm of wet concrete; against mom with the point kernel +0.29 … +0.66 %, +0.35 … +0.69 % in the same concrete), stars a few per cent above it, growing with the number of arms (+1.8 % for 3, +3.5 % for 4, +10.5 % for 8 arms of 10 m in 100 Ω·m; up to +12.6 % in 1000 over 100 Ω·m and +19.7 % in wet concrete; against mom, point kernel, +1.4 … +11.8 % bare, up to +18.8 % encased): the current crowds to the tips, which one current per arm cannot represent.

      Rectangles without cross-braces, given by their size or as an outline (one owner per side), with a short side of at least 1 m, an aspect ratio up to 50 and sides up to 150 m are at most 1.4 % high over the AP1 soils, bare or encased (150 m × 3 m bare in 30 over 200 Ω·m: +1.34 %; 0.9 % for 150 m × 7.75 m; at most 0.25 % up to aspect ratio 4); narrower ones more (150 m × 1 m: +1.65 %). For shells as thick as 0.2 m these numbers carry about 0.2 percentage points of model uncertainty.

      Surface potentials near grids, stars, strip ends and bonded multi-electrode clusters are worse. Against the per-segment mom solution, with the default line kernel, touch voltage with the feet 1 m outside the footprint and step voltage from 1 m to 2 m outside: a 20 m × 20 m grid with 4 × 4 meshes at a corner +6.4 % / −7.6 % (the step from the point above the corner 1 m outwards −11 %, the step at the middle of an edge +5.8 %); a 4-arm star with 5 m arms beyond a tip +8 % / −9 % (between two arms touch −17 %, step +40 %); rings, rods and closed loops within 2 % (the point kernel isolates the ansatz: +5 % / −8 %, +7 % / −10 %, within 1 %; groundfield.postprocess.safety).

      The 2 % hold for single electrodes. Example E02 (stream docex2, the executed notebook docs/examples/E02_single_family_house.ipynb): the 10 m × 10 m mesh foundation of the generators' residential house (2 × 2 cells, 0.8 m deep, 5 mm bar in 5 cm of 150 Ω·m concrete) with a bare ring 1 m outside, bonded; the largest touch voltage of touch_voltage_envelope 1 m from the bars (72 directions, the feet right above the ring) per ampere, image against mom at 0.5 m segments: +9.4 % in 100 Ω·m, +5.3 % in 300 over 30 Ω·m and +15.7 % in 50 over 500 Ω·m (\(h_1\) = 2 m); the foundation alone +1.2 … +2.0 %. The image family is high there (the safe side), and UniformCurrentFieldWarning fires for the bonded cluster.

    • Point kernel (kernel="point", and always in mom and mom_sommerfeld). It places each segment's current at its midpoint; its row sums carry an error of about \(-0.23/L\), which no refinement removes.

      How large: grounding resistances converge 0.9–1.9 % below the exact uniform-current value (Engine.kernel). Horizontal wires close to the layer interface need segments much shorter than \(2\lvert z - h_1\rvert\): a ring 5 cm below the interface of 100 over 1000 Ω·m reads −46 % at 0.5 m segments.

      Instead: use the line kernel (the default of the image family) or shorter segments. A horizontal point-kernel segment within 0.6 segment lengths of the interface warns with PointKernelInterfaceWarning; one closer than 0.387 segment lengths raises BackendCapabilityError.

      Details and audit trail

      The ring 5 cm below the interface is documented in changelog 2026.11.0, finding 10.28. A horizontal point-kernel segment within 0.6 segment lengths of the interface, on either side of it, warns with PointKernelInterfaceWarning (3–17 % low in the measured cases with an electrode through the interface, −2.5 % for a ring 0.2 m above it); one closer than 0.387 segment lengths, or one whose three-point rule would reach above the surface, raises BackendCapabilityError (a ring 1 µm above the interface read −1931 Ω instead of 4.18 Ω before).

    • Line kernel in two-layer soil. The exact line kernel leaves three small remainders in two-layer soil: the ends of a finite wire next to the interface, the reduced kernel of direct pairs of different wires, and the form of far images.

      How large: a 2 m strip 5 mm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface reads +3.0e-4 against the circumference average, a 10 m strip +5.5e-5; two strips 1 cm above that interface, 5 cm apart, −3.5e-4 in their mutual resistance. With Engine.image_far_field = "point" a wire on the interface is up to 3.0e-4 apart between the two paths (the default: 1.3e-6).

      Instead: keep the default Engine.image_far_field. With an electrode through the interface, give a galvanic distributed conductor sub-pieces of the order of \(h_1\).

      Details and audit trail

      Image pairs take the image's potential at the transverse distance of the wire from the image's axis (audit W3A-02). That is its average over the wire's circumference for a long wire; the ends of a finite one add a remainder of order a²/(d·ℓ) (audit W3R2A-05): a 2 m strip 5 mm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface reads +3.0e-4 against the circumference average, a 10 m strip +5.5e-5; rings have no ends.

      A ring 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface is within 0.004 % of its exact thin ring. A straight 10 m strip in 1000 / 50 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1 m is within 3.6e-6 of its uniform-current resistance for segment lengths of 0.125 … 2 m, not monotonically. A rod through the interface is within 1.4e-6 of its exact two-owner value.

      Direct pairs of different wires and the soil-surface image keep the reduced kernel of the direct term (audit W3R2A-06). Two strips 1 cm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface, 5 cm apart, read −3.5e-4 against their mutual resistance with the circumference average of the direct term too. A house grid at 0.8 m is 4e-7 lower than with the transverse distance on the surface image, at 0.1 m 1.7e-5.

      Far images carry the quadrupole term up to a vertical distance of 120 segment lengths (tapered from 60) and where it exceeds 1e-9 of a unit point term. With Engine.image_far_field = "point" they stay point sources and their near pairs stay Galerkin integrals. In that form a wire on the interface is up to 3.0e-4 apart between the series and the cross-layer path (the default: 1.3e-6), and a rod through the interface depends on the segment length by up to 6e-5 (ds 1 … 0.25 m; the default 4e-7).

      The field of sources in the lower layer (FieldResult.potential, solve_mutual_field) is evaluated in point form by the spectral kernel. With an electrode through the interface, a galvanic distributed conductor needs sub-pieces of the order of \(h_1\) (changelog 2026.11.0, finding 10.24).

    • Cost of the line kernel. The near pairs and the quadrupole far field over all pairs make the line kernel more expensive than the point kernel; the extra work grows like \(N^2\).

      How large: for 16 house grids (\(N\) = 1920) 3.8 times the solve time of the point kernel in homogeneous soil and 1.6 times in two-layer soil (\(h_1\) = 5 m) with numba, 5.8 and 1.4 times without (Engine.kernel).

      Instead: install numba (extra fast); for large two-layer studies Engine.image_far_field = "point" takes 0.49 … 0.94 of the default's time; kernel="point" reproduces the numbers of 0.15.0. See performance.

      Details and audit trail

      In two-layer soil the far images cost with the image density: a 40-house network in 1000 / 30 Ω·m with \(h_1\) = 1.5 m takes 1.8 times the solve before wave 3 (3.6 times without numba), 16 house grids below a 0.1 m top layer 4 times, and with eight 9.4 m pieces below a 0.5 m top layer 5 times (their near image pairs are Galerkin integrals). Engine.image_far_field = "point": 1.1 … 1.3 times within the upper layer; on the cross-layer path its near image pairs stay Galerkin integrals (audit W3R2A-01): 0.7 … 0.9 times at the AP1 station and the 40-house network, 1.5 … 2.4 times below thin top layers, 4.3 … 4.5 times with long pieces there — 0.49 … 0.94 of the default's time.

    • Line-kernel switch. Pairs closer than six segment lengths are integrated exactly, pairs beyond 7.5 use the far form, and between the two the values are blended.

      How large: results of the same world at different positions agree to 8e-14 over local shifts of 0.1 m … 8.8 km and to 5e-11 (impedance) at UTM coordinates — the rounding of the input vertices, as with the point kernel. Nothing to do.

      Details and audit trail

      The blend is user decision D2.

    Soil layers¶

    • Three or more layers are solved only by mom_sommerfeld (and fem). FieldResult.potential — and with it touch and step voltages, potential plots, potential-path CSV and the VTK field export — supports homogeneous and two-layer soil only and raises NotImplementedError for three or more layers.

      Instead: read impedances, electrode potentials and currents of such a solve from the result; only the field evaluation after the solve is missing.

    • Rod ends at a layer interface. The thin-wire model has no end face, so the contact of a rod end with the layer beyond is all or nothing.

      How large: against the conforming fem model and a finite-volume model of the solid rod (4.5 m, \(a\) = 8 mm, 1000 / 30 Ω·m) the thin wire reads +22 % with its tip at the interface, +7.2 % 1 mm above it and −4.4 % 1 mm below it (+3.3 % / +2.1 % 16 mm above / below).

      Instead: keep rod ends a few wire radii from an interface, or bracket the result by moving the end by ±2 wire radii; the axisymmetric fem solves the solid rod. A rod head or tip within one wire radius of an interface warns with InterfaceContactWarning and is listed by diagnose() (interface_contact).

    • Wires within one radius of the layer interface. A wire at least one wire radius from the interface is integrated exactly by the line kernel. Closer, its surface crosses the interface and the thin-wire model does not apply.

      How large: a ring 5 mm above a 1000 / 30 Ω·m interface is within 4.1e-5 of its exact thin ring (two references). At half a radius the two references (image sum, Hankel transform) differ by 8 %, and the line kernel lies 2 … 11 % below them.

      Instead: solve a ring that touches or crosses an interface with the axisymmetric fem (solid torus). Such wires lie within the reach of the next entry, so the solve warns with InterfaceContactWarning wherever the contrast is at least 1.3; kernel="point" keeps its own warning for wires within two segment lengths of the interface.

      Details and audit trail

      At half a radius the line kernel read +65 % before audit W3A-02. Within a/20 of the interface the image blends into the reduced kernel of the direct pair, so a wire on the interface is the same on both sides (4.1806 Ω for a 3 m ring at \(h_1\) = 5 m). Through ±a the impedance moves with wiggles of at most 0.17 % against its direction (strips and rings in 1000 / 30 and 30 / 1000 Ω·m; up to 24 % with the blend over one radius before audit W3R2A-03).

      The horizontal-wire rule of InterfaceContactWarning (audits W4A-07, W4R2A-04, W4R3A-02) covers every wire that the interface cuts at a contrast of 1.3 or more.

    • A horizontal wire within a few wire radii of an interface. The thin-wire engines (the image family, mom, mom_sommerfeld) and the 3-D fem carry the current on the wire axis; next to a more conductive layer the solid wire's current crowds to its near side.

      How large: against the solid torus a ring of \(a\) = 5 mm over a layer 33 times more conductive reads +0.59 % at 4 radii, +1.2 % at 3, +3.6 % at 2, +8.6 % at 1.5, +32 % at 1.1 and +89 % when the interface cuts the wire at 0.99 radii; under a more resistive layer up to +2.6 %. The 3-D fem reads +9.2e-3 for a ring with its axis 1.5 cm above 1000 / 30 Ω·m, and a rod tip on such an interface +43 %.

      Instead: keep horizontal wires outside the reach below, or solve rings with the axisymmetric fem, which takes them as solid tori (within 5.1e-5 of a boundary-element model down to contact, up to a contrast of 1000). Within the reach the solve warns with InterfaceContactWarning (diagnose() category interface_contact).

      Reach of the interface, wire axis to interface, in wire radii \(a\) (values in brackets interpolated; the warning holds the last value beyond 1000):

      contrast more conductive beyond more resistive beyond
      1.4 1.0 a 1.0 a
      2 1.3 a (1.14 a)
      3 1.9 a 1.3 a
      5.5 2.7 a (1.60 a)
      10 3.3 a 1.9 a
      18 3.7 a (2.05 a)
      33 3.9 a 2.2 a
      100 4.1 a 2.2 a
      1000 4.3 a 2.3 a

      (1.0 a: only a wire the interface cuts; below a contrast of 1.3 nothing is reported, a cut wire is within 0.96 % there.) Measured on rings with \(r_0/a \ge 18\); a thicker wire on a smaller ring, and straight wires against a solid reference, are not covered. No generated network and no world of the AP1 soils is reported.

      Details and audit trail

      The solid torus is the axisymmetric fem; a Hankel thin ring plus the exact 2-D proximity correction agrees with it to 5e-4. The +2.6 % under a more resistive layer is a 55 mm wire on a 1 m ring at 1.01 radii, contrast 1000. The 3-D fem is a thin wire too: its +9.2e-3 for the ring (\(r\) = 1 m) at 1.5 cm lies above its error estimate. The reach is the largest distance at which the thin wire is 1 % off in five ring geometries (\(r_0/a\) = 18 … 400, the layer beyond below and above the wire), rounded up to 0.1 a, plus 0.1 a between the nodes; no measured point 1 % or more off lies outside it (the thin wire converged in its segment length). The full measurement is under InterfaceContactWarning. Audits W4A-07, W4B-02, W4R2A-03, W4R2A-04, W4R3A-02.

    • High layer contrast. The Tagg/Sunde series converges like \(\lvert K\rvert^n\); the default cap of 300 terms covers \(\lvert K\rvert = 0.94\) and warns (SeriesTruncationWarning) beyond. mom_sommerfeld may not converge for \(\lvert K\rvert \to 1\) (SommerfeldConvergenceWarning).

      Instead: raise Engine.image_max_terms; use image_2layer for two layers.

    • Effective resistivity (effective_resistivity, the rho of a BusType in layered soil) is an empirical calibration on the LV networks of the AP1 catalogue (\(h_1\) = 5 … 30 m, \(\rho_1\) = 30 … 1000 Ω·m, \(\rho_2\) = 30 … 200 Ω·m), defined for homogeneous and two-layer soil only (three distinct layers raise ValueError).

      How large: its weight is limited to \([0, 1]\), so an upper layer thinner than 0.28 … 1.34 m, depending on the preset (0.78 m with the default), gives \(\rho_2\). A preset holds for its class only (the AP1 station with "lv_group_4_p40": rms 39.9 %); even "station" leaves up to 15.7 % at \(h_1\) = 5 m. The presets are not yet re-derived for this release, which changes LV-network results against 0.15.0 alone by up to +13.6 % in the input impedance and +42 % in a house's \(Z_G\) diagonal.

      Instead: name the preset of your bus (rho_preset) and check max_relative of a fit over \(\rho_\text{eff}\) (see Soil); a mismatch of presets warns with RhoPresetMismatchWarning.

      Details and audit trail

      The weight presets per electrode class (groundfield.soil.EFFECTIVE_RESISTIVITY_PRESETS) are the q50 values of the AP2 hand-over, derived from the catalogue computed with groundfield 0.14.0 and not yet re-derived for this release, which changes LV-network results against 0.15.0 alone by up to +13.6 % in the input impedance and +42 % in a house's \(Z_G\) diagonal at \(\rho_1\) = 30 Ω·m (concrete shell, line kernel; Numbers that change in the changelog). Even "station" leaves up to 15.7 % at \(h_1\) = 5 m, where the station rods end 0.4 m above the interface. A BusType records the preset you name (rho_preset, user decision Q39): the JSON document in metadata["rho_preset"], the live groundinsight.BusType and the groundinsight database in the last line of the description, groundfield rho_preset: <name> (bustype_rho_preset reads it back; audit W3B-06; a document's metadata["rho_preset"] must be a preset name or null, as the exporters write it, and only the exact line is the record, audit W3R2B-06); evaluate_spec and bustype_from_spec warn when the bus uses another one, nothing checks rho_at_fit against it, and a document without a recorded preset is not checked.

    • Penetration depth of layered soil. penetration_depth matches the layered earth return to second order in the horizontal wavenumber; it is exact only in limits (homogeneous soil, very thin or thick top layers, large separations).

      How large: in between, the earth-return correction of two wires is off by up to 28 % / 33 % (overhead / buried, AP1 soils, separations near \(\lvert p\rvert\)), on top of the complex-depth formula's own error (up to 13 % in homogeneous soil); three layers up to 46 %, four layers up to 77 % at 1 km, 1 kHz (37 % at 50 Hz).

      Instead: the reference integral per_metre_mutual_impedance, within 6e-8 of the earth-return correction up to 300 m and 7e-7 at 1 km for buried pairs.

      Details and audit trail

      Measured in audit W3B-08.

    Backends¶

    The limits that belong to one backend: the fem engine and the concrete shells of every backend. The limits of the integral engines' current model and kernels are under current ansatz and source kernels, those at layer interfaces under soil layers.

    The fem engine¶

    • fem is experimental (ExperimentalBackendWarning once per process). It has two conforming models (ADR-0016); FemOptions.model = "auto" takes the axisymmetric one wherever it applies and the three-dimensional one (optional extra fem3d; without it ImportError) for every other world. fem is a reference for single clusters and small groups, not for networks. Like every backend, FieldResult.potential of a fem result supports homogeneous and two-layer soil only.

      Details and audit trail

      The automatic choice of the model is user decision Q37.

    • Axisymmetric model. Rods on one vertical axis and horizontal rings centred on it, solved as the solid bodies they are in any horizontally layered soil, bare or encased. A concrete shell enters as the surface conductance \(1/(2\pi r_b C)\) (exact for a cylindrical shell). A ring wire must lie 5 % of its radius below the surface (the thin-wire engines accept \(2z > a\)).

      How large: port resistances agree with independent references to about 1e-4 at the default (within 5.1e-5 for a ring wire within 1.05 radii of an interface up to a contrast of 1000, −8.5e-4 at 10 000). Eight rings with a central rod need about a million nodes and 17 s (1.2 million nodes and 26 s when one ring lies on an interface).

      Instead: raise FemOptions.refinement for a smaller error; FemOptions.max_nodes (2 million, about 8 GB at about 4 GB per million nodes) refuses larger meshes, raise it on purpose.

      Details and audit trail

      The error estimate is about the finer level's error (0.94 … 1.07 of it next to an interface) and 10 … 130 times that of the extrapolated value. Audits W4A-01, W4R2A-02 (accuracy next to an interface), W4A-05 (mesh size).

    • 3-D model: accuracy. The wires are thin wires like those of the integral engines (a smoothed line source with an analytic radius correction: uniform around the wire, no end faces), the soil an isotropic tetrahedral mesh with immersed layer interfaces.

      How large: away from layer interfaces it agrees with the axisymmetric model to 2e-3 for rods and rings in one to three layers. Its two mesh levels are not asymptotic for every geometry (error ratios 3.0 … 4.4): the reported error estimate (3 … 5e-3; fem_error_estimate_transfer for the transfer entries) bounds the error while the ratio is at least 2.5, and differences below about 2e-3 are not resolved. Next to a strongly contrasting interface the thin-wire error applies (see A horizontal wire within a few wire radii of an interface).

      Instead: check a 3-D result with an integral engine as the second method; solve rods on one axis and rings around it with the axisymmetric model.

      Details and audit trail

      For rings the estimate is 2.1 … 4.4 times the error. Audits W4B-01, W4B-02.

    • 3-D model: wires beside each other. Wires that do not meet but run beside each other (side by side within 30° of parallel, a folded arm, a small ring around a rod head) are kept at least \(1.5\,R\) apart (\(R\) = source_radius, 6 cm), two wires end to end in line with a gap, such as stacked rods of two electrodes, at least \(5\,R\). The model takes a smaller \(R\) for the whole world, and the nodes grow as \(1/R\).

      How large: coaxial rings 4.5 cm apart need 1.0 million nodes, stacked 8 mm rods 10 cm apart 0.47 million; coaxial rings 4.5 … 10 cm apart lie within 1.8e-3 of the solid tori. The model refuses, before meshing, wires closer than 3.3 radii of the thickest bare wire of the world, axis to axis (end to end 11, so stacked 8 mm rods up to 8.8 cm apart; BackendCapabilityError).

      Instead: an integral engine for such wires (the refusal names them), or the axisymmetric model where the world allows it.

      Details and audit trail

      End to end, the transfer entry's error falls with \(R\) / gap; at \(3\,R\) it was still up to 1.18 times its estimate. Wires that meet at 10° or more are left alone, so a V or a branch of 10 … 30° is solved; wires that meet at less than 10°, nearly parallel or touching along their length, count as running beside each other (audit W4R3B-02). Two encased wires of different ports whose shells touch count as one conductor in the radius correction, so their separate port entries are not resolved. Audits W4R2B-03, W4R2A-01, W4R3B-01, W4R3B-05.

    • 3-D model: layer contrast. Its conjugate gradients are tested up to a layer contrast of about 300.

      How large: at 1000 they do not converge and the solve raises BackendCapabilityError.

      Instead: the axisymmetric model (any contrast) where it takes the world, otherwise an integral engine.

      Details and audit trail

      Audit W4B-03.

    • 3-D model: wires near the surface or an interface. A horizontal wire within \(R/0.8\) of the soil surface or a layer interface (\(R\) = source_radius, 6 cm) gets a smaller disc and a finer mesh.

      How large: a bare one within about 2.8 wire radii is refused (BackendCapabilityError); an encased one is solved.

      Instead: an integral engine (the refusal names them).

    • 3-D model: size, time and ports. The finer mesh and the number of ports set the cost.

      How large: the AP1 station (28 m of wire) takes 1.5 … 3 minutes and 4.1 … 4.3 GB. FemOptions.max_nodes_3d (2 million nodes of the finer mesh; the solve adds about 2.9 GB per million) refuses larger meshes before they are built, the house mesh of the generators (60 m) at the defaults included (it solves at source_radius = 0.1, growth = 0.6: 1.5 million nodes, 1.5 minutes, 4.6 GB). More than FemOptions.max_ports_3d ports (100; every leaking piece of a galvanic distributed conductor is one) are refused before meshing (ResourceLimitError).

      Instead: raise the option to solve such a world on purpose, or use an integral engine for networks.

      Details and audit trail

      The port limit is audit W4B-08.

    • 3-D model: resolved concrete (concrete_resistivity) needs straight axis-parallel encased wires and converges at first order. It serves differences on one mesh, not the shell term itself.

      How large: its per-metre shell conductance reads 2.8 … 4.6 % high at the default levels: the square prism's concrete is 3.8 % less resistive than the circular shell in the shell-dominated limit, and its faces converge at first order; its error estimate is no bound. A cover so thin that the smoothed source does not reach beyond the metal (a 5 mm bar with 5 mm cover) is refused before meshing.

      Instead: the per-wire shell coefficient (the default of both models) for absolute values.

      Details and audit trail

      Resolved concrete is user decision Q40; audits W4B-04 (accuracy) and W4R2B-04 (thin cover).

    Concrete shells¶

    • Overlapping shells at junctions. Concrete shells (concrete_shell_coefficient_ohm_m) are represented by the image family, solve_mutual_*, mom and mom_sommerfeld, and fem (each wire its own shell; the axisymmetric model as a surface conductance, the 3-D model as a radial resistance per length). Each wire carries its own radial shell; where wires meet — the corners of a ring, the crossings and T-joints of a mesh foundation, whose braces are encased since 2026.11 — the shells overlap within about \(r_b\) of the junction and each wire counts the shared concrete as its own.

      How large: against the concrete resolved as a volume in the three-dimensional fem model a junction acts like a few cm of wire, at most about \(2\,r_b\), sign not settled; the house mesh of the generators (60 m, nine junctions) changes by about 1 % or less (0.35 … 0.85 % with every sign aligned).

      Instead: no correction is applied, because the junction numbers are not converged (concrete shells).

      Details and audit trail

      The volume model is user decision Q40 (5 mm bar, 5 cm cover, 1 000 and 5 000 Ω·m concrete in 100 Ω·m soil). The junction numbers are not converged — one refinement of the prism faces moved the corner by about 5 cm, and two decompositions of the same runs differ in sign (audit W4B-05) — so no correction is applied.

    Earth return and conductors¶

    • carson_series is a per-metre approximation for long parallel conductors over homogeneous soil; in layered soil it uses \(\rho_1\) (with a warning), and it rejects conductor segments in the soil-surface plane. It tiles only near-parallel pairs of segments inclined by less than 8.1° and hands every other pair to the Sommerfeld kernel.

      How large: at that boundary the result steps by the error of the per-metre model (13 % for two 1 km leads 2 m apart at 1 kHz, 42 % for two 300 m leads at 50 Hz; earth return). It overstates the earth return of LV networks of short service drops (shorter than the skin depth): the generated star, radial and grid networks of 5 … 100 houses read X 4 … 21 % and R up to 11 % high at 1 kHz and X 21 … 50 % and R up to 0.9 % high at 50 Hz against sommerfeld.

      Instead: use the default sommerfeld for numbers and for networks.

      Details and audit trail

      The 80-house radial network reads +50 % at 50 Hz; the default sommerfeld agrees with a refined discretisation to 1e-3 for the 5-house radial network and 4e-5 for the 80- and 100-house ones. At 50 Hz carson_series is no closer than perfect_mirror for the 10-house radial network, the 30-house star and the 80- and 100-house radial networks, but closer for the 5-house radial network (+31 % against +45 %; audits W3B-02, W3R2B-03). The error changes with the geometry, so a geometry change can move the two models in opposite directions (the 100-house star across the cabinet fix: +1.2 % against −3.5 %). Under carson_series the stretch rule of tilted pairs (audit W1R3-07) moves generated networks by up to 3e-7 (the 10-house star with 5 small and 2 medium sites 2.8e-7, the 80-house radial network 3.4e-8; the 30-house star and radial networks are bit-identical; audit W3B-07).

    • perfect_mirror is a frequency-independent diagnostic baseline, not a model of buried conductors below 1 kHz.

      How large: across the soil surface its additive image has no physical basis (a lead 1 m above a PEN 0.7 m deep: 2.28 / 2.77 times the mutual reactance of sommerfeld at 50 Hz / 1 kHz), and such a solve warns.

      Instead: use the default sommerfeld for numbers.

    • Sommerfeld segment rules. Under sommerfeld the self term of every segment is integrated exactly in one dimension and close pairs along their line of closest approach. Pairs of long segments that are not close keep a composite 16×16 rule (pieces of a quarter skin depth, at most 512 per segment, then a SommerfeldResolutionWarning). The spectral quadrature resolves the most distant points of a conductor up to 2^17 · 2π (z + z′)/30 — 38 km for a conductor at 0.7 m, 33 km from a rod head at 0.5 m to a junction at 0.7 m.

      How large: the self terms are within 1.1e-9 of a refined rule from 1 m and 0.01 skin depths to 3 km (up to 3.4e-9 in high-contrast soils), close pairs within about 4e-8. A longer conductor than the spectral range warns (SommerfeldResolutionWarning) and is not converged (a lumped 100 km conductor solves in 10.6 s but warns).

      Instead: treat a result that carries SommerfeldResolutionWarning as not converged.

      Details and audit trail

      The segment rules are user decision Q34: within 1.1e-9 of a refined rule from 1 m and 0.01 skin depths to 3 km for the soils tested (100, 1000, 30 over 1000 and 300/50/2000 Ω·m; up to 3.4e-9 in high-contrast soils: 10 over 10 000 Ω·m, 1.8e-9 under a 0.3 m top layer; independent references, audits W3B-01, W3R2B-08), close pairs within about 4e-8 (3.5e-8 for 1 m wires 1 mm apart in 30 over 1000 Ω·m at 1 kHz), continuous in the length. The spectral range is audit W3B-10. Two long close conductors (two lumped cables 1 m apart) cost a few seconds (1.2 / 4.8 s at 1 / 5 km).

    • Cost of the Sommerfeld assembly grows with the number of segment pairs and frequencies. The default sommerfeld_assembly="tabulated" shares one table of the spectral integral per pair of depth groups and frequency (17–25× less CPU than the per-pair rule on TN networks of 8 and 30 houses); blocks of fewer than 64 sub-segment pairs and conductors in the surface plane keep the per-pair rule ("exact"), whose cost grows with every pair.

    • Skin effect is opt-in (resistance_model="skin", solid round conductors); the proximity effect between conductors is not modelled.

    • Concentric cables are read only with the marker: a thinner conductor parallel inside a conductor with conductor_type="cable_shield" is its core and couples at the shield radius, at every axial offset (so a shield of several conductors and a cable in sections add up); the shield has no internal inductance of a solid wire. The skin effect of a shield (a tube) is not modelled (resistance_model="skin" raises for a shield); a three-core cable in one shield is three cores at the shield radius each.

      Instead: mark the shield with conductor_type="cable_shield"; any two other conductors drawn on one axis are separate cables touching each other.

      Details and audit trail

      The marker and the coupling at the shield radius are user decisions Q32 and Q33. Any two other conductors drawn on one axis are separate cables touching each other; their sub-pieces add up to the lumped pair. Two unmarked cables in sections couple as filaments on the axis between sections that do not overlap, and a core tilted against the shield axis, or crossing it, is read the same way at any angle and in pieces of any length (they add up to the lumped pair within 3e-12; audits W1R2-01, W1R3-01). Under carson_series a tilted pair that comes close within its overlap is tiled per stretch, so a lumped pair and its pieces add up within 8e-6 of the loop reactance (audit W1R3-07; 5.2e-2 apart before at 4e-2 rad); a pair whose distance stays above 30 times its change keeps the mid-point value (within 5e-5 of that entry).

    Post-processing and export¶

    • EN 50522 permissible touch voltage (EN 50522:2022) is interpolated in Table B.4 for fault durations from 50 ms to 10 s and clamped to 725 V below 50 ms; every duration beyond 10 s, including an uncleared fault (math.inf), gets 80 V (the curve of Figure 8 ends at 10 s), so the value steps from 85 V at 10 s to 80 V just above.

    • Touch-voltage envelope samples 24 equally spaced directions around the electrode's footprint and nine feet on the arc around every corner of the footprint's convex hull (corners=True), a proxy for touched parts that are not known. Re-entrant corners of a non-convex outline get no corner feet; there the equally spaced directions apply. Up to the final documentation pass of 2026.11.0 the directions alone were sampled, and they miss the corners of a non-square grid: a 60 m × 40 m grid read 12 … 28 % low (case study E03; corners=False reproduces it). With the corner feet every probed outline (rectangles from 1 cm to 10 km, rotated, L- and U-shaped outlines, stars, at the origin and at projected coordinates) lies within 0.001 % of dense sampling. It samples nothing inside the footprint unless asked: interior="meshes" adds the centres of the meshes of grids, of rings and of the generated mesh foundations (bonded wires, one per wire line), interior=True also the inside of a closed polyline. The modes cannot see buildings.

      Instead: define the accessible metal parts with touch_voltage_at_part where they are known; use the interior modes where the inside is accessible open ground (a substation yard), not for foundations or a ring around a housing or a mast.

    • Touch points (touch_voltage_at_part) take the part's potential from the electrode or junction it is bonded to.

      Instead: a part bonded to a point along a distributed conductor between two nodes needs a junction there.

    • Reduced models. The standard form is a fixed ansatz; a vector fit belongs to one soil (rho_at_fit); passivity is checked from 0 Hz to twice the highest fitted frequency only.

      Instead: check max_relative (warning above 5 %, RhoFStandardQualityWarning).

    Generators and OSM¶

    • Foundation outline. By default a footprint becomes its oriented minimum bounding rectangle; foundation_outline="polygon" follows the outline (ring-style specs only). Outlines smaller than 1 m are widened with a warning. Every building=* object is a footprint unless excluded with exclude_building_tags. A rectangle is one owner per side and so is a building outline (one owner per straight side): a rectangular outline equals the same rectangle given by its size. The connection point of a rectangular foundation is the start of its local −y side, a corner (also at multiples of 90°, which are exact rotations), that of an outline its first vertex; conductors attached by name and anchor="connection_point" use it. The generators end their cables and service drops at the point of the foundation nearest to each conductor instead.

      Details and audit trail

      One owner per side is user decision Q38 for rectangles and user decisions Q30 and Q44 for building outlines. The twelve outlines of the tutorial T06 layout moved by at most −0.004 % each with that decision, the layout with an AP1-like station by −0.03 … −0.40 %. The nearest-point rule of the generators is user decision Q41.

    • Generated service drops are straight conductors from the closest point of the feeding cable to the closest point of the building's foundation (horizontal at the PEN depth in TnNetworkGenerator). Drops keep the shortest connection and are not routed around foundations, so a drop to a house behind another runs 0.2 m above that house's side wire (PEN 0.6 m, foundations 0.8 m). The field model has no inductive coupling between a drop and an electrode; a bare PEN couples through the soil.

      How large: moved 1 m aside as a whole, those drops change \(\lvert Z\rvert\) by at most 0.006 % with an insulated PEN and by up to −0.40 % with a bare one (default grids of 30 and 150 houses, 50 Hz and 1 kHz, carson_series and sommerfeld).

      Details and audit trail

      A cable parallel to the facing side meets it at the corner where that side's wire starts (the tie rule). A building that a cable of its feeding path crosses is joined to it and fed by it without a drop (radial: any trunk; star: the main that crosses it, whose cabinet then feeds it), and no drop runs along a cable farther than one minimum section (1 cm; "along" means both ends of a cable section within 1 cm of the drop's line, so a drop that leaves a node at a small angle to a cable and stays within 1 cm of it only over part of the section keeps its route; a foundation that touches the end node of its cable from beyond is tapped 1 cm before the node and its drop shares that 1 cm).

      The shortest connection is user decision Q42: since the contact lies at the corner of the side facing the cable, a drop to a house behind another runs over that house's side wire — with the cabinets per building group 0 … 100 % of the drops of the default grids of 10 to 200 houses (star, 30 houses: all 20 drops, 5 m each over the middle-row house next to the main, 100 m; star 50, 150, 200: none; radial 150: 87 of 140, 1 805 m).

      Every site with a horizontal earth electrode — foundation, RingElectrodeSpec circle, strip — is met at its nearest point (Q41, Q43); only sites with rods alone keep the head of their first rod. A house whose horizontal electrode touches or crosses the substation's (e.g. 10 m houses 5 m from the default 4 m ring) gets a drop of no length: the star topology feeds it from its main instead (drops of 2.39 / 1.61 m in the touching / crossing cases of audit W1B), the radial topology raises and names both sites.

      The following values were measured before user decisions Q22, Q35 and Q43 (see the release notes for the change they bring): with an isolated PEN the end of a drop changes the resistive model only through its length (radial default grid with 2 ring houses, 50 Hz: −0.025 %); the joints and the star reassignment of user decision Q41 move it by −0.12 … +0.13 % (star 50 houses −0.117 %, radial 30 / 50 houses +0.118 / +0.125 %). A galvanic (bare) PEN leaks along its drops, so their route moves its earthing too (the radial default grid with 2 ring houses, 50 Hz: Re +7.8 % against −0.025 % isolated). The inductive coupling of drops and cables depends on where the drops end: with a Neumann PEN at 1 kHz (seed 1, default grid, sommerfeld default and carson_series) the reactance moved by −3.1 … +16.3 % (star) and −8.6 … +37.7 % (radial) between the anchor corner and the nearest point, and lies −10.8 … +14.0 % from drops to the centre as in 0.15.0 (12 m × 8 m ring houses +5.7 … +14.0 %; default 10 m ring houses: star +1.2 … +2.1 % for the 30-house network — the networks of 1 … 5 houses have no drop, all on the main, and move by +0.6 … +10.4 % through the joint and the Q38 owner —, radial −10.8 … −1.1 %).

      A layout cable is joined to the substation and to every cabinet whose closed earth electrode it passes through, not only at its ends; a cable through a house foundation is not joined to it (the house keeps its drop, of no length in plan there). LvNetworkLayout.plot(world=...) draws the drops as built; without a world it draws the layout's stubs (centroid to tap).

    • Cabinets per building group. The star topology groups the buildings by bearing around the substation and puts each cabinet on the ray through its group's centroid, 1 m beyond the group; a cabinet that would stand there farther than CabinetConfig.max_far_end_gap_m (20 m) from its houses stands on the ray through the group's far-end building instead. The threshold is a step, and the results jump where a group crosses it. The houses share their group's main (a tree), so results of default TnNetworkGenerator networks depend on this topology choice.

      How large: with the house columns of the 50-house grid 32.0 / 32.2 m apart one cabinet moves 141.8 m, the drops by −19 %, the input resistance at 50 Hz by −0.14 % beyond the smooth trend and, with an inductive PEN, the reactance at 1 kHz by −21.6 / −18.9 % (carson_series / sommerfeld). With an inductive PEN the network reactance at 1 kHz is +55 … +64 % above the former cabinet strip with its parallel drops.

      Instead: read per cabinet which rule placed it and its distance on the centroid ray (PlannedSite.cabinet_rule, PlannedSite.centroid_ray_gap_m) to see where a group crosses; a given CabinetConfig.placement fixes the cabinets.

      Details and audit trail

      The groups have equal sizes, starting from the widest gap. A group of about 180°, e.g. 10 houses and one small commercial building, would put its cabinet 50.4 m from its houses on the centroid ray. The threshold is a step, not a separation of two populations (kept for 2026.11, user decision D7): groups lie on both sides of it — on the centroid ray the groups of the default 50-house grid stand 8.8 … 12.8 m from their houses (a wide group there, 206 … 341°, still has drops up to 113 m to its outer houses), those of the 100-house grid 10.05 … 23.01 m (15 / 20 / 25 m put four / two / none of its five groups on the far-end ray), and over the 80 AP1 mixes the 118 groups beyond 20 m stand 20.21 … 53.56 m from their houses while the largest gap kept is 18.84 m. The 100-house grid across 22.95 m moves two cabinets by 65.6 m (X at 1 kHz +1.5 / −3.1 %), and 80 houses with 5 medium commercial buildings (a group at 20.21 m) move one by 31.4 m when the threshold is 20.5 m (X +1.5 / +1.1 %).

      A single group around the substation (one cabinet for a row through it) has its cabinet on one side only. A cabinet whose electrodes would come closer than end_clearance_m to another site raises (3 of 100 random 200-house layouts 12 m apart); a given CabinetConfig.placement avoids it. The +55 … +64 % of the tree topology are the default star of 30 houses (sommerfeld / carson_series). Planning 8 000 default star houses takes about 14 s (6.9 s with the former strip). Audits W1R2-02, W1R2-04.

    • Measurement leads of the in-line set-up are laid side by side; their mutual inductance depends on the logarithm of that spacing, which is a modelling choice, not a measured value.

    • Overpass answers are capped at 16 MiB; live queries need network access (tests and tutorials use stored answers).

    • Service drops of one tap. The service drops that leave one tap in one direction lie side by side at the touching distance, as cables in one trench; a wider spacing is not modelled.

      How large: 5 cm apart the 5-house radial network's X at 1 kHz is 12.5 / 14.6 % lower (carson_series / sommerfeld).

      Details and audit trail

      The touching distance is user decision D9. Drops that are not exactly parallel (placement jitter) lie along one common normal, and a drop that would come closer than touching to a drop of a row joins it, also a drop of a tap 1 … 4 cm away on the same trunk. No generated network reports an overlap in diagnose(), also with jitter_m 0 … 0.1 m (audit W3R2B-01). A drop of a row starts up to (n − 1)/2 touching distances off its tap (2 cm in a row of five). With a bare PEN, diagnose() without an engine warns about those starts as "interpenetrating" against the substation ring (the 10-house radial network: 9 pairs at 0.5 … 5.0 mm) and about the row neighbours as "close"; with the default line kernel all of these are information (audit W3R2B-07).

    • Ties of the star topology under placement jitter. Two ties of the 10-house star move its reactance by steps.

      How large: from about 1e-7 m of jitter the cabinet main joins residential_4 at another foundation wire (−0.24 % at 1 kHz), and from 1e-6 m it runs to the other side (−x → +x, −11.8 % carson_series, −12.1 % sommerfeld).

      Instead: expect these steps in a Monte Carlo study over the jitter.

    Diagnostics and problem size¶

    • Problem size. More segments or nodal unknowns than the engine's limit (Engine.max_segments, default HARD_LIMIT = 20 000) raise ResourceLimitError before any allocation; the dense system grows like \(N^2\) in memory and work per excitation. The notice that a solve may take minutes comes from Engine.solve (not from solve_mutual_*, which enforce the limit but emit no notice) from Engine.warn_segments on (default SOFT_LIMIT = 5 000); it is not checked against max_segments, so a notice size above the hard limit never shows.

      Instead: raise the limit per engine to solve a larger world on purpose.

    • Touching electrodes. diagnose reports wires of different electrodes that are closer than five times the sum of their radii, but not two electrodes that meet at a common segment end point (for example a rod through a node of a separate grid).

      Instead: metal in contact is one galvanic cluster: bond such electrodes with a conductor.

    • Aliases. cim, bem and image_nlayer run image and mom; comparing them with their targets adds no independent information.
