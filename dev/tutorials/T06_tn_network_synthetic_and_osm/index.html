    T06 — TN networks: synthetic and from OpenStreetMap¶

    Building a whole LV network (Ortsnetz) electrode by electrode is tedious. groundfield has two generators for it:

    Generator Use it for
    TnNetworkGenerator synthetic, stochastic networks: parameter studies and Monte Carlo over building counts, soils and geometry
    LvNetworkLayout one deterministic network laid out on a map: real building footprints from OpenStreetMap, your substation and cabinet positions, PEN cables routed around the buildings

    This tutorial uses both. The OpenStreetMap part runs offline from a small cached Overpass response that ships with the tutorials, so no network access is needed.

    You will learn

    • what a TN network world contains (substation earth, foundation earth electrodes, cable cabinets, PEN cables) and how to configure it;
    • how to make a stochastic network reproducible with a seed;
    • how to go from building footprints to a solved network, and how the share of houses with a foundation earth electrode (penetration) changes the network impedance;
    • three checks that apply to every network: Kirchhoff's current law, the monotonicity of the impedance, and a second engine.

    Prerequisites: pip install "groundfield[geo]" (shapely and pyproj for the map part). Runtime: about 15 s.

    Part A — a synthetic network¶

    A TnNetworkConfig describes the network as specifications: a soil spec, how many buildings of which type, how they are placed, the substation and cabinet earthing and the PEN cables. Every numeric field accepts a constant or a distribution (Uniform, Normal, Discrete, ...); the generator draws the values from its random number generator. The default specifications follow typical German LV networks: a substation earth of a 4 m ring with four 2.5 m rods, and single-family houses with a 10 m × 10 m foundation earth electrode (a 2 × 2 mesh at 0.8 m).

    Here twelve houses stand in two rows along a street (ManhattanGridPlacement), and the PEN runs as two radial feeders west and east of the substation; after every four houses a cable cabinet (German KVS) with a 1.5 m rod is inserted (RadialTrunkTopology). The resistivity of the upper soil layer is drawn uniformly between 100 and 300 Ω·m.

    Reproducibility¶

    The seed belongs to the generator: TnNetworkGenerator(cfg, seed=42) draws the whole realisation — soil, building counts, presence of optional electrodes — from one random stream. The same seed gives the same world, byte for byte; gf.world_fingerprint (a SHA-256 of the canonical world JSON) makes that easy to check. Different seeds give different realisations of the same configuration — the basis of a Monte Carlo study (see the how-to guide on sweeps and Monte Carlo).

    Part B — a network on a map¶

    LvNetworkLayout.from_osm asks the Overpass API of OpenStreetMap for every building within a radius, projects the footprints into local east/north metres around the centre, and places the substation and the cable cabinets where you say. Overpass answers are cached on disk (cache_dir); a cached answer is used without any network access.

    The tutorials ship such a cache file in data/osm/. It is a synthetic hamlet written in the Overpass format, not real map data: houses, two garages and a barn along two streets. The how-to guide Work with OpenStreetMap offline shows how to create a cache for your own area.

    exclude_building_tags drops buildings that have no electrical service; gf.geo.NON_SERVICE_BUILDING_TAGS is a documented set (garages, sheds, carports, ...).

    PEN cables are routed on a Manhattan grid around the buildings (with a clearance) from an anchor — the substation or a cabinet — to another anchor or to a free end point. connect_buildings then assigns every house to its nearest cable; the world built from the layout gives it a service drop from the cable to the nearest point of its foundation (the closest pair of cable and foundation). A house whose nearest cable point is the substation is connected there: the default substation earthing is a ring electrode without a foundation, which keeps its connection point, so that drop starts at the ring's anchor. layout.plot(world=...) draws these drops as built; without a world it draws the layout's stubs from each footprint's centroid to the cable.

    Penetration of foundation earth electrodes¶

    Not every house has a foundation earth electrode connected to the PEN — older buildings often have none. layout.foundation_mask(p) equips every house with probability \(p\), using a stable pseudo-random number per footprint: the same \(p\) always selects the same houses (on average \(p\) times their number), a larger \(p\) only adds houses, and salt= gives an independent realisation. That makes a penetration sweep a clean experiment.

    Two checks come for free:

    • Kirchhoff: the leakage currents of all electrodes add up to the source current.
    • Monotonicity: every added electrode is an additional path to remote earth. In a resistive network adding a path can never raise the impedance (Rayleigh's monotonicity law), so \(Z_\mathrm{in}(p)\) must fall as \(p\) grows.

    Summary¶

    • TnNetworkGenerator(cfg, seed=...) builds stochastic networks from specifications; the seed reproduces a realisation exactly.
    • LvNetworkLayout lays out one network on real (or cached) building footprints; foundation_mask(p) gives nested, reproducible penetration scenarios.
    • Kirchhoff's current law, the monotonicity in \(p\) and a second engine are cheap checks for any network.
