    T08 — Touch and step voltage assessment¶

    A fault current that flows into an earthing system raises its potential; people standing nearby bridge part of that potential difference with their bodies. This tutorial computes the touch and step voltages around a substation earthing grid, compares them with the permissible values of EN 50522:2022, and shows which engine to use for field quantities close to a grid.

    You will learn

    • the definitions of earth potential rise, touch voltage and step voltage, and how groundfield places the feet;
    • the touch-voltage envelope around an electrode, and when to sample the inside of a grid;
    • touch voltages at touch points, the accessible metal parts such as a fence bonded to the grid;
    • why the image backend is fine for impedances but not for touch and step voltages next to a grid, and what to use instead;
    • EN 50522's permissible touch voltage \(U_{Tp}(t_f)\), also for an earth fault that is not cleared, and the prospective value with additional resistances;
    • the true-RMS touch voltage of a fault current with harmonics.

    Runtime: a few seconds.

    Definitions¶

    With the fault current \(I_E\) that flows into the earthing system and its impedance \(Z_E\), the earth potential rise is \(U_E = Z_E I_E\). A person who touches an earthed part while standing on the surface at a distance of 1 m bridges the touch voltage

    \[ U_T = \varphi_E - \varphi(\text{foot}), \]

    and a person walking near the electrode bridges the step voltage between two points 1 m apart, \(U_S = \varphi(\mathbf p_1) - \varphi(\mathbf p_2)\). These are the prospective values (no body current flowing); EN 50522 relates them to the permissible body voltage through the fault duration \(t_f\) and the resistances of hands, feet, shoes and surface layer.

    The touched parts are the accessible metal parts above ground. Where they are known, gf.touch_voltage_at_part puts the feet on the circle of radius distance (1 m) around a part bonded to an electrode or a junction. Where they are not, gf.touch_voltage puts the feet distance from the nearest wire of the electrode's footprint, on the side given by direction, and gf.touch_voltage_envelope walks around the whole footprint. The envelope samples nothing inside the footprint by default, because there usually stands the station, the house or the mast. A substation yard is different: people walk over the grid, and interior="meshes" also samples the centres of the meshes (the mesh voltage of IEEE Std 80).

    The grid resistance: two engines and IEEE Std 80¶

    Before looking at the field around the grid we check its resistance three ways: the image backend (one uniform leakage current per straight wire — each line of the grid carries its own current per metre, uniform along it), the mom backend (a separate current on every segment, so the current can also crowd along each wire towards the edges and corners) and Sverak's formula of IEEE Std 80 (Eq. 57, an empirical closed form for grids),

    \[ R_g = \rho\left[\frac{1}{L_T} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{20A}}\left(1 + \frac{1}{1 + h\sqrt{20/A}}\right)\right]. \]

    Why the two engines differ near a grid¶

    For the impedance the uniform-current ansatz of the image family is accurate to a few percent. For the field next to a grid it is not: in reality the current per metre also grows along each wire towards its ends, so the corners carry the most and the potential falls off more steeply at the edge. The image backend gives every wire line of the grid its own current but keeps it uniform along the line, which overestimates the touch voltage at the corner (+6 % here) and underestimates the step voltage there (−8 %) — the second error is not on the safe side. The warning says so, and recommends backend="mom" for field-grade touch and step voltages near grids, meshes, stars, strips, open polylines and bonded clusters. Rings, rods and closed loops are not affected.

    Touch points: a fence bonded to the grid¶

    EN 50522 assesses the voltage between a touched part and the feet 1 m away from it. A yard fence that is bonded to the grid carries the grid potential to wherever it stands. With the fence 2 m outside the grid, a person outside the fence stands 3 m from the grid, on a lower surface potential than 1 m outside it. gf.touch_voltage_at_part takes the post's position and the electrode it is bonded to; directions keeps the feet on the side where people stand (exclude would remove the feet inside the outlines of buildings, n_angles walks all round).

    EN 50522: the permissible touch voltage¶

    EN 50522:2022 gives the permissible touch voltage \(U_{Tp}\) as a function of the fault duration \(t_f\) up to 10 s (Figure 8, Table B.4); gf.permissible_touch_voltage_en50522 interpolates it. Beyond 10 s it returns 80 V, the value the standard allows for current-flow durations considerably longer than 10 s — also for an earth fault that is not cleared (math.inf), as in a resonant-earthed network. With additional resistances in the body circuit — shoes \(R_{F1}\) and a surface layer of resistivity \(\rho_s\) — the permissible prospective touch voltage of Annex B is

    \[ U_{vTp} = U_{Tp}(t_f) + \frac{I_B(t_f)}{HF}\,\bigl(R_H + R_{F1} + 1.5\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}\,\rho_s\bigr), \]

    with the body current \(I_B(t_f)\) of Table B.1 (gf.permissible_prospective_touch_voltage_en50522). We assess the largest touch voltage of the yard — the mom envelope and the fence — for a fault cleared after 0.5 s, without and with shoes (1000 Ω) on the 100 Ω·m soil, and for an earth fault that is not cleared.

    Fault currents with harmonics¶

    In medium-voltage networks the residual earth-fault current is not a pure 50 Hz sine. Because the field problem is linear, each harmonic \(k\) produces its own touch voltage \(U_T(f_k)\) scaled with its current \(I_k\), and the true-RMS value is \(U_{T,\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\sum_k |U_T(f_k)|^2}\). gf.touch_voltage_spectrum takes a multi-frequency result and the current per frequency. The grid alone has no inductive conductor, so its touch voltage per ampere does not depend on frequency and the result must equal the 50 Hz value scaled with the RMS current — a simple check. With inductive cables (PEN, cable shields) the harmonics see a different impedance and the scaling no longer holds.

    Summary¶

    • touch_voltage_at_part evaluates the touch voltage at a touch point, an accessible metal part bonded to an electrode or a junction.
    • touch_voltage, touch_voltage_envelope and step_voltage place the feet relative to the electrode's footprint; for an open-air grid the envelope also samples the mesh centres (interior="meshes").
    • Use backend="mom" for touch and step voltages next to grids and other extended electrodes; the image backend warns where its uniform current is not field-grade.
    • permissible_touch_voltage_en50522 and permissible_prospective_touch_voltage_en50522 give the EN 50522 limits (80 V beyond 10 s and for an uncleared fault); touch_voltage_spectrum the true-RMS value of a harmonic fault current.
